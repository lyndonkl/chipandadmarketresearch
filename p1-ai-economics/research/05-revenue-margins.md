# Who Earns What

Two American labs collect most of the money paid for top-end AI. They are OpenAI and Anthropic. This chapter follows their cash. How fast does it grow? Where does it come from? What does it cost to earn? How much is left at the end?

## Two words first

AI coverage mixes up two revenue words, so let us pin them down.

Recognized revenue is money truly earned in a period. It is the number an auditor signs.

Run-rate takes one strong month and multiplies it out to a full year. It runs ahead of the truth when a firm is growing fast. Both labs quote it. Read it with care.

## The top line

OpenAI earned $13.07 billion in 2025. That is up from $3.7 billion in 2024, so more than triple in a year.[^1] The figure comes from audited accounts that leaked in June 2026. The Financial Times checked them.

The run-rate is higher. It passed $20 billion as 2025 closed. By early 2026 it sat near $24 billion, or about $2 billion a month.[^2] That is our best guess for that point. We would not be surprised by anything from $22 billion to $28 billion. Growth then went flat for months.

Anthropic climbed faster. Its run-rate went from about $1 billion in early 2025 to $9 billion at year end. Then $30 billion in April 2026, and $47 billion in May.[^3] That is the firm's own number, and it is a floor.

Recognized revenue tells a calmer story. Anthropic earned about $4.5 billion in 2025 against that $9 billion run-rate.[^4] The gap is close to two to one. The same gap shows up at OpenAI. Halve any headline run-rate in your head and you will not be far off.

## Where it comes from

The two labs sell in opposite shapes.

Most of OpenAI's money comes from ChatGPT plans, which are seats paid for by people and firms. Our rough guess is about 70 percent, with the API near a quarter.[^5] The API is the paid pipe that lets other firms call the model from their own code. That split is a weak number. Treat 70 percent as the middle of a wide band, say 60 to 78 percent.

Anthropic leans the other way. Most of its money is API and business use, not consumer seats.[^3] So OpenAI's pricing power sits mostly in a monthly fee. Anthropic's sits in a meter that ticks with use.

## What a token costs to serve

Gross margin is the share of each revenue dollar left after the direct cost of serving it. For a lab that cost is mostly compute: chip time to answer each request. The trade calls that work inference.

OpenAI's accounts show cost of revenue of $7.5 billion against $13.07 billion of revenue. Gross margin was near 43 percent.[^6] One caution: a large slice of serving compute seems to sit in the research line instead. So the true serving margin is lower than 43 percent.

How much lower is not clear. Reported serving spend for 2025 runs from about $5.5 billion up to $14.5 billion. It depends on which cost pot you count.[^7] Our best guess is near $7.5 billion. The wide band is honest, not lazy. Nobody outside the firm can pin it down yet.

Anthropic guided investors to about 40 percent for 2025. It had aimed for 50 percent. Cloud bills for serving came in about 23 percent above plan.[^8]

Both numbers say the same thing. Selling model output is not like selling software of the old kind. Old software cost almost nothing to copy. Each answer here burns chip time, and the bill lands every month. A margin near 40 percent is healthy, but it is not a software margin.

It is also well above zero. That is the other half of the story. While supply is tight, these labs charge well above what a token costs them to serve.

## Burn

Now the fixed side. OpenAI lost $20.92 billion at the operating line in 2025. Total costs were $34 billion. Research and development alone took $19.18 billion.[^9] Training a model sits inside that line. It is spent before a single user pays.

Anthropic burned $5.6 billion in 2025. It told investors it expects about $3 billion of burn in 2026, and none by 2027.[^10] OpenAI plans to burn $25 billion in 2026 alone.[^11]

Anthropic also guided investors to $10.9 billion of revenue in the second quarter of 2026, with an operating profit of $559 million.[^12] Read that with three cautions. It was a forecast shared during a fundraise, not an audited result. It leaves out stock pay. And it falls in a quarter when a big compute deal was priced low during ramp-up. The firm does not claim the profit lasts.

## The bill ahead

OpenAI has signed about $1.4 trillion of data centre commitments over roughly eight years.[^13] Against that, its own plan shows $30 billion of revenue in 2026, burn rising for years, and its first positive cash flow in 2030.[^11]

So the question is not whether tokens sell above serving cost. They do. The question is whether that gap grows large enough, for long enough, to pay back the fixed bill.

## Everyone else

The money really is bunched up. Microsoft's AI business passed a $37 billion run-rate in the March 2026 quarter, more than double a year before.[^14] Google Cloud took $24.77 billion in the June quarter, up 82 percent.[^15] Both sell picks and shovels: compute for model builders and for everyone else.

Direct model revenue outside the two labs is small. The AI revenue of xAI was near $0.5 billion, on a wide range.[^16] Meta earns close to nothing direct from its models. It makes its money from ads instead.[^16]

Backers are betting the crowding holds. OpenAI raised at $852 billion.[^17] Anthropic raised at $965 billion.[^18] Those marks price in years of the current gap between price and serving cost.

## What we take from this

Three things.

First, revenue is growing faster than almost anything on record, and it is bunched in two firms.

Second, the margin is real but thin by software standards, and it moves when cloud bills move. Serving cost is a live cost, not a rounding error.

Third, the fixed bill is the open question. Revenue can triple and still leave a $21 billion hole, as 2025 showed. Anthropic's one strong quarter hints the hole can close. Its own caution hints it may not close soon.
