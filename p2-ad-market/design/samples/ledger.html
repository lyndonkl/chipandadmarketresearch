Sheet 2 · The Spine

What the market spent, 1867 to 2025

Two compilers measured this market. They did not measure the same thing. One counted what advertisers were charged. The other counted what media owners were paid. So they are drawn as two rails, and the space between them is drawn as an object with a name. Nothing on this sheet is spliced.

The same figures, exactly. Pull a rule to see them as bars.

A column of figures and a bar chart are the same thing at two speeds. Grab the right edge of any money column below and pull it, or press its button. The cell’s baseline rule extends into a bar. The numerals never move. Nothing is created or destroyed; a rule is extended.

Bars run 0 to $398,000m · one scale for all four columns · linear, from zero · a ruled cell left blank is a period with nothing filed, not a zero

Why it starts at 1867. The record says it plainly: “Before 1867 there is no estimate of any kind, so 1840–1866 is a hole, not a low number.”

Nine years, not a line. The marks on the left plate are nine separate guesses, made later, from different papers. Joining them would invent 42 years of readings that nobody ever took.

Sheet 3 · Thread 1 · The Audit Register

Who counted, and who paid the counter

For eighty-two years this market ran on one rule. Somebody other than the seller counted the audience. The rule was never written down. It held from 1914, and it broke inside 1994–2001, the era the record calls The Impression.

Read the two right-hand columns straight down. They do not describe the counting. They describe the money that paid for it, and how far that money sat from the party being counted.

Distance from the seller · 5 the buyer paid · 4 both sides paid together · 3 a broker or a rival seller paid · 2 a vendor sold the count to both sides · 1 the seller paid, or the seller was the counter

The last row is not a return. The Media Rating Council still audits the counter for television. It never audited the search count. The record is blunt about that era: “there was no Media Rating Council accreditation of search click counting during the era.”

Why order, not colour. Who paid is not a set of labels. It is a distance. Seven flat colours would hide that, because no colour is further along than another. A row of filled cells cannot hide it: the row gets shorter as the money that paid the counter moves closer to the thing being counted. It also survives grey printing and colour blindness.

Era 5, in the record’s own words. “This is the first era in which the seller counted the audience with its own instrument and the buyer had no third-party audit to appeal to.”

Sheet 4 · The Uncertainty Grammar

How wide is wide

Every calibrated figure is drawn as a hairline spanning its 80% range, with a heavier tick marking the middle. A tick is a reading on a ruler. It is not a dot, because a dot claims a spot.

Grade is the pitch of the hatch, and a printed letter as well

A filed or audited · B one compiler, or a well-sourced reconstruction · C our own construction

The suppression rule, run in code and not by taste

if ((hi − lo) / central > 0.60) → remove the central mark

When the range is wider than 60% of the middle value, a central mark is a lie the drawing would be telling on the data’s behalf. So the code takes it out. What is left is a span. It says the honest thing, which is that we know the width and not the place. Because the rule is mechanical, no one can quietly switch it off for a cleaner chart.

Five more, drawn the same way

Units differ, so each row carries its own scale and prints both ends. Not one of these has a central mark. Every one of the 24 wide claims in the file fails the rule.

Nothing here moves. Confidence is carried by a static span, a static hatch and a printed letter. A reader who turns motion off, prints the sheet or screenshots it gets the same encoding, not a fallback.

Counted, not felt. The grade mark in the page header is a count of the records drawn on these five sheets. Reliability should be a number, not a mood.

Sheet 5 · The Unruled Block

Four pools. Three of them cannot be put in order.

This sheet has one convention and it is worth learning once. A ruled, numbered row is a place in an order. An unruled row inside a brace is a member of a set. Ruled means ranked. Unruled means we do not know.

The vertical order carries no information. Prove it.

What the evidence settles, and what it does not

Pairs that cannot be ordered
Pairs that can

Classified can be placed because its range touches none of the others. The three above it all overlap, and every overlap is shaded on the sheet. Where two spans share ground, neither one is bigger.

There is a second reason, and the file states it in capitals. Of the four pools, three carry the verdicts no source, pool not sourced and not sourced as a money type. Only classified is sourced for 2008–2021 at grade A on three independent legs. The one pool we can rank is the one somebody counted.

Why not a stacked bar. A stack asks the eye to judge the length of a block that does not start at zero, which is close to the least accurate reading it does. Worse, a stack asserts a full order, and the evidence refuses one.

Why alphabetical. Any order taken from the numbers would be read as a ranking. Alphabetical is visibly not about the numbers, so it cannot be mistaken for a finding.

Motion is off, so the shuffle changed form. The three rows still swap places. They swap at once and stay lit for a moment, so you can see which rows moved and check that nothing else did.
