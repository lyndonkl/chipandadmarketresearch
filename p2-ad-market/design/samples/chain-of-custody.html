The Docket · Register A, B and C

US total advertising, 1867–2025

Nobody ever counted this market end to end.

What exists is a handful of counts. Each was made by a different body, on a different basis, in a different run of years. So the counters are drawn first, at the top, and the money second. Where two counters cover the same year, both are drawn and the gap between them is given a name. Where nobody counted, the gap is drawn as an object, not as blank paper.

Reading rule. Two different materials are never joined. The Coen ribbon is a filled band. MAGNA is a row of stones. Our own bridge is a dashed outline with nothing inside it. You cannot chain them into one line by eye, which is the point: they are not one line.

Thread 1 · Who counted, and who paid the counter

The paired lifeline

The colour is not a category. It is a distance.

For most of this history the seller was not allowed to count the audience. Somebody else did, and that somebody was paid by a named party. Who paid kept changing.

The bottom track is a ladder. Rung 0 is the seller counting itself. Rung 5 is a rival medium paying for the count, hoping to prove the seller wrong. The rung is drawn twice over, once as a height and once as a colour, so the order survives a grey screen or a colour-blind reader. Each counter on the top track is tied down to the rung of whoever paid it.

The second instrument

Two positions · same object · 34 years apart

Change the instrument. The world holds still. The price moves.

These two switches are the same object. Each one swaps the tool used to count a television audience, and nothing else. The viewing is the same viewing. Flip it and watch what happens to money that was already sold.

The custody mark

One glyph, used everywhere a number appears

When the range is wider than the estimate, we take the estimate away.

Every number here is drawn as a span, not a dot. The span is the 80 percent range. A tick inside it marks the middle estimate. A notch says who counted. The line style says how good the sourcing is: A, B or C.

If the range is wider than the middle estimate itself, the tick comes off. A printed middle is something a reader stands on, and here there is nothing to stand on. Removing it is the only reliable fix.

Grade and width are two different things. The 1914 total on the first screen is graded C, so it is drawn dashed. It still keeps its tick, because its range is 38 percent of its middle, not more. Sourcing rides on the line style. Doubt rides on the length. Neither one is allowed to stand in for the other.

The unranked field

Era 7 · four pools of money

Three of these four cannot be put in order.

These are the four kinds of money in the market today. Their ranges overlap, so any one of the top three could be the largest. The record settles only one comparison: classified is the smallest, and its range does not touch any of the others.

So the three that cannot be ranked carry no middle mark. Classified keeps its tick. The difference between a span with a tick and a span without one is the whole finding. And the spans are not sorted — they sit in the order this essay met them, which the axis says out loud.

Mobile reduction, stated honestly

At 390 px the real build rotates the time axis to vertical, because 186 years across a phone gives about two pixels a year and every era becomes unreadable. Time then runs down the screen and the scroll gesture does the panning. This mockup only hints at that: the wide figures scroll sideways inside their own frame, and the prose reflows.

Dropped on a phone: hover, side-by-side comparison, and the full label gutter. Never dropped: every span, every grade, every void block, every custody rung and every “cannot be ranked” verdict. If something has to go it is an interaction, not a caveat.
