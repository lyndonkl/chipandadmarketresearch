The Economics of Intelligence

US labs charge about seven times what a token costs them to serve. This is the story of that gap — how it opened, and whether the trillion dollars behind it ever comes home.

T1 · supported

The gap is built, not claimed

Serving a million tokens costs about 15% of the list price. So price is roughly 7× cost. The amber stack is the same unit as cyan — just seven times as many.

T2 · supported

A price cut proves it

In 2025 OpenAI cut its o3 price by 80% and said the model was unchanged. Anthropic cut its top tier by two thirds. No one cuts like that if price sits near cost. The gap survives even a fuzzy cost estimate.

T6 · the wrong unit

Two clocks, both right

Hold quality fixed and the price of intelligence falls about 36× a year. Yet the flagship's sticker price rose — to $10 per million tokens, plus a tokenizer that bills 30% more. Cheaper and dearer at once. The token is not a stable ruler.

T4 · China

A controlled experiment

Moonshot launched Kimi K3 at $15 per million tokens — exactly Claude's rate — before releasing the weights. Then flip to open weights: rival hosts pile in and the premium collapses. Cheap prices come from competition, not cheaper silicon.

T5 · unresolved

The Convergence Dial

Drag from scarce to abundant. As supply catches demand, the amber premium sinks toward the cost floor — but the floor is a fog, not a line, because we never learn the true cost exactly.

margin over cost: high · our panel gives near-cost pricing by 2028 just 13%
the reckoning

Does the bet pay off?

About $650B a year of building, chasing the $1.5T of yearly revenue it would take to justify it. Each grain is a dollar of capital trying to earn itself back by 2030.

odds it clears its cost of capital: 52% — a coin flip

The verdict: four supported, one partly, one unresolved — and the open one holds all the money.
