ROLE
You build one rung of an ABSTRACTION LADDER over a knowledge graph about the history of the US
advertising market. You are given a GROUP of lower-layer nodes that a clustering step judged related.
Your job: define the PARENT META NODES one level up that describe these nodes at a higher level of
abstraction. Each parent is a NEW node that points DOWN to its children; the children are never
changed, moved, or deleted. You are not merging anything — you are naming higher-level concepts and
saying which nodes sit beneath each. You choose the name of every parent.

WHAT A PARENT META NODE IS
A parent groups children that are THE SAME THING AT A HIGHER LEVEL. The details that distinguish the
children — the exact YEAR, the reporting SOURCE/series, the exact WORDING and TYPE label — are
abstracted away at the parent and kept on the children below. Climb down and the specifics are still
there.
- Children "Google revenue 2004" and "Google revenue 2007" -> ONE parent "Google revenue". The years
  live on the children; the parent is the year-free concept.
- Forty nodes all naming the company Google, typed company / organization / internet company -> ONE
  parent "Google".
- Newspaper national-brand advertising spend for 1935, 1936, ... 1978, from one or several compilers
  -> ONE parent "newspaper national-brand advertising spend"; each year and source is a child.

WHAT YOU SEE
Each node is listed as: [idx] name | type | grain | origin | facts. You see the full node, including
the number on a measurement. GRAIN is given explicitly: Entity, Measurement, or Dimension. Read the
properties, not just the name; the TYPE label is what one reader happened to pick and is weak evidence.

NODE CLASS — the one hard rule
A parent keeps its children's node class. An Entity/Dimension parent has only Entity/Dimension
children; a Measurement parent has only Measurement children. A measurement ABOUT Google — "Google
revenue" — is a different node from the entity "Google". NEVER put a Measurement under a non-measurement
parent. (An Entity and a Dimension that denote the same thing DO belong together — the claim medium
"direct mail" and the money-graph dimension "direct mail" share a parent; that is how the money numbers
join the claims.)

WHAT SHARES A PARENT
- Same ENTITY, however typed or spelled -> one parent (all "Google"; "Overture"; the medium
  "newspapers" across its type labels).
- Same MEASURE = same (SUBJECT, QUANTITY, BREAKDOWN), abstracting away YEAR and SOURCE -> one parent
  ("Google revenue" across years and sources; "newspaper national-brand spend" across all years).

WHAT STAYS UNDER DIFFERENT PARENTS
- Different QUANTITY about one subject: newspaper spend vs newspaper circulation vs newspaper count.
- Different BREAKDOWN: newspaper TOTAL spend vs NATIONAL-BRAND spend vs LOCAL-RETAIL spend -> three
  parents. A total and a component are different measures, even though the components sum to the total.
- WHOLE vs SUBSET: "US advertising spend" (the whole market) vs "US internet advertising revenue" (a
  segment). A narrowing word — internet, digital, national, local, search — marks a subset, never a
  wording variant, and is never abstracted away.
- Different ENTITY kind: the medium "newspapers", the market "newspaper advertising", the ad inventory
  "newspaper space", the outlet "newspaper", the group "US newspapers" are different things.

DON'T FORCE IT. The clustering only proposed this group; you decide. If a node shares no higher concept
with the rest, give it its OWN parent — a singleton, which is normal and expected. A missed grouping is
safe (the next rung up can still catch it). A WRONG grouping — two genuinely different things under one
parent — corrupts every layer above. So when unsure, give a node its own parent. Split is the safe default.

PROCEDURE
1. Read every node; name to yourself the real thing it denotes and note its grain.
2. Split the group by node class first: Measurement vs Entity/Dimension. Never open a parent spanning both.
3. Within each class, form parents: gather children that share the same higher concept (same entity, or
   same subject+quantity+breakdown with year and source abstracted away).
4. Name each parent from MEANING — a clean canonical concept name, never a verbatim sentence fragment
   and never a bare year. Give it a TYPE (the kind of concept) and the GRAIN (Entity or Measurement).
5. Assign every node's idx to exactly one parent. A lone node is its own singleton parent.
6. Think privately, then print ONLY the output lines.

OUTPUT — line-based pipe, nothing else
For each parent, print one GROUP line, then one MEMBER line per child:
  GROUP|<gid>|<parent_name>|<parent_type>|<grain>|<reason, at most 14 words>
  MEMBER|<gid>|<idx>
- <gid> is G1, G2, G3, ... in order. <grain> is Entity or Measurement.
- <idx> is the [n] of the node; copy it exactly; never invent, drop, or renumber.
- Every node's idx appears in exactly one MEMBER line.
- A singleton is a GROUP with one MEMBER; begin its reason with "singleton: ".
- No JSON, no prose, no headers, no blank lines, and no "|" inside any field. First character is "G".
