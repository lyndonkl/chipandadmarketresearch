ROLE
You build a HIGHER rung of an abstraction ladder over a knowledge graph about the history of the US
advertising market. You are given a GROUP of meta nodes from the layer below — each already an
abstraction ("Google", "Newspaper national-brand advertising spend"). Your job: define the PARENT
KIND nodes one level up that group these by the SAME KIND OF THING. Each parent is a NEW node pointing
DOWN to its children, which are never changed. You group by KIND, not by identity: Google, Yahoo and
Overture are different companies but the same KIND — a search advertising platform. You name each kind.

WHAT A KIND PARENT IS
It groups children that are THE SAME KIND OF THING, abstracting away WHICH specific one:
- Google, Yahoo, Overture, AOL Search -> "search advertising platform".
- NBC, CBS, ABC -> "broadcast network".
- newspapers, radio, television, direct mail (as media) -> "advertising medium".
This is looser than the rung below (which grouped the SAME thing); here, different specific things of
one kind share a parent. The specific identities live on the children.

PREFER BROAD MARKET ROLES. Different SUB-TYPES of one role belong to ONE kind at this rung. Circulation
audit, audience audit and directory audit are one "audit" kind — not four. Different auction mechanisms
are one "auction mechanism" kind. A spend total and a spend-by-medium are one "media-spend measurement"
kind. Do NOT split a role into its sub-types here — that hair-splitting barely compresses and defeats
the whole point of the rung.

WHAT YOU SEE
Each node: [idx] name | type | grain | member types: <histogram of its children's types>. The member
types are your best signal for kind. GRAIN is Entity, Measurement, or Dimension.

NODE CLASS — the hard rule
An Entity/Dimension kind has only Entity/Dimension children; a Measurement kind has only Measurement
children. A company-kind and a spend-measurement-kind never share a parent.

NAME THE KIND BY ITS MARKET ROLE — NEVER GENERICALLY
BANNED kind names: "company", "organization", "entity", "thing", "measurement", "metric", "concept",
"business". They carry no information and collapse the ladder. Name the kind by what it DOES in the ad
market: search advertising platform, broadcast network, advertising agency, ad exchange, audience-
measurement firm, advertising medium, media-spend measurement, audience-share measurement, circulation
metric, auction mechanism, pricing rule, cost measurement, ...

WHAT STAYS UNDER DIFFERENT KINDS
- Different market role: a search platform is not a broadcast network is not an agency is not a
  measurement firm.
- Different measured quantity: a spend, a share, a count, a circulation, a duration and a price are
  DIFFERENT kinds — never one "measurement" blob. The newspaper spend, receipts, count, circulation
  and share measures are five kinds, not one.
- When two children are genuinely different kinds, keep them apart even if a generic word would cover
  both.

GROUP DECISIVELY BY ROLE. The danger at THIS rung is the opposite of the one below: OVER-splitting
into hair-thin singletons that barely compress. When two children play the SAME role in the ad market,
put them under one kind — even if their sub-type, period, or wording differs. Split only when the ROLE
genuinely differs: a search platform vs a broadcast network vs an agency vs a measurement firm; a
spend measure vs a share measure vs a count vs a price. Reserve a singleton for a child whose market
role nothing else in the pile shares. Never split a single role into sub-types, and never reach for a
banned generic name.

PROCEDURE
1. Read every node; name to yourself its KIND — its role in the ad market — ignoring its specific identity.
2. Split by node class: Measurement vs Entity/Dimension. Never a parent across both.
3. Group children that share a market-role kind. Name each kind by its role, never generically.
4. Assign every node's idx to exactly one kind. A lone kind is a singleton.
5. Think privately, then print ONLY the output lines.

OUTPUT — line-based pipe, nothing else
For each kind, one GROUP line then one MEMBER line per child:
  GROUP|<gid>|<kind_name>|<kind_type>|<grain>|<reason, at most 14 words>
  MEMBER|<gid>|<idx>
- <gid> = G1, G2, ... ; <grain> = Entity or Measurement ; <idx> = the [n], copied exactly.
- Every idx appears in exactly one MEMBER line. A singleton is a GROUP with one MEMBER; begin its
  reason "singleton: ". No JSON, no prose, no blank lines, no "|" inside a field. First character is "G".
