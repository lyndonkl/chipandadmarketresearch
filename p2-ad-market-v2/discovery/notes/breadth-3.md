# Breadth sweep 3 — L3 themes ranked 109-144 (the tail)

**Slice:** 32 single-member (`member_count = 1`) leaf themes, ranked 109-141 by size.
**Sweep finding:** the tail is not junk. Each single-node theme resolves to exactly one
anchor, and those anchors are of two kinds:
- **Synthesis nodes** (`tu:era:N:field:*` / `tu:era:N:event:*`) — the project's own
  dense, cross-era traveling-unit thread fields. Grade-less *summaries* that quote the
  underlying sourced claims. Treat as SECONDARY/synthesis in ledger terms.
- **Directly-sourced claims** (`e#-*`, `mech-*`) — graded A/B/C with named sources.

Because a leaf theme = one field summary, the tail is where the richest pre-woven
cross-era threads surface fastest. Grounding discipline below preserves each anchor's
grade; where a `tu:era` field quotes a number, that number traces to an `e#-` claim.

## FLAGGED — high narrative value

### 1. The audience is counted by a third party — for a century, then it breaks
**Themes:** `audience measurement service`, `Nielsen TV accreditation`, `market discipline`
**Why:** A clean cross-era spine and an opposition. The market's founding discipline
("the buyer could audit someone other than the seller") is born with ABC circulation
audit in 1914, and dies in era 7 when the same firm sells inventory, runs the auction,
counts impressions and attributes conversions. Rhymes across 75 years.
- `tu:era:2:event:9` (1946-07-31) — the industry's buyer-controlled rating service (CAB)
  dies after the Zeisel study exposed Hooper's numbers ran 20% above CAB's; Nielsen (meter
  commercial since Dec 1942; 97% of US homes by Apr 1949) takes over. "Measurement passed
  permanently from a committee of buyers to private vendors selling to both sides."
- `tu:era:7:field:MEASUREMENT` — "the century-old rule that a third party counts the
  audience finally broke." Facebook overstated video view time ~2 yrs (plaintiffs: up to
  900%), settled $40M in 2019.
- `e7-measurement-002` (grade B) — MRC suspended Nielsen's national+local TV accreditation
  Sept 2021 over 2-6% pandemic undercounts; VAB valued it at ≥$468M lost national ad
  revenue; national reinstated 2023-04-17, local still suspended.

### 2. The auction died three times over — and a smoking gun on price
**Theme:** `rGSP replacement of format pricing`
**Why:** Opposition + surprise + a quantified, sourced admission of hidden price control.
- `tu:era:7:field:PRICING` — header bidding (2014-15) broke the waterfall. Google moved
  Ad Manager to unified first-price on 2019-09-05. The DOJ trial exposed Google's internal
  "intentional pricing" knobs. There were three: squashing (launched as "Butternut Squash"),
  format pricing, and rGSP (2019). rGSP raised top-slot CPCs ~5.9% desktop / 4.9% mobile in tests;
  **advertisers cannot opt out**; Jerry Dischler testified Google moved prices ~5% on
  average, up to 10% on some queries, to hit quarterly revenue targets — without telling
  advertisers. Take-rate opacity: ISBA/PwC 51% reaches publisher with 15% "unknown delta".

### 3. The 36% reveal — disclosed by accident, then redacted
**Theme:** `legal venue`
**Why:** A vivid courtroom mechanism; the number the parties fought to keep sealed.
- `mech-default-003` (grade B) — Google pays Apple **36%** of Safari-generated search ad
  revenue; "disclosed inadvertently in open court" by Google's own economic expert, later
  confirmed by Alphabet's CEO; FOF para 298 of the public opinion is redacted.

### 4. A whole buyer category legislated out overnight
**Theme:** `Cigarette makers payment to broadcasters`
**Why:** Dated, quantified, external state-change. Regulation removes a buyer class.
- `tu:era:3:field:BUYERS` — from 2 January 1971 cigarette makers, "who had been paying
  broadcasters roughly $225 million a year, could no longer buy television or radio at all."
  Same field's larger surprise: the four money pools barely moved 1950→1975 (43/36/7/14 →
  40/38/8/15); TV's rise 3%→19% was reallocation *inside* national brand money.

### 5. The holding company modelled on General Motors
**Theme:** `General Motors`
**Why:** Vivid actor + structural origin that still governs the industry.
- `tu:era:3:event:3` (1961-01) — Marion Harper incorporates Interpublic, the first ad
  holding company, "explicitly modelled on General Motors' divisional structure." It solved
  the conflict-policy constraint that capped agency size (one owner could serve competing
  clients) and set the template Omnicom, WPP and Publicis still follow.

### 6. A price rule invented the soap opera
**Theme:** `Radio money`
**Why:** A pricing mechanism → a cultural artifact. Clean causal, vivid.
- `tu:era:2:field:TARGETING` — a daytime network hour cost exactly half an evening hour,
  and the daytime audience was overwhelmingly women at home; "P&G and its agencies built
  the soap opera to occupy cheap hours in front of the people who bought soap." Also: by
  end of era more radio money went to local spot than to the networks (spot local $35M
  1935 → $245M 1949).

### 7. The 15%-commission myth vs the Economic Census
**Themes:** `Fixed commission concept`, `pricing component`
**Why:** A received story the data contradicts — a myth-buster spine. Plus the agency's
origin as a *space broker*, not a maker of ads.
- `tu:era:1:field:CREATORS` — the agency was invented as a space broker (Palmer 1841, paid
  by publishers ~25%); N. W. Ayer's 1875 "open contract" made the agent work for the buyer
  at a disclosed fixed percentage. "The full-service agency, the pitch, and the fixed
  commission all existed before 1917."
- `tu:era:4:field:PRICING` — "The received story is that the 15% commission died in the
  1980s; the Economic Census says otherwise." Commissions actually received: 14.13% (1977),
  13.92% (1982), 14.16% (1987), 13.18% (1992) — and only then collapsed to 10.98% (1997).
  What eroded first was the mix, not the rate.

### 8. Concentration rhymes across 90 years — and the antitrust bookend
**Themes:** `Mutual network net time sales share`, `market position`, `Rival take rate`
**Why:** Seller concentration in 1938 and 2025 rhyme; both drew government intervention.
- `tu:era:2:field:SELLERS` — in 1938 NBC+CBS took 44% of the industry's $100.9M net time
  sales, +7% from owned stations = two firms handling >half; they kept 73% of network net
  time, paid affiliates 27%. Government forced RCA to sell NBC Blue → Edward Noble buys it
  Oct 1943 for $8M, renames it ABC.
- `tu:era:7:field:SELLERS` — Google/Meta/Amazon ~61% of US digital ad spend 2025; top ten
  sellers 84.1% of US internet ad revenue (up from 78.6% in 2021). Two courts ruled it
  unlawful: Mehta (2024-08-05, search) and Brinkema (2025-04-17, ad server + exchange).
  DOJ: AdX held a 20% take rate since 2009 while rivals charged closer to 10%.

## FLAGGED — supporting / quantified anchors

- **Hidden giants most histories skip** — `market position` → `tu:era:4:field:SELLERS`:
  RBOC Yellow Pages sold $9.5B in 1993 (more than radio, more than magazines); USPS was
  "effectively the distribution monopoly underneath the country's third-largest medium"
  ($9.8B third-class postage FY1993 vs $2.4B FY1980).
- **The 1991 clean macro event** — `US advertising spend nominal decline`
  → `tu:era:4:field:SCALE`: "1991 was the first nominal decline in US advertising spend
  since 1961." Era grew 4.2x nominal 1976-93 (8.9% CAGR), ad/GDP plateau 2.3% 1986-89.
- **The automation endpoint** — `earnings call` → `e7-targeting-005` (grade B): Meta's
  end-to-end AI ad solutions (Advantage+) surpassed a $60B annualised run rate in Q3 2025,
  ~30% of Meta's 2025 ad revenue (caveat: instantaneous annualisation).
- **The auction's yield effect, isolated** — `market effect` → `e6-pricing-008` (grade C):
  Google ad revenue per search rose ~4.7x, $0.0047/query (2002) → $0.022 (2007), the yield
  effect distinct from the volume effect of distribution.
- **How you count flips who leads** — `contribution amount`/`performance measurement`/
  `pricing-basis component`/`attribution component` → `e5-scale-016` (grade C): under the
  unified-intent rule direct response leads local retail (29.21 vs 23.82); under the
  era-native geographic rule local retail leads (28.32 vs 24.71). "The flip is exact and
  this one block [local Yellow Pages] is its sole cause." A measurement-integrity artifact.
- **Grade-A minor quantifieds** — `priced quantity` → `e1-measurement-004`: US
  newspaper+periodical circulation per issue grew 25.0% 1909-1914 (164.5M → 205.6M copies).
  `advertising service sector` → `e4-creators-005`: US direct-mail services (SIC 7331)
  receipts $8.3B in 1993, up from $3.8B in 1985; 3,900 establishments, 81,000 employees.
