## Spine (ABT, cited per slot)

- **AND** — For a century the US ad market's founding discipline was that a *third party* counts the audience: the Audit Bureau of Circulations, 1914, jointly funded by advertisers, agencies and publishers, promising "facts without opinions" `[e1-measurement-002 B; tu:era:1:field:MEASUREMENT]`.
- **BUT** — each consolidation put the counter on the payroll of the side it judged. By eras 6–7 the same company sold the inventory, ran the auction, counted the impressions and attributed the conversions. No third party could adjudicate `[tu:era:6:field:MEASUREMENT; tu:era:7:field:MEASUREMENT; e6-measurement-006 A]`.
- **THEREFORE** — the discipline took 45 years to build (1869→1914). It then reverted to the pre-1914 default of self-counting, now at platform scale. The era's answer to *who pays the counter* is "increasingly the seller, or nobody" `[tu:era:7:field:MEASUREMENT]`. The IAB scores that failure at >$26B of misallocated US media `[e7-measurement-005 B]`.

**Structure (McPhee):** SPIRAL over chronology — the same question (*who audits the counter?*) re-entered at
widening radius each era; chronology carries it (1869→2026). Runner-up: strict LINEAR/seven-era timeline —
lost because recurrence, not sequence, is the argument.

## Systemic protagonist — POSIWID sheet

- **Stated purpose (quoted):** an independent, auditable count so both sides trade on a common currency — ABC 1914 "facts without opinions," members suspendable for padding numbers `[tu:era:1:field:MEASUREMENT]`.
- **Revealed function (2-regime test PASS):** the count drifts toward whoever pays for it, and the counter repeatedly ends up selling to — or being — the party it judges. Regime A: 1946+, Hooper/Nielsen sell the count to both sides `[tu:era:2:event:9]`. Regime B: era 6–7, Google/Facebook count their own `[tu:era:6/7:field:MEASUREMENT]`.
- **Gap (the engine):** the discipline is *designed* to keep the counter independent of the counted; the revealed function keeps collapsing that independence, and it is re-imposed only by external force (govt 1912; threatened regulation 1963–64) — never chosen.
- **Constraint that fills the opposition slot (not a villain):** the *economics of counting*. The count is expensive; whoever funds it controls it; tripartite/industry funding is the only arrangement that keeps the counter independent, and the cheapest place to put the counter is inside the seller. Google/Facebook are load-bearing actors, **surfaced not adjudicated** (real named living parties in an adverse slot → human decision).
- **MANDATORY RESIDUAL — outcomes the "seller captures the counter" function does NOT explain:**
  1. **1987 people meter** — the *honest instrument hurt the seller*: measured prime-time fell ~10% (CBS/ABC −13%), make-goods owed exceeded the upfront time sold; "nothing about the audience had changed; only the instrument had" `[e4-measurement-001 B; tu:era:4:field:MEASUREMENT]`. Counter-example to "the count always favours whoever pays."
  2. **2021 MRC–Nielsen** — the industry-funded auditor *bit the incumbent counter*: MRC stripped Nielsen's national+local TV accreditation for 2–6% pandemic undercounts, VAB valued ≥$468M; national reinstated 2023-04-17 `[e7-measurement-002 B]`. Documented partial reform → blocks the fatalism reading.

## Hypothesis diff (what the evidence changed)

- **BEFORE:** three regimes (buyer-committee → third-party vendor → seller-platform); era-7 "different in kind or scale?"
- **AFTER:** it is a **four-state spiral**, and the 1914 third-party discipline was itself a *correction* of era-1 self-counting (publishers stated their own circulation, rate card unenforceable, `e1-measurement-001` / `tu:era:1:field:MEASUREMENT`). Era 6–7 "seller counts its own" is therefore a **reversion to the pre-1914 default**, not a novel terminal state.
- **Answer to the sharpest question:** *different in scale and in enforceability, largely the same in kind.* Era 1 self-counting was eventually backstopped by an enforceable filing — the Newspaper Publicity Act 1912, sworn circulation, upheld *Lewis Publishing v. Morgan* 1913. Era 6–7 self-counting has **no enforceable equivalent**, only voluntary MRC accreditation the platforms largely evade. Independent third-party counting (1914–~2004) was the ~90-year **anomaly**; self-counting is the mean the market keeps reverting to. The frame flips from "collapse of a norm" to "a fragile norm re-imposed by external force — and in era 6–7 no external force re-imposed it."

## Best cited findings (the goods)

1. **The discipline was built, not given.** ABC 1914 — publisher-led Advertising Audit Association + advertiser-led Bureau of Verified Circulations merge in Chicago, world's first third-party circulation audit, tripartite-funded, "facts without opinions," members suspendable for padding `[e1-measurement-002 B; tu:era:1:field:MEASUREMENT]`. It corrected era-1 self-counting `[e1-measurement-001 B]`.
2. **Regime transfer #1 (1946): buyers lose the count.** CAB "should not be continued"; three networks (its funders) threaten withdrawal; Hooper takes over 31 Jul 1946; "sixteen years of buyer-controlled measurement ended … measurement passed permanently from a committee of buyers to private vendors selling to both sides" `[tu:era:2:event:9]`.
3. **The cost of that transfer, quantified.** Even after CAB adopted Hooper's own method in 1944, Hooper's ratings still ran **~20% above** CAB's (Zeisel/McCann traced the residual to ring counts, unidentified-program and busy-signal handling) — the count inflated toward the vendor's paying side `[e2-measurement-003 B]`.
4. **The auditor-of-the-counter is born under threat.** Harris House hearings on ratings methodology 1963–64; the industry "pre-empted regulation" by funding the Broadcast Rating Council (1964), later the MRC — "the institutional answer to *who audits the counter* … the digital platforms later inherit" `[tu:era:3:field:MEASUREMENT]`.
5. **The instrument, not the audience.** People meters (Sept 1987) measured ~10% fewer prime-time viewers (CBS/ABC −13%, NBC −4%); make-goods owed exceeded upfront time sold; "nothing about the audience had changed; only the instrument had" — **L1**, and a residual: the honest count hurt the seller `[e4-measurement-001 B; tu:era:4:field:MEASUREMENT]`.
6. **The seller becomes the auditor.** "Every price in the search market was set from a count that the seller alone produced." Google recorded the impression, ran the auction, decided which click was valid, and billed its own log. The court-appointed click-fraud examiner (Tuzhilin) was "commissioned by the defendant and paid for by a settlement" `[tu:era:6:field:MEASUREMENT]`.
7. **The seller publishes the rate, never the count (grade A).** Google disclosed only aggregate paid-click *growth* (~18% YoY Q4 2008) and "never an absolute click count, an average CPC, or a query count" `[e6-measurement-006 A, SEC 8-K]`. The hardest-graded hinge of the thread.
8. **The crisis nobody could adjudicate (contested).** Independent estimators put invalid clicks ~14–17% (2007–08) against Google's low-single-digit internal figure; different denominators; "no accredited third party able to adjudicate" `[e6-measurement-002 C, CONTESTED, CI 3–20]`.
9. **The century-old rule breaks.** Facebook overstated average video watch time ~2 years; plaintiffs *alleged* the sub-3-second exclusion inflated it *by up to 900%*; $40M settlement, 2019 `[e7-measurement-001 B]`. "Who paid the counter is the era's quiet answer: increasingly the seller, or nobody" `[tu:era:7:field:MEASUREMENT]`.
10. **The measured cost of the failure — and the surviving auditor's bite.** MRC stripped Nielsen's TV accreditation 2021 (2–6% undercount, VAB ≥$468M), reinstated national 2023 `[e7-measurement-002 B]`; bots >50% of web traffic (IAB/Imperva 2025) `[e7-measurement-003 B]`; AI summaries cut the funding click from 15%→8% (Pew) `[e7-measurement-004 B]`; IAB scores measurement failure at >$26B of misallocated US media, 1–2 yr `[e7-measurement-005 B]`.

## Agency reading (required)

Strongest agency/intent reading considered: *Google and Facebook chose to internalise the count to extract
rents.* Not adopted as the spine because (a) the corpus documents the mechanism as **structural** — every
prior consolidation (1946, 1950) drifted the same way absent any platform intent — and (b) the residuals
(1987, 2021) show the count sometimes hurting whoever paid it. The honest claim is the weak/structural one:
self-counting is the market's default and independent counting the fragile exception, re-imposed only by
external force. Named-party intent is surfaced, not asserted.
