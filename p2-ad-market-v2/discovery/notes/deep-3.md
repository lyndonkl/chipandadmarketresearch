## Phase 2 — the spine, derived backward, contemporaneity-gated

Endpoint: by 2025 two courts found the top of the stack unlawfully concentrated. Inverting to a starting weakness and gating on pre-outcome sources gives a spine the evidence actually supports — **and it is not my starting hypothesis unchanged.** See the diff below.

**Through-line (the corrected spine):**
> Concentration at the top of the ad stack is **built by the market** — network effects, below-cost distribution deals, acquisitions. It has only ever been **moved by the state**. But *moved* is not *broken*. Across ~90 years the state has **relocated** the concentration (1943, 1984), **ceilinged** it (1954), or **named it unlawful with the remedy still pending** (2025). The one genuine deconcentration came from a **supply shock the state licensed but did not design** — the postwar station boom. The 2024–25 rulings are the fourth turn of the same wheel.

This is a **systemic protagonist** (the seller-plus-intermediary layer), an **explanatory/braided** form, not a Truby arc — there is no single continuous willing entity and the outcome (2025 rulings) is already known. The opponent is a **constraint/actor pair**: the market builds, the state moves.

## The best cited findings (the goods)

**F1 — The 1938 peak: two firms, half the market, a 73% keep.**
Path: L3 *Broadcast Media* → `tu:era:2:field:SELLERS`. In 1938 NBC+CBS took 44% of the industry's $100.9M net time, +7% owned = **51% for two companies** [A `e2-sellers-001`]; the networks **kept 73%** and paid 27% to 253 affiliates [A `e2-sellers-002`]. The dominant seller was also the dominant intermediary. Source: FCC 1941 *Report on Chain Broadcasting* (the era's best audited numbers).

**F2 — The state broke it: FCC forces RCA to sell NBC Blue, Oct 1943, $8M → ABC.**
`tu:era:2:field:SELLERS`: "Government broke the top of that structure" — the FCC chain-broadcasting rules forced the divestiture; Edward J. Noble bought NBC Blue for $8M and renamed it the American Broadcasting Company. **Corrective (load-bearing):** the actual reallocation of dollars from network to spot came *after*, from the postwar licensing boom — **~900 AM stations (1945) → ~2,500 (early 1950s)** [B `e2-sellers-006`]. The divestiture moved ownership; a **supply expansion** did the deconcentration.

**F3 — The state's standing ceiling: FCC 7-station cap (1954).**
`e3-sellers-002` [A]: from 1954 the FCC capped any owner at seven TV stations (≤5 VHF), 43 FCC 2797 — "which is why the networks needed affiliates." A binding **constraint**, not a rupture. Meanwhile the market moved the other way: the networks "took the inventory back" (advertiser-owned shows → network-owned time) — `tu:era:3:field:SELLERS`.

**F4 — The 1984 rupture created new sellers: AT&T divestiture → RBOCs get Yellow Pages.**
`e4-sellers-004` [B]: the Regional Bell Operating Companies, "handed the directory business at the 1984 AT&T divestiture," sold **$9.5B of Yellow Pages advertising in 1993** — more than radio, more than magazines. A state antitrust action **reshaped who sells**, not toward deconcentration. (Concurrently the Big-3's grip fell 90%→61–70% via a *market/tech* shock — cable — `e4-sellers-001` [B].)

**F5 — The 2025 peak rhymes with 1938: three firms, an outsized take, a court that named it.**
Top-10 sellers **84.1%** in 2025, up from 78.6% in 2021 [A `e7-sellers-001`]. The intermediary cut echoes era 2: **AdX ~20% take rate since 2009 vs rivals ~10% (DOJ-alleged)**; publishers keep ~69¢ of an advertiser dollar inside Google's stack, DOJ-alleged Google keeps ≥35¢ of open-web display [B `e7-pricing-006`]. And the seller **set the clearing price**. Three pricing knobs — squashing ("Butternut Squash"), format pricing, rGSP — are named in Mehta's findings of fact, all on the text-ads auction, all "intentional pricing" [A `mech-knobs-001`]. The court found supracompetitive prices and repeated 5%+ price tests [A `mech-mehta-004`]. The 2019 move to unified first-price ended second-price pricing (`tu:era:7:event:4`).

**F6 — The fourth state rupture: Mehta 2024-08-05, Brinkema 2025-04-17.**
`tu:era:7:field:SELLERS` / `tu:era:7:event:8`: Mehta held Google monopolised general search and search text advertising (89.2% / 88% shares); Brinkema separately held it monopolised the open-web publisher ad server and ad exchange and unlawfully tied them. The state again names the concentration unlawful. **DEAD/corrective:** the remedy is *not* deconcentration on this record. Mehta's 2025-09-02 remedies opinion **declined to order a Chrome divestiture** and wrote that generative AI "had changed the course of the case". Brinkema's remedies order was still pending at the 2026-06-30 freeze (`tu:era:7:boundary`).

**F7 — The load-bearing human: Judge Brinkema, E.D. Va., 2004 → 2025.**
The same judge sits at both ends of the digital seller story. In 2004 she ruled that selling a trademark as an AdWords keyword did **not by itself** violate the Lanham Act [B `e6-events-004`; `tu:era:6:field:TARGETING`] — a narrow ruling that let the keyword-auction seller model stand. Twenty-one years later she found Google monopolised the ad server and exchange (`tu:era:7:event:8`). One courtroom, two rulings, both structural.

**F8 — Corrective: the market built the concentration by capital, not by a better product.**
`tu:era:5:event:2` carries a **DID_NOT_CONTRIBUTE** edge: Google did *not* contribute to the open per-click auction — GoTo/Overture did. Entering era 6, **Overture led** — $667.7M in 2002 vs Google's $439.5M total [A `e6-sellers-001`]. "Google's answer was not a better auction but a bigger cheque." That was the AOL deal: a reported ~$100M guarantee + ~85% revenue share [B `mech-aol-001`], with TAC at 91% of network revenue in 2002 [A `mech-tac-001`]. Concentration was **bought and distributed**. That is precisely why competition did not undo it and the state had to.

**F9 — Corrective: "capture" is contested; the concentration sits inside a *shrinking* share of GDP.**
`tu:era:7:boundary`: measured US ad spend fell from ~2.3–2.4% of GDP (2000) and 2.0% (2007) to **~1.3% in 2025** while the platforms grew. So "Google captured the ad market" **cannot be asserted from the aggregate series alone**. Silk & Berndt (NBER WP 28161) treat it as an open question, and this record favours a boundary-shift reading (platform revenue includes money older series never counted as advertising). Benchmark: the all-time ad/GDP max was **3.0% in 1922** (`tu:era:2:boundary`). The rhyme is *structural* concentration, not a growing pie being seized.

## Opposition web — "market or state?"

Central question (answerable more than one way): **When the top of the stack concentrates, does the market break it or does the state?**

| Corner | Position | Warrant / citation | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — the market | Builds concentration; does not undo it | network effects + distribution cheques + acquisitions: `mech-aol-001`, `e6-sellers-001`, DoubleClick/Overture/aQuantive buys (`tu:era:6:field:SELLERS`) | driver, not opponent |
| B — the state (constraint/actor) | The only force that has moved the structure | 1943 NBC Blue; 1954 cap `e3-sellers-002`; 1984 divestiture `e4-sellers-004`; 2024–25 Mehta/Brinkema `tu:era:7:event:8` | **constraint w/ documented carriers** (FCC, DOJ, Brinkema) |
| C — supply shock | Deconcentration the state licensed but did not design | postwar station boom `e2-sellers-006`; cable `e4-sellers-001` | environmental, evidenced |
| D — measurement/"capture" skeptic | Concentration ≠ capture; pie is shrinking | ad/GDP 3.0% (1922) → 1.3% (2025) `tu:era:7:boundary` | corrective, evidenced |

No corner is cast as villain-by-losing. The state is a constraint with named carriers, not an antagonist; the market is a driver, not a mind. Personifying verbs ("the market wanted consolidation") are cashed out to mechanisms above.

## Hypothesis diff (what the evidence changed)

- **Before:** "the state, not the market, moves the structure — an external rupture resets concentration each time."
- **After:** the state *moves* the structure but rarely *deconcentrates* it. It relocated ownership (1943, 1984), ceilinged it (1954), or named it unlawful with the remedy pending (2025). The one real deconcentration came from a **supply shock** (era 2 stations), not the divestiture. And concentration recurs **inside a shrinking share of GDP**, so "capture" is the wrong frame.
- **What did not change (worth suspecting):** the raw rhyme — two/three sellers who also run the intermediary layer and keep an outsized, regulator-named cut — holds in 1938 and 2025 alike. That it survived the outcome-blind pass is why it's load-bearing.
