# S2 dig — the middleman's cut was never one number: the broadcast stack (eras 2–3)

**Role.** Narrative Architect (Bash), Discovery Session 2. Facet: the MIDDLEMAN'S CUT braided across
eras 2–3 — the intermediary fee as media and buyers changed — drilled deeper than Session 1's Thread 2

`EXCLUDES` agency). This dig walks the SELLERS / PRICING / BUYERS rows of eras 2–3, where the rest of the

## 0. The one-line finding

**Session 1 said the visible "15%" hid *who knows the price*. Drilling the broadcast eras sharpens it at
the structure layer: the visible "15%" hid *how many hands take a cut, and which cut the rate card shows.*
The middleman's take was never a single number and never the same number twice. It was a stack of three or
four hands. And the only plate anyone could publish a figure for was the smallest one — the disclosed agency
15%.** The independent number was the anomaly here too: the one cut that was public was the least of them.

**Through-line.** For fifty years the received story is "the 15% agency commission" — one stable, disclosed
intermediary fee. The broadcast record denies both the singular and the stability. In the **sponsorship
era** the *same* 15% silently bought a second business. The agency stopped being a space broker and became a
program factory. It delivered a finished show "to the line." So program-and-talent — roughly a *third* of
the sponsor's outlay — rode inside a commission the rate card only ever showed as media. Above the agency
sat the era's **dominant intermediary, the network**. It kept 73% of net time sales and paid 27% to the
affiliates who put the signal in the air. Beneath both, the *original* network middleman was the telephone
company, renting the wires at $2,600 an hour until RCA bought the business. Then, in the **spot-market
era**, buyers stopped buying programs and started buying audiences. The 15% held; it *peaked* at 71%
adoption in 1982. But what it bought collapsed — the production plate was stripped out. And a *new* hand
stacked on top. The station representative took another 5–20%, calculated on the money *left after* the
agency's 15%, plus a 2% cash discount "usually omitted from take-rate comparisons." The cut did not move as
a number. Its **composition** churned while its one public plate held steady. That same structure reappears
in programmatic: Google Ads ~14% + DV360 ~13% + Ad Manager ~18%, with the ≥35¢ open-web take never
disclosed, only alleged.

## 1. Frame lock (narrative-evidence-ledger, Step 1)

- **UNIT:** one intermediary cut taken on an advertiser's dollar as it travels to the audience, in a
  broadcast transaction. *Rejected alt:* "the agency commission rate" alone — that is Session 1's unit and
  it hides every other plate of the stack.
- **DENOMINATOR:** deliberately *not* one base. Each hand computes on a different one. Agency = % of gross
  media billing. Network = % of net time sales *retained*. Station rep = % of the amount *left after* the
  agency's 15%. Production = % of the sponsor's *total* outlay. *Rejected alt:* a single "% of advertiser
  spend." The plates sit on incompatible bases and cannot be summed into one number. **That non-summability
  is the finding**, wired as `e3-pricing-003 CALCULATED_AFTER_DEDUCTION_OF agency commission`.
- **WINDOW:** 1925 (AT&T toll network) → 1982 (commission-adoption peak), with a 2002–2020 programmatic
  coda kept as a *profile*, not spliced. *Rejected alt:* 1875 start (Ayer) — Session 1's window. Starting at
  1925 isolates the broadcast stack from the print-commission origin story.
- **BOUNDARY:** the broadcast intermediary chain — AT&T/RCA network wires → network → affiliate → agency →
  station rep. Excludes classified/direct (no agency, no commission: the un-agencied channels Session 1
  already holds, `e1-buyers-007`, `e3-buyers-005 EXCLUDES advertising agency`). The FCC and the trade texts
  drew these lines, not me. The **network is a seller-intermediary**. The synthesis states it plainly: "the
  networks were **also the era's dominant intermediary**," which "kept 73 percent … and paid just 27 percent
  to affiliates who put the signal in the air" (`tu:era:2:field:SELLERS`, synthesis).

**Instrument check (a real one, and it *is* the story).** The plates we can number to the dollar are
grade A because the state audited them — network 73/27, the affiliate step formula, the volume discounts
(FCC, *Report on Chain Broadcasting*, 1941). The plates that stay soft are the ones only a rate card or a
trade textbook ever covered — production ~⅓ (C, interval 20–55), the station rep 5–20% (C, central is a
construction). The A/C split across the stack is a *measurement* artefact of **who audited what**. It points
the same way as the finding: the hidden plates have numbers at all only because a regulator went and got
them.

## 2. Systemic protagonist — POSIWID sheet (systemic-protagonist)

**Subject:** the **broadcast advertising intermediary stack** (a system, held constant) — the layer of
hands between the advertiser and the audience.

**Character sentence:** *This system reliably converts the advertiser's dollar into a stack of intermediary
cuts. Only its smallest plate is publicly numbered — the disclosed agency 15%. The larger takes stay off the
published rate: network retention, program-production control, the station-rep add-on, the cash discount.*

**Two regimes (required, satisfied):**
- **Sponsorship regime (era 2):** the 15% buys media *and* production (`e2-creators-002 COVERS program
  production` + `media buying`, B); the network retains 73% (`e2-sellers-002`, A); AT&T/RCA rents the wires
  (`e2-pricing-002`, A).
- **Spot / participation regime (era 3):** the 15% holds but buys *only audiences* — production stripped out
  (`e3-buyers-002 sponsorship COLLAPSED_INTO participations`, B); the station rep stacks 5–20% on top
  (`e3-pricing-003`, C); a 2% cash discount hides (`e3-pricing-002 OMITTED_FROM take-rate comparisons`, B).
- **Programmatic coda (era 7), profile not splice:** Google Ads ~14% + DV360 ~13% + Ad Manager ~18%
  (`e7-pricing-006`, B) — the same three-plate shape, top plate disclosed, depth alleged.

**Residual — outcomes the "maximise the hidden cut" function does NOT explain (mandatory, all kept):**
1. **Mutual** retained only **3.5%** on member-station proceeds and *published no rate card*
   (`e2-pricing-006`, A; `Mutual DID_NOT_PUBLISH network rate card`). A middleman that declined the big cut.
   A pure-maximiser function does not predict Mutual.
2. **The affiliate's share rose, not the network's.** NBC's payout to affiliates climbed 20.5% (1928) →
   28.5% (1940) as time sales grew $7.3M → $37.1M (`e2-sellers-007`, A). The dominant intermediary's
   *retention shrank* as the hands beneath it gained leverage.
3. **The take reverses under competition.** Google's syndicated take-rate ratchet ran 9% (2002) → 24.7%
   (2006) → **fell** to 21.3% (2008) "when competition for partners intensifies" (`mech-tac-003`, A). Kept
   as the forward-rhyme residual: the stack is contestable, not monotone.

**Gap (the engine):** the *stated* purpose of the disclosed 15% (a transparent, fixed, fair intermediary
fee) vs the *revealed* function (the disclosed 15% is the smallest, most-public plate of a multi-hand stack
whose real depth is unpublished).

## 3. ABT spine (extract-thread-spine)

- **AND** the agency's cut was born a single disclosed number — 15% of gross media billing, the price Ayer
  made visible in 1875. Through the broadcast era that number held rock steady. It *peaked* at 71% adoption
  among large national advertisers as late as 1982 (`e3-creators-002`, B; `Era 3 EXCLUDES commission
  death`).
- **BUT** the 15% was never the whole cut and never the same cut twice. In radio it silently also bought the
  program factory — the agency "delivered a finished program to the line," and program-and-talent was
  roughly a third of the sponsor's outlay, "the cost the rate card never showed" (`e2-creators-002` B;
  `e2-pricing-008` C). Above it the network kept the biggest slice — 73% retained, 27% out to affiliates
  (`e2-sellers-002`, A). Then TV buyers "stopped buying programmes, started buying audiences"
  (`tu:era:3:field:BUYERS`). The production plate was stripped out. And a new hand — the station rep —
  stacked 5–20% *on the money left after the agency's 15%* (`e3-pricing-003`, C), plus a 2% cash discount
  omitted from every take-rate comparison (`e3-pricing-002`, B).
- **THEREFORE** the "15% that would not die" is the disclosed top plate of a middleman's stack whose real
  depth was never published. That same structure returns in programmatic. The ~14% buy-side plate is
  disclosed and the ≥35¢ open-web take is only ever *alleged* (`e7-pricing-006`, B). The independent number
  was the anomaly at the structure layer too: the one plate anyone could cite was the smallest one.

## 4. Evidence ledger — the stack, plate by plate (best findings, grades preserved)

Each: finding → graph path/edge → claim id (grade, verdict) → the real statement.

**F1 — The keystone: one number, two businesses.** The 15% "held at 15 percent of gross media billing
through the whole era, and **on radio it covered program production as well as media buying**."
Path: era-2 CREATORS → `e2-creators-002` (**B, adjusted**); edge `agency commission COVERS program
production` + `COVERS media buying`. Synthesis corroborates: the agency "stopped being a space broker,"
became a "production company," "wrote the show / hired the star / booked the orchestra … delivered a
finished program to the line," on "the same 15 percent commission on media." So "the marginal cost of a
program to the advertiser looked like zero" (`tu:era:2:field:CREATORS`).

**F2 — The biggest slice was off the card.** "Program and talent production was roughly a third of a
sponsor's total network radio outlay — **the cost the rate card never showed**."
Path: era-2 PRICING → `e2-pricing-008` (**C, confirmed; interval 20–55**); edge `rate card DOES_NOT_SHOW
program and talent production share`. *Do not harden "a third":* the claim's own method note says part of
the residual route may be list-price discounting, not production (Silk & Berndt on the McCann-Erickson
list-price basis). The width is the honesty.

**F3 — The dominant intermediary's cut, audited to the dollar.** "NBC and CBS **retained 73 percent
($32,046,218)** of their network net time sales in 1938 and paid 27 percent ($12,267,560) to the **253
affiliated stations** that carried the programs."
Path: era-2 SELLERS → `e2-sellers-002` (**A, confirmed**). The precision is the point; the FCC audit is why
we have it.

**F4 — The original network middleman was the phone company.** "By the end of 1925 **AT&T was selling time
on its basic thirteen-station toll network at $2,600 per hour**, generating ~$750,000 a year from time
sales **before RCA bought the business**."
Path: era-2 PRICING → `e2-pricing-002` (**A, adjusted**). The first intermediary layer was distribution
itself — renting the wires. And it changed hands (AT&T → RCA, 1926) before advertising money even settled.

**F5 — The pivot: buyers stop buying programs, start buying audiences.** Broadcast was "20% of US national
advertising expenditure in 1956, **at the point when sponsorship was collapsing into participations**."
Path: era-3 BUYERS → `e3-buyers-002` (**B, confirmed**); edge `sponsorship COLLAPSED_INTO participations`.
Mechanism (synthesis, `tu:era:3:field:PRICING` / `:BUYERS`): "Pat Weaver's magazine concept at NBC broke
that — selling participations inside network-owned shows such as Today and Tonight." "Rising production
costs made single sponsorship unaffordable." Buyers now bought "dozens of 30-second units across many shows
… instead of owning one show." This is what strips the production plate out of the agency's 15%.

**F6 — The new hand stacks on top of the old one.** "Station representative firms … took a negotiated
commission in a normal range of about **5–20%, calculated on the amount left after the agency's 15% had been
deducted**." (Central 10 is the range midpoint, a construction, not a source figure; TV averages ran below
radio's.)
Path: era-3 PRICING → `e3-pricing-003` (**C, adjusted; interval 5–20**); edge `station representative firm
commission rate CALCULATED_AFTER_DEDUCTION_OF agency commission rate`. Explicit stacking, wired as an edge.

**F7 — Even the small plate is hidden.** "On top of the 15%, agencies took a **2% cash discount** on the net
for paying media within ~10 days — a second, smaller intermediary take that is **usually omitted from
take-rate comparisons**."
Path: era-3 PRICING → `e3-pricing-002` (**B, confirmed**); edge `cash discount OMITTED_FROM take-rate
comparisons`.

**F8 — The rate peaked here; it did not die here.** "The 15% commission outlived the decree: ANA tracking
studies show **71% of the largest US national advertisers were still** compensating agencies by media
commission" in 1982.
Path: era-3 CREATORS → `e3-creators-002` (**B, confirmed**); edge `Era 3 EXCLUDES commission death`. Note
the 1956 consent decree that killed *enforcement* (`e3-creators-001`, A) **EXCLUDED broadcast advertising**
(`e3-buyers-003 EXCLUDED broadcast advertising`, B). The broadcast stack was never even touched by the
state action usually cited as the commission's end.

**F9 — The forward rhyme (profile, not splice).** "Publishers keep about 69 cents … inside Google's stack
(**Google Ads ~14%, DV360 ~13%, Google Ad Manager ~18%**); the DOJ alleged Google keeps **at least 35 cents**
of every open-web display dollar and that AdX had held a 20% take rate since 2009."
Path: `e7-pricing-006` (**B, confirmed**); support `mech-tac-004` (**A**): a 4.5× retention gap between
owned and syndicated dollars in 2008 (95.5¢ vs 21.3¢), 11× in 2002. The three-plate broadcast stack returns
as a three-plate programmatic stack — top plate disclosed, depth alleged.

**Supporting quantified detail (the stack's internal prices, all FCC-audited, grade A):**
- NBC affiliate step formula — 0% for the first 16 unit hours, then 20% / 30% / 37.5% (`e2-pricing-007`).
- NBC volume discounts 2.5% → 25% for advertisers billing $1.2M/yr, on Red+Blue combined (`e2-pricing-005`).
- Mutual's 3.5% member-station take (`e2-pricing-006`).

## 5. Opposition — constraint inventory (narrative-opposition-web, system/explanatory mode)

Not a four-corner web (system subject, explanatory material). The corpus wired the correctives as real
L0→L0 edges — the built-in BUTs that pre-empt "the middleman's cut was a stable single 15%":

| Constraint / corrective | Edge · claim (grade) | What it kills |
|---|---|---|
| The 15% covered **two** jobs, not one | `agency commission COVERS program production` `e2-creators-002` (B) | "the 15% is comparable across eras" — it bought media in print, media+production in radio, media-only in TV spot |
| The biggest cost was **off the card** | `rate card DOES_NOT_SHOW production share` `e2-pricing-008` (C) | "the disclosed rate is the take" |
| The rep's cut is **stacked, not substituted** | `rep commission CALCULATED_AFTER_DEDUCTION_OF agency` `e3-pricing-003` (C) | "there was one intermediary" |
| Even the 2% is **omitted** | `cash discount OMITTED_FROM take-rate comparisons` `e3-pricing-002` (B) | "take-rate comparisons are complete" |
| The commission **peaked**, not died, in this era | `Era 3 EXCLUDES commission death` `e3-creators-002` (B) | mis-dating the death to the broadcast era |
| The 1956 decree **excluded broadcast** | `1956 consent decree EXCLUDED broadcast advertising` `e3-buyers-003` (B) | "the state ended the broadcast commission" |

**The binding constraint (system framing, not a villain):** the **disclosure / rate-card regime** — only
some plates of the stack are ever numbered. Its documented carrier is the **state's own audit**. The FCC's
1941 *Report on Chain Broadcasting* is the sole reason the network's 73% retention, the affiliate steps and
the volume discounts carry grade-A numbers at all. Competition is the second constraint. It produced
Mutual's 3.5%, the affiliates' rising share, and the reversing programmatic ratchet. No intent is attributed
to "the market."

## 6. Connection to the meta-spine

**Extends it.** Session 1: *the independent number was the anomaly; by default the judged party owns the
number.* Thread 2 rendered that as *who knows the price* on a single agency rate. This dig adds the
**structure-layer twin**: *the middleman's cut was always a stack, and the one plate the market could cite
was the smallest and most public.* The disclosed 15% is the anomaly — the exception that was numbered. The
network's retention, the production control, the rep's add-on and the cash discount were the default: real,
larger, and un-numbered except where a regulator forced them into the light. This is the *same POSIWID gap*
as Thread 1 (who counts) and Thread 2 (who knows the price), now at the take-rate layer.

**Complicates Session 1's Thread 2.** "The 15% that would not die" reads, after this drill, as slightly the
wrong frame. It was not that *one number* survived. It is that **one plate of a churning multi-plate stack
stayed visible** while everything around it changed. The network middleman changed hands (AT&T→RCA). The
production business was folded into the 15% and then stripped back out. A whole new intermediary — the
station rep — was added on top. The buyer's unit went from a show to an audience. The stability of "15%" is
an artefact of measuring the one plate that was public. **The correct unit is the stack, not the rate.**

**Agency reading (required).** Strongest agency reading: *agencies and networks engineered a hidden
multi-layer stack to extract rents behind a fig-leaf 15%.* Considered, **not adopted.** The disclosed 15%
predates broadcast by fifty years — a competition/cartel artefact, not a broadcast design. The hidden plates
are visible to *us* only because the FCC audited them; the actors did not conceal them from the regulator.
Mutual chose 3.5%. Affiliates *gained* share against the network. The take reverses under competition. The
stack is structural, not a designed conspiracy — consistent with the meta-spine's structural reading. Named
parties surfaced, not adjudicated.
