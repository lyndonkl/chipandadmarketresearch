# Breadth sweep 1 — L3 themes ranked 37–72

Narrative architect, breadth sweep. Slice = the 36 smallest substantive L3 themes
(member_count 2–3), pulled via `q.py … SKIP 36 LIMIT 36`. For each I drilled
`L3 →*3→ L0` and pulled the real statement with `claim.py`. Every finding cites node +
claim id + preserves grade/hedge. These are tail themes, so several are thin measurement
plumbing; the flagged ones below carry real narrative charge.

**Method note:** two claim-id shapes appear. `e#-topic-###` are the sourced measurement
claims (grade A–C, CI, sources). `tu:era:#:…` are era timeline units — dated events and
field-summaries, several with an embedded `claim` object. Both trace to the frozen corpus.

## FLAGGED — strong (surprise / opposition / quantified / cross-era / vivid actor)

### 1. Advertising Rating Framework — *the funder shapes the number* (measurement politics)
- `tu:era:2:event:3` (1934): a group of magazine publishers funded **Clark-Hooper's**
  telephone-coincidental rating. Their promotion lead, McCall's Don Parsons, believed the
  advertiser-funded CAB was **inflating radio's audience**. "The first coincidental ratings did
  come in lower… the cleanest demonstration in the whole history that the funder of a
  measurement shapes the number it produces."
- `e2-measurement-002` (grade B): only **31% of US families had a telephone in 1934-35**,
  while **96% of telephone subscribers owned radios** — a structural upward bias baked into
  every telephone-based rating of the era.
- Why promising: named actors, a documented opposition over a *number*, and a mechanism
  (sampling frame bias) you can prove. Anchor of a cross-era **"who controls measurement"** spine.

### 2. Advertising Measurement Governance — *buyers lose the scoreboard* (cross-era)
- `tu:era:2:event:9` (1946-07-31): after the Zeisel study exposed why Hooper's numbers ran
  ~20% above the CAB's, the buyer-controlled Cooperative Analysis of Broadcasting was wound up;
  **"sixteen years of buyer-controlled measurement ended."** Nielsen's meter service reached
  63% of US homes in 1946 and **97% by April 1949**, then bought Hooper's national service in
  1950. "Measurement passed permanently from a committee of buyers to private vendors."
- Why promising: a clean, dated power-transfer of the measurement layer — rhymes directly with
  today's platform-controlled measurement (see #5). Pairs with #1 as the same spine, 1930→2025.

### 3. Deceptive Data Practices — *the 177% jump that did not happen* (self-aware / meta)
- `e5-medium-006` (grade B): the Coen "Out of Home" category (\$4.780B, 1999) is 2.77× the
  "Billboards" category (\$1.725B) it replaced; billboards was dropped in 2000, so **"any chart
  that splices the two lines without annotation manufactures a 177% jump that did not happen."**
- Why promising: a rare *meta* thread — the very charts that would illustrate this history can
  lie by definitional splice. Surprising, quantified, and a built-in caution the piece can wear openly.

### 4. Workforce Reduction Metrics — *marchFIRST, the dot-com collapse in one line* (vivid actor)
- `e5-creators-004` (grade B): marchFIRST posted a **\$437M net loss on \$369M of sales** in Q3
  2000; cut **~2,100 jobs (~30% of staff)** from Nov 2000, filed **Chapter 11 on 13 Apr 2001**,
  converted to **Chapter 7 liquidation on 1 May 2001** — "the interactive-agency sector's
  collapse in one line."
- Why promising: a single named body with a fast, brutal arc; concrete face for the era-5 bust.

### 5. App-based User Tracking — *a platform rule beats every privacy law* (surprise / quantified)
- `tu:era:7:event:5` / `e7-events-006` (grade B): Apple shipped App Tracking Transparency in
  iOS 14.5 on **2021-04-26**; opt-in ran **~16%**. **Meta told investors ATT would cost it
  ~\$10B of revenue in 2022.** "It was a platform rule, not a law, and it cost the largest
  buyer of mobile signal more than any privacy statute had."
- Why promising: quantified power move, surprising (rule > law), modern bookend to the
  measurement-control spine (#1/#2).

### 6. Advertising Structural Shift — *the death of the 15% commission, mis-dated* (myth-buster)
- `e6-creators-002` (grade B): the share of US advertisers paying agencies on a billings
  (commission) basis fell to **~10% by 2003, from 61% in 1994** — and "the collapse followed
  the **1991-94 unbundling** of media buying from creative, **not the arrival of search
  advertising.**"
- Why promising: a causal correction against the obvious story (search killed the agency).
  Great for a proportion/attribution beat. Supporting figure: agency income \$3.17B (1977) →
  \$13.61B (1992), `e4-creators-003` (grade A).

### 7. Regulated Product Categories — *patent medicine and the birth of the legitimacy problem* (origin)
- `tu:era:1:event:6` (1905-10-07): Samuel Hopkins Adams's *Collier's* "The Great American
  Fraud" showed the leading cure-alls were **alcohol, opiates and poisons** and named the papers
  carrying them; the **Pure Food and Drug Act (1906)** followed. "The industry's biggest single
  advertiser category was regulated out of its old form, and advertising's own legitimacy became
  the trade's central problem."
- Why promising: vivid origin scene; the recurring "advertising vs. its own legitimacy" motif.

### 8. Broadcast Media / Telecommunication Services — *renting airtime like a phone circuit* (origin)
- `tu:era:2:event:0` (1922-08-28): AT&T's WEAF sold the Queensboro Corporation a ten-minute
  talk; **AT&T's "toll broadcasting" theory** was that a broadcaster rents airtime the way the
  phone company rents a circuit — "the seller supplies the pipe, the buyer supplies the message."
  (Price disputed: \$50/10min vs \$100/15min, `e2-pricing-001`.)
- Why promising: the conceptual origin of the entire ad-supported broadcast model; both themes
  point at the same event, so the telco lineage of advertising is real and citable.

### 9. Inventory Take-Rate / Pricing Framework — *the middleman's cut, quantified* (quantified move)
- `tu:era:6:field:PRICING`: Google's traffic-acquisition cost consumed **91% of network ad
  revenue in 2002, 84% in 2003, 79% in 2004** — i.e. a take rate on others' inventory of
  **~9% rising to ~21% by 2008**; on total ad revenue TAC peaked at **39% in 2004**, fell to
  **28.1% in 2008**. Entry price engineered trivially low: 5-cent minimum bid + \$5 activation
  vs Overture's 10-cent + \$20/month.
- Why promising: hard numbers on how an intermediary's economics evolve; feeds an auction/take-rate thread.

### 10. Precision Targeting Tools / Targeting & Matching — *the query as the unit; brand names as inventory* (surprise/litigation)
- `tu:era:6:field:TARGETING`: "the era's targeting unit was the **query, not the person**." In
  **April 2004** Google reversed policy to let advertisers **bid on third parties' trademarks**,
  "converting brand names into auctionable inventory and forcing brand owners to bid for their
  own names." **GEICO sued**; Dec 2004 Judge Brinkema ruled from the bench that selling a
  trademark as a keyword did not by itself violate the Lanham Act; settled 2005.
- Why promising: surprising mechanism + named legal fight + a clean "intent vs audience" contrast.

## FLAGGED — medium (feeds a thread; strong number or line)

- **US Advertising Spend Levels** `tu:era:2:field:SCALE`: advertising's share of GDP hit
  **3.0% in 1922 — the highest in the entire 1919-2007 Coen/BEA series** — then fell to a 1.2%
  wartime low in 1944. "Anyone arguing a later era 'captured' an unprecedented share of the
  economy has to explain 1922 first." Cross-era myth-buster; pairs with the next.
- **US Media Owner Ad Revenue Metrics** `tu:era:7:field:SCALE`: US ad intensity is only
  **~1.3% of GDP in 2025** (MAGNA \$398B / eMarketer \$422B — different universes), *below* the
  2.0% of 2007 and 2.3-2.4% of the 2000 peak, **even as the platforms grew**. Silk & Berndt
  (NBER WP 28161): "a growth problem, a measurement problem, or both." The era's central paradox.
- **Performance Outcomes / Ad Product Revenue** `e5-unit_econ-003` (grade C): Yahoo's audited
  gross margin per web impression was **86.5% in 2000** (78.1% in 2001). The paradox is the
  contrast — print-beating margins alongside catastrophic operating results, because selling an
  impression cost far more than serving it. Ad networks made only 25-40%, since they paid the
  publisher out of the same impression. A surprising unit-economics beat.
- **Media Scheduling Metrics** `tu:era:3:event:4` (1962): ABC concentrated premiere week after
  Labor Day to align with **car-makers' model-year launches**, and sold the season in advance —
  the birth of the **upfront**, a clearing institution still live today. Origin story.
- **Advertising Industry Meetups** `tu:era:2:event:2` / `e2-events-002` (grade B, 1930): after a
  Yale Club meeting, Crossley's CAB began the first continuous national audience survey
  (**17,000 radio families/report across 50 cities**) at his own financial risk —
  "broadcasting became priceable the moment it became countable." Feeds the measurement spine.
- **Traffic Metrics** `tu:era:7:event:4` (2019-09-05): Google Ad Manager completed the move to a
  **unified first-price auction**; buyers responded with **bid shading**, so the clearing price
  became a function of the buyer's model, not the runner-up's bid. Feeds auction-mechanics thread.
- **Ad Impression Measurement** `mech-knobs-001` (grade **A**): the Mehta opinion names three
  Google pricing knobs — **squashing** ("Butternut Squash"), format pricing, rGSP — all called
  "intentional pricing" internally; the squashing exponent "has never been publicly disclosed,
  so its price effect cannot be reproduced." Also `e5-measurement-002` (grade B): **172B US
  impressions in Q4 2000**; Amazon alone bought 6.1B. Court-grade primary source on price manipulation.
