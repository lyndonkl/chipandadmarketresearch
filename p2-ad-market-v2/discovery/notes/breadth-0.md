## FLAGGED THREADS (ranked by narrative strength)

### A. "Who counts the audience?" — the recurring measurement-legitimacy crisis (STRONGEST)
Cross-era spine, opposition-driven, quantified, spans 1914→2026. The market's oldest discipline is
that a *third party* counts the audience; the recurring crisis is the seller counting its own results.
- **`tu:era:6:field:MEASUREMENT`** — "This is the era in which the seller became the auditor." Google
  recorded the impression, ran the auction, decided which click was valid, billed against its own log;
  no third party audited in period (IAB Click Measurement WG convened late 2005, guidelines not
  published until 2009). Lane's Gifts sued 2005; Google settled Mar 2006 for **up to $90M** in credits;
  court appointed NYU's Tuzhilin — "an auditor commissioned by the defendant and paid for by a settlement."
- **`e6-measurement-002`** (grade **C**) — independent estimators (Click Forensics) put invalid-click
  rates in the **mid-teens (~14–17%), central 15%, CI80 [3,20]**, 2007–08, vs Google's "low single digits";
  "no accredited third party able to adjudicate." (Note: different denominators — surfaced in the claim.)
- **`tu:era:7:field:MEASUREMENT`** — "the century-old rule that a third party counts the audience finally
  broke." Facebook overstated video view time for ~2 years (plaintiffs: up to **900%**), and paid **$40M**
  (2019). The MRC stripped Nielsen TV accreditation Sept 2021 over 2–6% pandemic undercounts (VAB valued
  ≥**$468M**), then restored it only Apr 2023 — 19 months later. By 2026 the IAB reports **bots >50% of web
  traffic, ~40% malicious**.
- **`tu:era:2:field:TARGETING`** — the thesis in one line: **"Nothing narrower was purchasable, because
  nothing narrower was countable."** Measurability gates the entire market.
- Rhyme back to origin: **`tu:era:1:field:PRICING`** / Circulation & Title Metrics — era 1 rate cards
  were fiction "because circulation was unverifiable," fixed only by the Audit Bureau of Circulations (1914).
- Themes touched: Advertising Measurement & Analytics (#1), Ad Measurement & Metrics Framework (#2),
  Advertising Market Vulnerabilities (#31: "The counting crisis arrived as click fraud" / "Programmatic
  added its own counting crisis"), Click Fraud Measurement & Detection (#25), Quantitative Market Metrics,
  Open‑Web Inventory Quality Issues, Advertising measurement standards.

### B. The 15% commission — one 1875 contract reform, a century-long cartel, two deaths
Cross-era; kills a tempting teleology (that *digital* killed the agency model).
- **`tu:era:1:field:CREATORS`** + **`tu:era:1:field:PRICING`** — agency invented as a *space broker*
  (Palmer, Philadelphia 1841–42, paid by publishers, ~25%); commissions ran a hidden **10–50%**; N.W.
  Ayer's **open contract (1875)** put the agent on the buyer's side, disclosed the rate, fixed the
  percentage. It "stabilised at 15% by the early 1890s"; Curtis no-rebating (1891) + publishers' 1893
  "recognised agents" resolution "turned the 15% into a cartel price that survived until the 1990s."
- **`e6-creators-002`** (grade **B**) — billings-based agency compensation fell from **61% (1994) to ~10%
  (2003)**; "the collapse followed the 1991–94 unbundling of media buying from creative, **not** the arrival
  of search advertising." (Explicit anti-teleology anchor.)
- **`tu:era:7:field:CREATORS`** — the rhyme closes: the ANA/K2 report (2016) "documented pervasive
  undisclosed rebates in US media buying" — hidden rebates return a century after the open contract killed
  them; 82% of ANA members ran an in-house agency by 2023; Omnicom–IPG closed 2025-11-26 (~$25–26B holdco).
- Themes: Advertising Structural Shift (#33: "unbundling of media buying from creative"), Advertising
  financial measurement suite, Ad Pricing & Measurement Regime, Advertising Financial Metrics, In‑House Media Planning.

### C. Measurability creates culture — the soap opera as a pricing artifact (vivid, causal)
- **`tu:era:2:field:TARGETING`** — a daytime network hour cost exactly *half* an evening hour, and the
  daytime audience was overwhelmingly women at home; "**Procter & Gamble and its agencies built the soap
  opera to occupy cheap hours in front of the people who bought soap.**" National spot radio $15M→$123M
  (1935–49). A price rule created a cultural form — a rare clean, sourced causal claim.

### D. "Intent money" / response — the same pool migrates across five media (cross-era)
- **`tu:era:4:field:MEDIUM`** — "The share chart of this era does not look like the story usually told."
  Direct mail grew into the gap: **$4.79B (14.4%) 1976 → $27.27B (19.3%) 1993**, third-largest medium and
  largest *national* medium; behind it, US third-class mail volume **30.4B → 65.8B pieces**. TV first passed
  newspapers only in 1992 ($31.1B vs $30.7B). **Yellow Pages reached $9.5B and passed both radio and magazines.**
- **`tu:era:6:field:BUYERS`** — the "intent money" (classified, directories, lead-gen, catalogue/mail-order)
  moved to search. AdWords removed Overture's gates (5¢ bid, $5 activation, no minimum, auto-approval). That
  turned the buyer base "from a directory of firms into a long tail of plumbers, locksmiths, affiliate marketers."
- **`e3-scale-010`** (grade **B**) — classified $377M (1950) → **$2,159M (1975)**, 18.2%→26.2% of newspaper
  revenue — the rise before the collapse (Classified Ad Revenue Metrics #20 holds the automotive/real-estate/
  recruitment breakdown at the 2000 peak). Themes: US Mail Advertising Metrics (#6), Ad Spend Metrics Suite.

### E. The wartime tax subsidy — the government paid ~80% of advertising (surprising, quantified)
- **`e2-events-005`** (grade **C**) — under the wartime excess-profits tax, "a dollar of deductible
  advertising cost roughly **20 cents after tax**" (CI80 [0.10, 0.28]) for an excess-profits payer. Reframes
  the 1940s ad boom as a fiscal artifact. Themes: Advertising Taxation & Accounting (#24), Advertising
  Spend & Revenue Metrics (Ad Council founded alongside the Office of War Information).

### F. AI Overviews collapse the click — the live frontier (2024–25, quantified)
- **`tu:era:7:event:7` / `e7-events-008`** (grade **B**) — result clicks on **8% of AI-summary visits vs
  15% without** (Pew, Mar 2025 panel; CI80 [7,10]). Ads followed into the unit Oct 2024. The measurement/
  attribution crisis (Thread A) meets a demand-side shock. Themes: Ad interaction and performance metrics
  (#17), Open‑Web Inventory Quality Issues (#26).

### G. The sell side invented the market — free/below-cost media as the original engine
- **`tu:era:1:field:SELLERS`** — "The structural invention of the era belongs to the sell side." Benjamin
  Day's penny *Sun* (1833) sold *below cost* and said so; Munsey cut *Munsey's* to 10¢ (1893), the ten-cent
  magazine sold below production cost "and let advertising make up the difference." By 1914 advertising
  supplied **~65% of newspaper gross income** (vs ~44% in 1879). The ad-funded-free-media model is 1833, not 1998
  (note `tu:era:1:event:0` explicitly links it forward to "the portal"). 22,754 papers in 1914 = no pricing
  power = why brokers existed.

### H. Meta / dataset-integrity threads (for a piece *about the numbers*)
- **`tu:era:1:boundary`** — the corpus flags the famous half-my-advertising quote: "the Wanamaker line is
  an attributed legend"; and carries *two conflicting spend lineages* (Historical Statistics vs Coen 1999,
  ~15% apart) across 1919–34 — honest measurement uncertainty baked in.
- Deceptive Data Practices / Ad Creative Elements (#28): **`e5-buyers-014`** "a chart must not sort these two
  bars"; Advertising Metrics & Discourse: "the pp gap is a GDP-vintage difference"; Dataset Quality Assessment
