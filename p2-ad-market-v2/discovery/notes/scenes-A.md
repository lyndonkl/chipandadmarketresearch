# Scene-seeds A — mined from deep-1, deep-2, deep-3

Scope: the three deep-dig threads (Who counts the audience; The 15% that would not die; The
state moves the top of the stack). Method: SCAM gate + telling-detail + numbers-in-narrative,
run over the frozen claims only.

**Vocabulary contract:** every detail below is *sourced*, not verified. I track provenance;
I cannot confirm truth. Grades and hedges are the sources' own and are preserved.

**Form verdict for the whole corpus.** This is a documentary record — ratings books, FCC
filings, SEC exhibits, court opinions, census tables. Nobody wrote down the room, the light,
the gesture. So almost nothing here qualifies as a full SCAM scene (Setting + Character +
Action + Meaning, all with physical specifics). Most seeds are **telling details** or
**summary/exhibit** moments. Two seeds (SS15 NBC-Blue, SS16 Dischler) reach *scene distance*
— dated, located, named actor, ordered action — but even they have no sensory interior in the
record, and it must not be invented. Where a scene is wanted and the record is thin, I emit a
GAP, not prose.

Convention per seed: **moment in a line / claim id(s)+grade / sourced detail (verbatim in
"quotes" only where the record is verbatim) / SCAM slots / form verdict / the one telling
detail / number landed / vividness / caveat.**

## THREAD #1 — Who counts the audience

### SS1 — A rating written into a contract
- **Moment:** In January 1949 CBS bought Jack Benny from NBC by guaranteeing his sponsor a
  cash rebate for every rating point he might lose in the move.
- **Claim:** `e2-measurement-008` (B, confirmed; Beville 1988, quoting Robert Metz).
- **Sourced detail:** "CBS guaranteed American Tobacco $3,000 a week for every Hooper rating
  point Jack Benny lost by moving from NBC to CBS in January 1949 — a rating written directly
  into a commercial contract."
- **SCAM:** S = Jan 1949, no room recorded [thin]. C(system) = the Hooper point, priced at
  $3,000/wk. A = one event (the guarantee), not three ordered [empty]. M = the count had
  become the currency itself.
- **Form: TELLING DETAIL.** Fails Action (single event) and Setting (no physical specifics).
  Superb anchor line, not a full scene.
- **Telling detail:** a rating *point* — an abstraction — carries a hard weekly dollar tag.
- **Number:** $3,000 per Hooper point per week (1949 dollars). Land as: the count was money.
- **Vivid:** the moment audience measurement stops describing the market and *becomes* the
  contract term.

### SS2 — The rival medium buys the audit
- **Moment:** In autumn 1934 a group of magazine publishers paid for a phone survey in
  sixteen cities designed to prove radio was overcounting its own listeners.
- **Claim:** `e2-measurement-004` (B, Beville 1988); field summary era-2 MEASUREMENT.
- **Sourced detail:** Clark-Hooper's first syndicated coincidental survey covered 16 cities
  in autumn 1934, "financed by a group of magazine publishers who believed the CAB overstated
  network radio's audience." The coincidental call ran — per Beville — as "Were you listening
  to the radio just now?" **The first coincidental ratings did come in lower, which is what
  the publishers had paid for.**
- **SCAM:** S = autumn 1934, 16 cities [located, coarse]. C(system) = the coincidental method,
  state = lower ratings than CAB's recall method. A = publishers fund → survey runs → ratings
  come in lower. M = whoever pays the counter shapes the count.
- **Form: TELLING DETAIL / near-scene.**
- **Telling detail:** a *rival medium* bankrolled the audit of its competitor — and got the
  answer it bought.
- **Caveat:** the call script "Were you listening to the radio just now?" is reported by
  Beville (secondary, 1988), not lifted from a survey transcript. Render as **reported**, not
  as a verbatim quoted line. Bright-line #1 applies.

### SS3 — Same method, different answers, and the cooperative dies
- **Moment:** On 7 February 1946 Marion Harper Jr. presented a study showing two ratings
  services using the *identical* method still disagreed by a fifth; within months the
  industry's own cooperative was gone.
- **Claim:** `e2-measurement-003` (B; Beville 1988 ch.1 & appendix D, Zeisel study presented
  by Marion Harper Jr., 7 Feb 1946).
- **Sourced detail:** After the CAB adopted Hooper's coincidental technique in 1944, "Hooper's
  ratings still ran about 20 percent above the CAB's on average"; Hans Zeisel traced the
  residual gap to ring counts, unidentified-program handling and busy-signal treatment. Hooper
  absorbed the CAB's accounts that July.
- **SCAM:** S = 7 Feb 1946, an industry presentation [no room]. C(system) = two counters, one
  method, ~20% apart. A = Zeisel traces the gap → Harper presents it → cooperative collapses →
  Hooper absorbs accounts (July). M = same technique, different answers, is what made it fatal.
- **Form: SUMMARY + dated actor.** A real named human (Marion Harper Jr.) doing a dated thing.
- **Number:** ~20% gap (CI 15-25), on the *same* method. Direction-before-magnitude: Hooper's
  ran *higher*, by about a fifth.

### SS4 — The instrument, not the audience
- **Moment:** In September 1987 Nielsen's people meters became the national currency and the
  measured prime-time audience fell about a tenth overnight — the audience had not changed,
  the meter had.
- **Claim:** `e4-measurement-001` (B; Christian Science Monitor, 6 Jan 1988).
- **Sourced detail:** the people meters "measured about 10% fewer people watching network
  prime time than the previous method: CBS and ABC each down 13%, NBC down 4%."
- **SCAM:** S = Sept 1987 [dated, no place]. C(system) = the meter, state = −10% measured
  audience. A = new instrument goes live → measured audience drops → networks lose inventory
  value. M = the count moved with the instrument, not the world.
- **Form: TELLING DETAIL / SUMMARY.**
- **Telling detail:** CBS and ABC each lost 13% of their audience without a single viewer
  leaving the couch — a **residual where the honest count hurt the seller** (Thread #1's
  anti-fatalism ballast).
- **Number:** −10% overall; CBS −13%, ABC −13%, NBC −4%. One number per sentence.

### SS5 — The seller becomes the auditor
- **Moment:** In March 2006 Google settled a click-fraud suit and the court appointed an
  examiner to inspect Google's own click-counting — an auditor commissioned by the defendant
  and paid out of the settlement.
- **Claim:** `e6-measurement-001` (B; Search Engine Watch 2006, Tuzhilin report 2006, Google
  AdWords blog); field summary era-6 MEASUREMENT.
- **Sourced detail:** Google settled *Lane's Gifts v. Google* in March 2006 "for up to $90
  million, mostly in advertising credits," and the settlement produced the Tuzhilin report —
  NYU's Alexander Tuzhilin examining Google's invalid-click detection, "commissioned through
  the settlement rather than by an independent industry auditor." In the same era Google
  "recorded the impression, ran the auction, decided which click was valid, and billed against
  its own log."
- **SCAM:** S = March 2006, court settlement [dated, coarse place]. C(system) = the search
  auction, state = counter and seller are the same firm. A = Lane's Gifts sues (2005) → Google
  settles ≤$90M in credits → court names Tuzhilin to examine Google's own systems. M = the
  party being judged now produces its own number — the pre-1914 condition, at platform scale.
- **Form: SUMMARY + named human.** Tuzhilin is a real, named, dated actor.
- **Telling detail:** the auditor of the counter was *paid by the counter*.
- **Number:** up to $90M, "mostly in advertising credits" — keep the qualifier; it is not cash.

### SS6 — Publishes the rate, never the count
- **Moment:** In its Q4 2008 earnings release Google reported that paid clicks grew about 18%
  — and never told anyone how many clicks that was.
- **Claim:** `e6-measurement-006` (**A**; Google Q4 2008 Form 8-K, exhibit 99.1).
- **Sourced detail:** Google disclosed "aggregate paid-click growth ... but never an absolute
  click count, an average CPC, or a query count"; aggregate paid clicks rose approximately 18%
  year over year in Q4 2008.
- **SCAM:** S = Q4 2008 filing [dated document]. C(system) = the disclosure regime, state =
  a growth rate with no denominator. A = files the 8-K → reports +18% growth → withholds the
  count. M = the seller publishes the rate the market cannot audit and withholds the count it
  could.
- **Form: TELLING DETAIL (grade-A hinge).**
- **Telling detail:** a number-shaped hole — the disclosure is a *rate of change* of a figure
  never disclosed.
- **Number:** +18% YoY, Q4 2008. The vividness is the negative space around it.

### SS7 — The century-old rule breaks
- **Moment:** In October 2019 Facebook paid $40M to settle claims it had overstated video
  watch time for two years; advertisers alleged the error inflated the figure by up to 900%.
- **Claim:** `e7-measurement-001` (B; Variety, 7 Oct 2019).
- **Sourced detail:** Facebook agreed to "$40M to settle an advertiser class action over video
  metrics it had overstated for roughly two years from 2015-16; plaintiffs *alleged* the
  exclusion of sub-three-second views inflated average watch time by up to 900%."
- **Form: TELLING DETAIL.**
- **Telling detail:** the seller's own instrument over-reported the exact thing the seller was
  paid for.
- **Number:** $40M settlement; "up to 900%" — **preserve "alleged"** (it is a plaintiff claim,
  not a finding). Relative-vs-absolute: 900% is the plaintiffs' relative figure; no absolute
  base is in the record.

### SS8 — The surviving auditor bites
- **Moment:** In September 2021 the Media Rating Council suspended Nielsen's TV accreditation
  over a pandemic-era undercount; networks put the cost near half a billion dollars.
- **Claim:** `e7-measurement-002` (B; AdExchanger 2021, MRC 2023, TV Tech).
- **Sourced detail:** MRC "suspended Nielsen's national and local TV accreditation in
  September 2021 after finding pandemic-era undercounts of 2-6% among adults 18-49; the VAB
  estimated the undercount cost networks at least $468M ... National TV accreditation was
  reinstated on 2023-04-17."
- **Form: SUMMARY / mandatory residual.** The through-institution (MRC, b.1964) still has
  teeth — blocks the fatalism reading of Thread #1.
- **Number:** 2-6% undercount → ≥$468M (VAB estimate; a separate VAB out-of-home analysis:
  $350M-$700M). Keep both the range and the estimator's name.

## THREAD #2 — The 15% that would not die

### SS9 — The open contract (keystone, thin on scene)
- **Moment:** In 1875 N. W. Ayer & Son offered advertisers the "open contract": the agent
  would work for the buyer, disclose the media rate, and take a fixed percentage.
- **Claim:** field summary era-1 CREATORS; nearest dated figure `e1-pricing-002` (B — the
  commission "stabilised at 15 percent of the gross space rate by the early 1890s").
- **SCAM:** S = 1875 [date only, no place/artifact]. C = Ayer. A = offers the open contract →
  discloses the rate → income no longer depends on hiding the price. M = "once income no
  longer depended on hiding the price, the agency had to sell something else."
- **Form: SUMMARY / KEYSTONE.** No located moment, no primary artifact quoting the contract.
- **GAP (reporting):** to seed an actual scene here, need a dated 1875 primary — the open-
  contract text, an Ayer ledger entry, or a client letter. Not in the claim set. **Escalate.**

### SS10 — Before the reform: the hidden, negotiable cut
- **Moment:** In the 1850s a dozen agents each claimed to represent every paper of importance,
  and the commission one could extract on a placement ran anywhere from 10% to 50%.
- **Claim:** `e1-creators-001` (B; Palmer paid by publishers ~25%); era-1 CREATORS summary
  ("about 10 to 50 percent"). Puffery colour: `e1-creators-002` (**C**, self-reported) —
  Palmer's 1849 claim to be sole agent for 1,300 of ~2,000 US newspapers.
- **Form: SUMMARY.**
- **Telling detail:** the *same* placement could carry a 10% cut or a 50% cut — price was
  hidden, so price was whatever could be hidden.
- **Caveat:** the 1,300-newspapers figure is grade C, self-reported agency puffery; interval
  widened down to 700. Do not present as fact.

### SS11 — The census refutes the legend (exhibit, not scene)
- **Moment:** The commission the trade keeps declaring dead held near 14% straight through the
  1980s and did not fall until after 1992.
- **Claim:** `e4-pricing-001` (**A**; census of media commissions received).
- **Sourced detail:** 14.13% (1977), 13.92% (1982), 14.16% (1987), 13.18% (1992), then 10.98%
  (1997). "The 15% commission survived this era largely intact; its collapse comes after it."
- **Form: SUMMARY / EXHIBIT — not a scene.** A five-point series belongs in a chart. It is the
  load-bearing corrective that kills "15% died in the 1980s" (CONTRADICTED). Ship it as the
  exhibit the scenes hang off, not as prose.
- **Number rule:** four near-identical figures — the *flatness* is the point; keep the digits.

### SS12 — The cartel dies in court, the commission peaks anyway
- **Moment:** On 1 February 1956 a consent decree ended the trade body's collective
  enforcement of the 15% — and twenty-six years later 71% of big advertisers were still paying
  it.
- **Claims:** `e3-creators-001` (**A**; complaint filed 12 May 1955 SDNY, final judgment 1 Feb
  1956) + `e3-creators-002` (B; 71% of largest advertisers still on commission in 1982).
- **SCAM:** S = SDNY, 1955-56 [dated, court]. C = the "recognition" system / the 4As. A =
  complaint filed → decree entered → collective enforcement ends. M = the *legal* cartel died
  in 1956; the *custom* peaked in 1982.
- **Form: SUMMARY + dated legal event.**
- **Telling detail:** the gap itself — 1956 (legal death) vs 1982 (71% peak). The number that
  refused to die outlived the law that once enforced it.

### SS13 — The price goes dark again
- **Moment:** By the 2010s agencies had moved to "principal media" — buying inventory in bulk
  and reselling it to their own clients at an undisclosed markup — and a 2016 report found the
  hidden rebates were pervasive.
- **Claim:** field summary era-7 CREATORS (ANA/K2 2016); backed dated figure `e7-creators-002`
  (B; 82% of ANA members ran an in-house agency by 2023, up from 78% in 2018).
- **Form: SUMMARY.**
- **Telling detail:** the trade Ayer reformed *away* — concealed media price as intermediary
  income — is back, now called "principal media."
- **Caveat / HYPOTHESIS:** the 1875 disclosure reform ↔ 2016 re-concealment rhyme is a hedged
  L4 hypothesis in deep-2, **not** a graph-proven causal identity. Keep it a hypothesis.
- **GAP (reporting):** the ANA/K2 2016 report is narrated in the era-7 summary but has no
  dedicated sourced claim in the set. To lead on it, request the claim. **Escalate.**

## THREAD #3 — The state moves the top of the stack

### SS14 — Two firms, half the market, counted to the dollar (1938)
- **Moment:** In 1938 the FCC's audited ledger showed two companies, NBC and CBS, taking
  nearly half of all US broadcast net time sales — and keeping nearly three-quarters of what
  their own networks billed.
- **Claims:** `e2-sellers-001` (**A**) + `e2-sellers-002` (**A**); FCC Report on Chain
  Broadcasting (1941).
- **Sourced detail:** NBC + CBS took 44% of the industry's **$100,892,259** of net time sales
  in 1938; their 23 owned stations a further 7%. The networks **retained 73% ($32,046,218)**
  of network net time sales and paid **27% ($12,267,560)** to the **253 affiliated stations**.
- **Form: SUMMARY / EXHIBIT.**
- **Telling detail:** the figure to the last dollar — **$100,892,259** — the whole industry's
  net time sales as the FCC counted it. The precision *is* the point: the state counted the
  seller to the dollar.
- **Number:** dominant seller (44% + 7%) = dominant intermediary (73% keep). Two shares, one
  actor.

### SS15 — The Life Savers man buys a network the state forced loose (1943)
- **Moment:** On 12 October 1943, after the FCC's chain-broadcasting rules forced RCA to sell
  one of its two networks, the man who made Life Savers bought NBC Blue for $8 million and
  renamed it the American Broadcasting Company.
- **Claim:** `e2-events-006` (B; Wikipedia & Britannica, 'Blue Network').
- **Sourced detail:** "Edward J. Noble paid $8 million for NBC's Blue Network on 12 October
  1943." Event desc: Noble, "the Life Savers owner who already held WMCA," acquired "the Blue
  name, the line leases, the New York studios, two and a half stations and roughly 60 affiliate
  agreements, and renamed the company the American Broadcasting Company. Antitrust, not
  technology, created the third network."
- **SCAM:** S = 12 Oct 1943; New York studios [dated + located]. C = Noble, the confectioner
  who now owned a radio network; the Blue Network as a bundle of leases, studios, 2.5 stations,
  ~60 affiliates. A = FCC forces RCA to divest → Noble buys Blue for $8M → renames it ABC.
  M = the third network was made by antitrust, not invention.
- **Form: SCENE-SEED (closest in the set to a full SCAM scene).** Setting + Character + Action
  + Meaning all fill from the record.
- **Telling detail:** *the Life Savers owner* buying a broadcast network — the incongruity is
  in the record, not manufactured.
- **Caveat:** the rich specifics (Life Savers, "two and a half stations," ~60 affiliates, the
  New York studios) live in the **event description**, sourced to the two encyclopedia entries
  (grade B); only the $8M + date is the formal claim. Do **not** add a single sensory detail
  about the studios — none is in the record.

### SS16 — The knobs enter the public record (2023 courtroom)
- **Moment:** On 18 September 2023, in the DOJ search trial, a Google executive testified that
  the company nudges its ad prices to hit quarterly targets and does not tell advertisers — and
  the court later found it had tested how far it could push and been told, in effect, as far as
  it liked.
- **Claims:** `e7-events-007` (**A**; Mehta FOF ¶254) + `mech-knobs-001` (**A**) +
  `mech-mehta-004` (**A**); all *United States v. Google LLC*, Mem. Op. (D.D.C. 5 Aug 2024).
- **Sourced detail:** Jerry Dischler testified Google adjusts auction and reserve prices —
  "typically about 5%, up to 10% on some queries — to hit quarterly revenue targets, and ...
  does not tell advertisers." The opinion named three knobs: "squashing (launched internally
  as 'Butternut Squash'), format pricing, and rGSP," all "described internally as 'intentional
  pricing'." The court found Google "had repeatedly tested whether it could raise text-ad
  prices by 5% or more without losing substantial advertisers — **'and the results have been
  largely consistent — it can'**."
- **SCAM:** S = 18 Sept 2023, DOJ search trial, D.D.C. [dated + located courtroom]. C(system)
  = the text-ads auction, state = prices tuned ~5% to hit quarterly targets, opaque to buyers.
  A = Dischler testifies → the knobs enter the record → Mehta's opinion names them. M = the
  seller set the clearing price and did not tell the buyers.
- **Form: SCENE-SEED (courtroom).** The inner quote — "the results have been largely
  consistent — it can" — is **verbatim from the written court opinion**, so it clears
  bright-line #1 (contemporaneous written record).
- **Telling detail:** the internal codename **"Butternut Squash"** for a pricing lever — a real
  name in the findings of fact.
- **Number caveat:** the squashing exponent "has never been publicly disclosed, so its price
  effect cannot be reproduced from the record." Do not quantify the effect; land the ~5% test,
  not a manufactured impact figure.

### SS17 — The fourth state rupture (2024-25)
- **Moment:** On 5 August 2024 a federal judge ruled Google an illegal monopolist in search
  and search text ads; eight months later a second judge found it had monopolised the open-web
  ad server and exchange.
- **Claims:** `tu:era:7:event:8`; `e7-events-009` (**A**; Mehta Mem. Op.) + `mech-mehta-004`
  (**A**).
- **Sourced detail:** the court found Google held 89.2% of US general search queries and 88%
  of general search text advertising and "had charged supracompetitive prices for text ads";
  "Google paid $26.3B in search default revenue share in 2021, the spending the court found
  had foreclosed competition." The 2025-09-02 remedies opinion "declined to order a Chrome
  divestiture ... Mehta wrote that generative AI had changed the course of the case." Brinkema
  found (2025-04-17) Google monopolised the ad server and exchange.
- **Form: SUMMARY / dated legal events.**
- **Telling detail / number:** **$26.3B** paid in one year (2021) simply to be the default —
  the state named the price of foreclosure to the tenth of a billion.
- **Caveat:** remedy is *not* deconcentration on this record — the Chrome divestiture was
  declined; Brinkema's remedy was pending at the freeze. Keep the correction.

### SS18 — Not a better auction, a bigger cheque (2002)
- **Moment:** In May 2002, while a rival's per-click auction was out-earning it, Google won the
  AOL account with a reported $100M revenue guarantee and an ~85% revenue share.
- **Claims:** `mech-aol-001` (B) + `e6-sellers-001` (**A**; Overture led 2002).
- **Sourced detail:** the May 2002 Google-AOL agreement "is reported to have carried an
  approximately $100 million revenue guarantee and an approximately 85% revenue share. The
  contract was never filed with the SEC; the figures originate in secondary accounts." Google's
  traffic-acquisition costs equalled 91% of Google Network ad revenue in 2002, and AOL supplied
  ~63% of that network revenue. Entering the era, Overture led: $667.7M revenue in 2002 vs
  Google's $439.5M total.
- **Form: TELLING DETAIL / SUMMARY.**
- **Telling detail:** Overture (GoTo) *invented* the open per-click auction and out-earned
  Google that year — concentration was bought and distributed (a $100M guarantee), not won on a
  better product.
- **Caveat:** the AOL figures are grade B from **secondary accounts, never SEC-filed** —
  preserve "reported" / "approximately." Do not harden.

### SS19 — One courtroom, twenty-one years (Brinkema)
- **Moment:** The same judge who in 2004 let the keyword-auction model stand — ruling that
  selling a trademark as a keyword did not by itself break trademark law — found in 2025 that
  Google had monopolised the ad server and the exchange.
- **Claims:** `e6-events-004` (B; 2004 keyword/Lanham Act) + `tu:era:7:event:8` (Brinkema,
  2025-04-17).
- **Form: TELLING DETAIL — the load-bearing human thread.** Judge Leonie Brinkema (E.D. Va.)
  at both ends, 21 years apart, both rulings structural.
- **Vivid:** a single bench spans the model's licensing and its condemnation — a human anchor
  the parent asked for, entirely in the record.
