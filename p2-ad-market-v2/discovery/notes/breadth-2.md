## FLAGGED — cross-era spines (the promising ones)

### A. The 15% commission: a 150-year cartel that would not die
The single most cross-era thread in my slice. Six of my themes are vertebrae on it.
- **1875 open contract / fixed commission born** — `tu:era:1:field:CREATORS` (theme "creative
  element", "Fixed commission concept"): N.W. Ayer's 1875 open contract; "the full-service agency,
  the pitch, and the fixed commission all existed before 1917; the trade body that froze them, the
  4As, was founded in June of that year."
- **1893 cartelized** — `e1-events-003` (via `tu:era:1:event:5`, theme "Customary commission
  rate", grade C): the newspaper publishers' association resolved commissions be paid "only to
  recognised advertising agents and never to advertisers — the enforcement rule that turned a
  customary 15 percent into a cartel price."
- **1956 legally dismantled, survives anyway** — `tu:era:3:field:CREATORS` (themes "regulatory
  framework", "specialized service"): a 1 Feb 1956 federal consent decree ended the collective
  "recognition" machinery; "the rate survived anyway... 71% of large national advertisers still
  used it in 1982."
- **1977–92 the money it moved** — `e4-creators-002` (theme "Advertising agency billing growth
  factor", grade A): US agency billings $69.59B in 1992 up from $15.45B in 1977, 4.5x nominal.
- **eras 5–7 the death** — `tu:era:7:field:PRICING` (theme "rGSP replacement of format pricing"):
  "Agency compensation completed its own long slide from commission to fee to principal media...
  after the ANA/K2 rebate report of 2016."
Why promising: a documented rise → cartelization → antitrust attack → survival → eventual collapse
arc spanning 1875–2016. A single institution (the 15% cut) tracked start to finish.

### B. Who controls the count: buyer → vendor → seller
A measurement-independence spine. The party that owns the number keeps moving.
- **1946 buyers lose control** — `tu:era:2:event:9` (theme "CAB's numbers"): after the Zeisel
  study exposed why Hooper ran 20% above the buyer-run Cooperative Analysis of Broadcasting, the
  networks (its main funders) threatened withdrawal; Hooper took over CAB's subscribers 31 Jul
  1946. "Sixteen years of buyer-controlled measurement ended... Measurement passed permanently
  from a committee of buyers to private vendors selling to both sides." Nielsen reached 97% of US
  homes by Apr 1949.
- **~2001 self-reported reach, unaudited** — `e5-targeting-002` (themes "Engage 4,700-site
  network", "ValueClick network", grade C): DoubleClick and Engage both self-claimed >half the US
  internet audience; only ValueClick's ">30%" was third-party measured (Jupiter). Method note:
  "self-reported network reach in this period was routinely inflated by duplicate-audience
  counting."
- **era 6 the seller becomes the auditor** — `tu:era:6:field:MEASUREMENT` (theme "IAB Click
  Measurement Guidelines"): "Every price in the search market was set from a count that the seller
  alone produced." No third party audited in period (IAB working group not until 2005, guidelines
  2009). Click-fraud crisis: Lane's Gifts settled Mar 2006 for up to $90M in credits; the court
  appointed NYU's Tuzhilin — "an auditor commissioned by the defendant and paid for by a
  settlement."
- **era 7 endpoint** — `e7-events-008` / `e7-measurement-004` (themes "AI summary", "content
  type", grade B): the count that matters (the click) is vanishing — 8% result-click rate on
  AI-summary visits vs 15% without; source cited inside the summary clicked on just 1%.
Why promising: a clean POSIWID system-character thread — the ad market reliably relocates the
measuring instrument toward whoever it most benefits. Two regimes (broadcast, search) plus a
current rupture.

### C. The state breaks the top of the stack — three times, 80 years apart
- **1943** — `tu:era:2:field:SELLERS` (theme "Mutual network net time sales share"): FCC
  chain-broadcasting rules forced RCA to sell NBC Blue; Edward J. Noble bought it Oct 1943 for $8M
  and renamed it ABC. Same field quantifies the concentration broken: NBC+CBS took 44% of 1938 net
  time sales, plus 7% from owned stations; they "kept 73 percent of network net time sales and
  paid just 27 percent to the 253 affiliates."
- **2004** — `tu:era:6:event:3` / `e6-events-004` (theme "Priced entry point", grade B): GEICO v.
  Google — Judge Brinkema ruled keyword sale of trademarks alone did not infringe. "The change
  converts brand names into a priced entry point and forces brand owners to bid on themselves."
- **2024–25** — `tu:era:7:field:SELLERS` (theme "judge"): Judge Mehta held 2024-08-05 that Google
  monopolised general search and search text advertising; Judge Brinkema held 2025-04-17 that it
  monopolised the open-web ad server and exchange and unlawfully tied them. Google/Meta/Amazon ~61%
  of US digital ad spend in 2025.
- Origin layer of the same thread — `tu:era:1:event:7` (theme "Cheap second-class mail rate") and
  `tu:era:2:event:4` (theme "enforcement sanction"). Three beats. The Printers' Ink model statute
  of 1911 reached 22 states by Nov 1921 (`e1-events-004`, grade B). The Newspaper Publicity Act of
  1912 conditioned the cheap second-class mail rate on sworn circulation. Wheeler-Lea in 1938
  "completed the private-plus-federal governance layer that US advertising carried for the rest of
  the century."
Why promising: recurring rupture beat (external, dated, verifiable state change) at three era
boundaries — the antitrust brackets around the whole 505-claim corpus.

### D. Content as advertising's loss-leader — the model's birth and its possible death
A two-point bookend spine that reaches across the entire timeline.
- **Birth 1893** — `e1-events-003` / `tu:era:1:event:5` (theme "Customary commission rate", grade
  C): Munsey cut Munsey's Magazine to 10 cents. The cut let a publisher "sell a magazine below its
  cost of production and let advertisers pay the difference." The ten-cent magazine grew the
  magazine-buying public from ~250,000 to ~750,000 by 1899. This is an interested-party estimate;
  the hedge is preserved.
- **Possible death 2024–25** — `e7-events-008` / `tu:era:7:event:7` (themes "AI summary", "content
  type", grade B): AI Overviews launch 2024-05-14; clicks that fund content collapse (8% vs 15%).
Why promising: the ad-subsidized-content bargain that Munsey invented is exactly what the AI answer
box threatens. Clean open-and-close on the same idea, 130 years apart.

### E. The pricing mechanism: print-analogy → auction → the auction's triple death
- **Print-analogy pricing (era 5)** — `e5-events-001` / `tu:era:5:event:0` (theme "ad type", grade
  B): HotWired's first paid banners, 27 Oct 1994, ~12 brands at ~$30k/12 weeks, "a price set by
  analogy to a $10,000 page in the print magazine." Debunks the famous 44% CTR as "self-reported
  and unaudited; participants... recall 15%." And `e5-pricing-008` (theme "Netscape Net Search
  page", grade C): Excite's ~$5M/slot/yr Premier Provider anchor tenancy, "priced like a magazine
  cover position, not per impression."
- **TV's own mid-century mechanism shift** — `tu:era:3:field:PRICING` (theme "pricing component"):
  sponsorship → network-owned fungible inventory; the unit shrank (60s→30s standard by 1970–71);
  the upfront invented 1962; price became CPM ($30k→$65k per prime-time commercial minute,
  1960s→1970).
- **The auction's triple death (era 7)** — `tu:era:7:field:PRICING` (theme "rGSP replacement of
  format pricing"): "the auction that defined era 6 died three times over." The three deaths were
  header bidding (2014–15), unified first-price (2019-09-05, `tu:era:7:event:4`), and "intentional
  pricing" knobs (squashing, format pricing, rGSP). rGSP raised top-slot CPCs ~5.9% desktop / 4.9%
  mobile. Dischler testified Google moved prices ~5% on average, up to 10%, to hit quarterly
  targets "without telling advertisers."
Why promising: vivid + quantified + a genuine surprise (the secret price knobs). A mechanism-change
thread the whole corpus can hang on.

### F. The addressable base: title → household → person → person-opts-out
- **era 1: buy a title, not a person** — `tu:era:1:field:TARGETING` (theme "housing category"):
  precision meant choosing a publication and a place; classified as "intent-matching by category,
  done by hand"; Sears filled 5x as many orders in 1913 as 1912 after Parcel Post.
- **era 2: the household radio S-curve** — `e2-targeting-005` (theme "radio receiver device",
  grade B): 83% of US households owned a radio by 1940 (up from 40.3% in 1930, ~95% by 1950) — "the
  addressable base that made national broadcast targeting possible."
- **era 7: the person opts out** — `e7-targeting-002` (theme "Gaming apps", grade B): App Tracking
  Transparency opt-in ~16% at May 2021 launch, ~25% by mid-2022, gaming apps ~30%.
Why promising: the unit of targeting precision tracked across 150 years, each step quantified.
