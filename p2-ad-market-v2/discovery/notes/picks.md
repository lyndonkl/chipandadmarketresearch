## THE 6 (numbered briefs — thread · L3/L2 + node anchor · sharpest question)

**1. Who counts the audience.** The market's founding discipline is that a *third party*
counts the audience; the recurring crisis is the seller counting its own results — buyer-
committee → third-party vendor → seller-platform.
*Anchor:* L3 *Advertising Measurement & Analytics* / *Advertising Measurement Governance* →
`tu:era:2:event:9` (buyers lose the count, 1946) · `tu:era:6:field:MEASUREMENT` ("the seller
became the auditor") · `e6-measurement-002` (C) · `e7-measurement-002` (B).
*Sharpest question:* **Across the three regimes where the counter changed hands, what did each
transfer cost the side that lost the number — and is the era-7 break different in kind, or only
in scale?**

**2. The 15% that would not die.** One 1875 contract reform → a century-long cartel price → a
death that is mis-dated in the received story.
*Anchor:* L3 *Advertising Structural Shift* / *In-House Media Planning* →
`tu:era:1:field:CREATORS` (Ayer 1875) · `e1-events-003` (1893 cartel, C) ·
`tu:era:4:field:PRICING` (Economic Census: rate held to ~14% into 1992) · `e6-creators-002`
(B: 61%→~10%, "not the arrival of search advertising").
*Sharpest question:* **If the census shows the commission rate held near 14% into 1992, what
actually collapsed the agency's cut — and does the 2016 return of undisclosed rebates put the
trade back where Ayer found it in 1875?**

**3. The state breaks the top of the stack.** Seller concentration rhymes across ~90 years, and
each time an external, dated rupture — not competition — resets it.
*Anchor:* L3 *Broadcast Media* / *Advertising Governance & Pricing Framework* →
`tu:era:2:field:SELLERS` (FCC forces RCA to sell NBC Blue, 1943, $8M → ABC) · `e6-events-004`
(B: GEICO/Brinkema, 2004) · `tu:era:7:field:SELLERS` (Mehta 2024 + Brinkema 2025; ~61%).
*Sharpest question:* **At the 1938 and 2025 concentration peaks, how close are the two-/three-
firm shares and the take-rates — and in each rupture, was it the market or the state that moved
the structure?**

**4. The loss-leader that built free media — and the box that may end it.** Ad-funded, below-
cost content was invented on the sell side in 1833/1893; the AI answer may be undoing it at the
same mechanism.
*Anchor:* L3 *US Media Owner Ad Revenue Metrics* / *Regulated Product Categories* →
`tu:era:1:field:SELLERS` (penny *Sun* 1833; Munsey's 10¢ 1893) · `e1-events-003` (C) ·
`e7-events-008` (B: result click 8% with AI summary vs 15% without).
*Sharpest question:* **Munsey sold a magazine below cost and let advertisers pay the
difference. If AI Overviews cut the funding click from 15% to 8%, is the 130-year subsidy
being broken on the very mechanism that created it — and what do the sourced numbers on each
end license us to claim?**

**5. The neutral auction was a governed price.** The "open" second-price auction "died three
times over," and a court found the seller was turning the knobs.
*Anchor:* L3 *Ad Impression Measurement* / *Ad Market Pricing & Measurement* →
`tu:era:7:field:PRICING` (three deaths) · `tu:era:7:event:4` (unified first-price, 2019) ·
`mech-knobs-001` (grade **A**, Mehta FOF: squashing/"Butternut Squash", format pricing, rGSP).
*Sharpest question:* **What is the strongest court-graded evidence that the clearing price was
set by the seller rather than the runner-up bid — and how large was the price move (Dischler's
~5%, up to 10%) advertisers could not opt out of?**

**6. Capture, not expansion — the ad-intensity paradox.** Every era's "explosion" is mostly
reallocation inside a share of the economy that peaked a century ago.
*Anchor:* L3 *US Advertising Spend Levels* / *US Media Owner Ad Revenue Metrics* →
`tu:era:2:field:SCALE` (ad/GDP 3.0% peak, 1922) · `tu:era:3:field:SCALE` ("television took
share from other media rather than enlarging the pot") · `tu:era:7:field:SCALE` (~1.3% of GDP,
2025).
*Sharpest question:* **Drilling each SCALE field to its `e#-` backing, what were ad/GDP at the
1922 peak, the 2007 level, and 2025 — and does the record support "reallocation, not growth"
for both the TV rise and the platform rise?**

## Runner-ups (4–6, worth a later look)

- **R1. Intent money migrates across five media.** The same response/"intent" pool moves
  classified → direct mail → Yellow Pages → search. `tu:era:4:field:MEDIUM` (direct mail
  $4.79B→$27.27B; Yellow Pages $9.5B passed radio+magazines) · `tu:era:6:field:BUYERS`
  ("long tail of plumbers, locksmiths") · `e3-scale-010` (B) · `e5-scale-016` (C, the
  filing-rule flip). Cross-era, quantified; overlaps Pick 4 at the demand end.
- **R2. A price rule invented the soap opera.** Daytime hour = half price + women-at-home →
  "P&G built the soap opera to occupy cheap hours in front of the people who bought soap."
  `tu:era:2:field:TARGETING`. Vivid, clean causal — but a single beat; best as a *scene inside
  Pick 1*, not a standalone spine.
- **R3. The wartime tax subsidy.** Under the excess-profits tax a deductible ad dollar cost
  ~20¢ after tax (`e2-events-005`, C, CI80 [0.10,0.28]) — the 1940s boom as fiscal artifact.
  Surprising, quantified, but one grade-C beat; a sidebar.
- **R4. The addressable base: title → household → person → person-opts-out.**
  `tu:era:1:field:TARGETING` · `e2-targeting-005` (B, radio 40%→83%→95%) · `e7-targeting-002`
  (B, ATT opt-in ~16→25%). Precision unit tracked 150 years; overlaps Picks 1 and 5.
- **R5. "How we know what we know" — dataset integrity as its own register.** The charts that
  would tell this history can lie. `e5-medium-006` (B, the "177% jump that did not happen" from
  splicing Out-of-Home onto Billboards) · `e5-scale-016` (C, the ranking that flips on where
  Yellow Pages is filed) · `tu:era:1:boundary` (the Wanamaker line is "an attributed legend";
  two spend lineages ~15% apart). A meta framing the piece can wear openly.
- **R6. Vivid single actors for scene-work** (not spines): marchFIRST — $437M loss on $369M
  sales, ~2,100 cut, Ch.11→Ch.7 in 2001 (`e5-creators-004`, B); Interpublic modelled on GM,
  1961 (`tu:era:3:event:3`); the 36% Apple/Safari reveal disclosed in open court
  (`mech-default-003`, B); the cigarette-TV ban of 2 Jan 1971, ~$225M/yr buyer class removed
  overnight (`tu:era:3:field:BUYERS`).
