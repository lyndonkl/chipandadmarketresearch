## The built tableaux (middleman thread — the only located, artifact-anchored scenes in either dig)

### SB1 — The first middleman was the phone company, renting the wires by the hour

**One line:** By the end of 1925 the original network middleman was not an agency or a broadcaster. It was
AT&T, selling time on its thirteen-station toll network at $2,600 an hour. It sold the whole business to RCA
before advertising money had even settled.

**Claim id:** `e2-pricing-002` (**A, adjusted**; FCC, *Report on Chain Broadcasting*, May 1941, ch. I).

**The telling detail (one particular, verbatim):** *"By the end of 1925 AT&T was selling time on its basic
thirteen-station toll network at $2,600 per hour, generating gross revenues at a rate of about $750,000 a
year from time sales before RCA bought the business."* The middleman's cut, before there is a stack to speak
of, is a phone bill: the wires that carry the signal are rented by the hour. And that first plate changes
hands (AT&T → RCA, 1926) before the advertiser's dollar arrives.

**SCAM slots (system protagonist):**
- **S —** the AT&T basic toll network, end of 1925; a thirteen-station chain sold by the hour.
- **C —** system state: $2,600 per hour, ~$750,000/yr from time sales — a distribution layer priced as a
  utility, not a medium.
- **A —** three ordered, sourced events: (1) AT&T assembles the thirteen-station toll network; (2) it sells
  time at $2,600/hr, ~$750k/yr; (3) RCA buys the business.
- **M —** the intermediary chain begins one layer below the agency: the *original* cut is paid to whoever
  owns the wire — the layer Session 1's 15% story never counted.

**Why it is vivid:** a grade-A price on a datable object (rented airtime, by the hour), and a handover that
happens *before* the ad market forms. Gate 4 passes hard — it cannot be written without the FCC number.

**Non-negotiable caveats for drafting:**
- **Protected quotation (readability exception):** the AT&T telling-detail runs to 47 words as a *verbatim*
  source statement (`e2-pricing-002`). It fails the 40-word gate and must stay whole — splitting a source
  quote breaks provenance. This is the one over-gate sentence in the note, and it is intentional.
- **Representativeness:** this is the *origin* of the chain, atypical-and-vivid, not a steady-state price. Flag
  it as the first layer, not the market rate.
- **Anachronism flag:** "middleman / the stack / take rate" are modern frames over 1925. The period called
  this a *toll network* and *facilities charges*. Mark ours as ours.
- No added sensory detail. The record holds a rate and a sale; that is all.

### SB2 — The one plate we can number, because the state went and audited it

**One line:** In 1938 NBC and CBS kept 73 percent of their network time sales. They paid the other 27 percent
to the 253 stations that put the signal in the air. The only reason those figures exist to the dollar is that
the FCC audited them.

**Claim id:** `e2-sellers-002` (**A, confirmed**; FCC, *Report on Chain Broadcasting*, May 1941, ch. VI).

**Overlap flag (mandatory).** This exhibit is already in the dossier §3 ("Counted to the dollar") and in
Thread 3, where it reads as *concentration* (two firms, 51% of the industry). **Do not double-build the same
scene.** Here it carries a **different frame**: the middleman's *retention plate*. Casting it in the middleman
thread makes one point. This is the one plate of the whole stack that carries an A grade **only because a
regulator forced it into the light.** The hidden plates (production ~a third, the rep's 5–20%) stay grade C
precisely because no one audited them.

**The telling detail (one particular, verbatim):** *"NBC and CBS retained 73 percent ($32,046,218) of their
network net time sales in 1938 and paid 27 percent ($12,267,560) to the 253 affiliated stations that carried
the programs."* The precision *is* the point: the middleman's cut is knowable to the dollar exactly where the
state stood over it, and nowhere else.

**SCAM slots (system protagonist):**
- **S —** the FCC's 1941 chain-broadcasting audit, reporting 1938; the network time-sales settlement.
- **C —** system state: 73% retained ($32,046,218) vs 27% paid out ($12,267,560) across 253 affiliates.
- **A —** three sourced facts: (1) networks book net time sales; (2) retain 73%; (3) pay 27% to the 253
  affiliates that carried the signal.
- **M —** the stack's largest measurable plate is the network's retention — and it is measurable only under
  audit. Where the state does not stand, the take goes dark (SB13, SB10).

**Why it is vivid:** the audited-to-the-dollar precision against the un-numbered plates around it. It earns
its place in *this* thread as the contrast that makes the hidden plates legible.

**Caveats:** ship the reframe explicitly so an architect does not run it twice. The `about_year` is 1938; the
audit that surfaces it is 1941 — keep both dates straight.

## The systemic peak (denominator thread — an exhibit, not a room)

### SB3 — The instrument's instruction to itself: "name its rail in the axis label, not the footnote"

**One line:** The denominator war has one legible climax, and it is not a scene. It is the moment the corpus
turns and instructs itself: no chart of the ad market may hide which rail it stands on. The two credible rails
for 2022 differ by $148 billion.

**Claim id:** `e7-scale-006` (**C, adjusted**; method note). The wedge sits in the same claim's sources. IRS
Statistics of Income corporate advertising deductions read **$473,153m for 2022**, against MAGNA's
**$325,000m for the same year**. The note calls it *"a $148B rail wedge, 45.6 percent of the MAGNA base"* and
*"overwhelmingly direct-response and retail money."*

**The telling detail (verbatim, from the method note):** *"Any chart of this split must name its rail in the
axis label, not the footnote."* Two instruments measuring the same year, one $148B apart from the other, and
the difference is almost entirely the response-priced money — direct mail chief among it — that the
media-owner rail cannot see.

**Numbers-in-narrative (contested-figure grammar — both values, both rails, one sentence):** for 2022,
corporate advertising deductions came to about **$473B** on the IRS rail and about **$325B** on the MAGNA
media-owner rail — a **$148B** gap, roughly **46 percent** of the MAGNA base. **Never average the two.** Name
the rail.

**Why it is an exhibit, not a tableau (the honest classification):**
- The "quote" is the **corpus's own method note** — the instrument/verifier's voice, not a period document.
  There is no room, no named human, no dated action. It fails the SCAM floor and is correctly demoted to a
  LEGIBILITY exhibit (the same disposition `scenes-B.md` gave rGSP at B9).
- **Grade C, do not harden.** The $145.7B national-brand construction it rides on is explicitly *"a
  construction and must stay one"* (the claim's own method: "SOURCED BY: NO SOURCE"). Only the two rail
  totals and the $148B wedge are firm.
- Load-bearing actor = **the instrument** (per `systemic-protagonist`): Coen/McCann-via-Galbi at one end, the
  MAGNA/IAB media-owner rail at the other. The human proxy is the corpus verifier who writes the guard.

## Summary / number / telling-detail beats (correctly NOT scenes)

### SB4 — The arc's hinge: biggest single line in 2007, gone from the base by 2025
**Ids:** `e6-medium-001` (B; 2007) · `e7-scale-001` (B; 2025).
**Landing (dual-profile, direction before magnitude):** direct mail did not shrink to nothing between 2007
and 2025 — it **stopped being counted**. In 2007 it was the **largest single line in Coen's taxonomy,
$60,225m, 21.5%** of total US ad spend, ahead of broadcast television every year. By 2025 the headline basis
(MAGNA media-owner revenue, about **$405B**) **excludes it entirely**, because a mailer sells no inventory
and is not a media owner. **Non-removable caveat (verbatim from `e6-medium-001`):** the "largest single line"
result *"depends on Coen booking broadcast and cable separately"*; television taken as one medium
($70,840m in 2007) stayed larger. Ship the caveat inside the claim. A data tableau; no scene.

### SB5 — 1949: the #2 medium subtracted before the "real" total is struck
**Id:** `e2-scale-010` (C, confirmed).
**Landing:** in 1949 direct mail was the second-largest medium in the country (14.5%, `e2-medium-004` B). The
compiler struck the national-brand total by first taking it out. The claim reads: *"National brand
advertising, excluding direct mail, was about $2,234 million in 1949 — roughly 43 percent of all US
advertising spend."* The channel-sized hole is drawn *into* the accounting, not overlooked. Mechanism beat,
not a scene.

### SB6 — Same quantity, two bases, unresolved (the war made explicit)
**Ids:** `e2-medium-003` (B; radio 1932) · `e1-scale-004` (B; total 1900).
**Landing (contested-figure grammar — both values, never averaged):** radio was **3.9 percent** of all US
advertising in 1932 on the Coen series. It was *"nearly 11 percent"* the same year on a narrower
national-media base — *"and the conflict is not resolved."* Reach back to the origin and the same fracture is
there. The 1900 US total is **$450M** (Coen's 1999 revision) or **$542M** (the Printers' Ink lineage) — two
credible figures that *"disagree by 20 percent and are both carried."* Every "share" a casual reader treats as
a fact, the corpus flags as a basis. Telling detail; no scene.

### SB7 — The second channel-sized hole: trade promotion, 44.9%, never counted
**Id:** `e4-buyers-004` (B).
**Landing:** by 1992, **44.9 percent** of packaged-goods marketing-communications budgets went to trade
promotion — *"money that never appears in the measured-media advertising total,"* having overtaken media
advertising during the 1980s. Direct mail is the recurring casualty of the denominator, but it is not the
only one; nearly half of CPG persuasion spend sat outside the base by definition. Number, not scene.

### SB8 — 1956: direct mail already larger than broadcast
**Id:** `e3-buyers-001` (B).
**Landing:** direct mail was **24 percent of all US national advertising in 1956 — a larger share than
broadcast** — *"which is why direct-response buyers cannot be treated as a later-era phenomenon."* The
"long-tail plumber" the search era claims to have invented was a quarter of national spend in the Eisenhower
years. Number, not scene.

### SB9 — The 15% was two businesses (structure beat)
**Id:** `e2-creators-002` (B, adjusted).
**Landing:** the disclosed number never changed, but what it bought did. *"The standard agency commission
held at 15 percent of gross media billing through the whole era, and on radio it covered program production
as well as media buying."* The same 15% silently bought a second business — the agency became a program
factory — so program-and-talent rode inside a commission the rate card only ever showed as media.
**SECONDARY / color, do not harden:** the vivid synthesis phrase *"delivered a finished program to the line"*
is from `tu:era:2:field:CREATORS`, not the graded L0 claim; re-pull before any use. Structure beat, no scene.

### SB10 — The stack made explicit (the rep, and the discount even the small plate hides)
**Ids:** `e3-pricing-003` (C, adjusted; interval 5–20) · `e3-pricing-002` (B, confirmed).
**Landing:** a *new* hand stacked on the old one without replacing it — station representative firms took
*"a negotiated commission in a normal range of about 5–20%, calculated on the amount left after the agency's
15% had been deducted."* And even the small plate hides one: on top of the 15%, agencies took *"a 2% cash
discount on the net for paying media within about ten days — a second, smaller intermediary take that is
usually omitted from take-rate comparisons."* **Do not harden:** the rep's central "10" is a range midpoint,
*"a construction, not a source figure"* (TV averages ran below radio's). The stacking is the finding; the
plates sit on incompatible bases and cannot be summed. Number, not scene.

### SB11 — The residual that blocks the malign-optimiser read: Mutual keeps 3.5%
**Id:** `e2-pricing-006` (A, confirmed).
**Landing:** a middleman that declined the big cut. Mutual, *"which published no network rate card and billed
at its stations' own card rates, retained a 3.5 percent commission on network proceeds from member stations
after agency commissions."* Against NBC/CBS's 73% retention, 3.5% is the residual a "maximise the hidden cut"
function cannot predict — **mandatory, must ship.** Number, not scene.

### SB12 — The forward rhyme (profile, never spliced into the broadcast series)
**Id:** `e7-pricing-006` (B, confirmed).
**Landing:** the three-plate broadcast stack returns as a three-plate programmatic stack, top plate disclosed,
depth alleged. In June 2020 Google disclosed that publishers keep about **69 cents** of an advertiser dollar
that starts and ends inside its stack — *"Google Ads ~14%, DV360 ~13%, Google Ad Manager ~18%."* The DOJ
**alleged** Google keeps *"at least 35 cents of every open-web display dollar and that AdX had held a 20% take
rate since 2009."* **Keep "alleged"; keep it a profile, not a splice.** Number, not scene.

### SB13 — The biggest cost was off the card (telling detail; do not harden)
**Id:** `e2-pricing-008` (C, confirmed; interval **20–55**).
**Landing:** *"Program and talent production was roughly a third of a sponsor's total network radio outlay —
the cost the rate card never showed."* The single most vivid line in the middleman thread, and the most
fragile: the claim's own method warns part of that residual *"is discounting, not production"* (Silk & Berndt,
the McCann-Erickson list-price basis). **The width is the honesty — keep the interval 20–55; never render it
as a clean "a third" without the hedge.** Telling detail, not a scene.
