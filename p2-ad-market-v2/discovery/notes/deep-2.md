# Deep dig — Thread #2: "The 15% that would not die"

**Architect:** narrative-architect (Bash), deep dig.
**Method:** top-down walk L3 → L2 → L1 → L0. Every finding is grounded to a graph node plus a claim id. Hedge, grade, and interval are preserved. This is discovery, not prose.
**Skills applied as lenses:** narrative-evidence-ledger (frame lock, A–F class, L1–L5 causal grades, DEAD column), narrative-opposition-web (the corrective edges), systemic-protagonist (POSIWID sheet), extract-thread-spine (the ABT).

## The through-line (one paragraph)

For ~185 years the US ad agency has been paid out of one gap: the space between what the advertiser pays and what the media costs. It is paid most when that gap is hidden. Before N. W. Ayer's 1875 "open contract" the agent worked for the publisher and took a concealed cut of 10–50% (Palmer's was ~25%). Ayer flipped the loyalty to the buyer and disclosed the rate. The concealed cut became a fixed, visible 15% of gross. That number then refused to die. A publishers' cartel enforced it from 1893. A 1956 federal consent decree struck the enforcement down. The commission still outlived the decree by decades: 71% of large national advertisers were on it in 1982. The Economic Census shows the rate held near 14% to 1992, and only slipped to ~11% by 1997. What finally collapsed the agency's cut was not the rate and not search advertising. It was the mix. The 1991–94 unbundling of media buying from creative moved advertisers onto labour fees. Then, in era 7, the agency dropped the disclosed cut for principal media — buying inventory in bulk and reselling it at an undisclosed markup. The ANA/K2 report of 2016 documented pervasive hidden rebates. The visible surface was always "15%." The real variable was always who knows the price. It has now gone dark again.

**Sharpest-question answers, grounded:**
- *What actually collapsed the agency's cut, if the rate held near 14% into 1992?* The mix, not the rate. Advertisers on a commission basis fell 71% (1982) → 61% (1994) → ~10% (2003). Over the same span, media buying was unbundled from creative and repriced as labour fees (8% → 31%). The rate itself only fell from ~14% to ~11% by 1997. `e6-creators-002` explicitly rules out search advertising as the cause.
- *Does the 2016 return of undisclosed rebates put the trade back where Ayer found it in 1875?* Structurally yes, with a hedge. Ayer in 1875 found agents whose income depended on hiding the price. His reform disclosed it. Era-7 principal media restores income-from-hidden-markup (undisclosed rebates, ANA/K2 2016). This is a structural rhyme the corpus supports. It is not a causal identity the graph proves. Grade it a hypothesis, not a fact.

## Frame lock (narrative-evidence-ledger, Step 1)

- **UNIT:** one observation of agency compensation. Either the *rate* (% of gross media billings the agent keeps) or the *mix* (% of advertisers who pay on a commission basis). These are two different measures. The thread turns on not conflating them. *Rejected alt:* "agency revenue in $" — it hides that the rate held while the mix collapsed.
- **DENOMINATOR:** rate = per dollar of gross media billing; mix = per large national advertiser surveyed. *Rejected alt:* per agency — the ANA series is advertiser-side.
- **WINDOW:** 1841 (Palmer) → 2026 (era-7 principal media), all seven eras. *Rejected alt:* start at 1875 (Ayer) — it loses the pre-disclosure opacity regime that mirrors era 7.
- **BOUNDARY:** the commissioned/fee agency channel. It excludes classified, mail-order coupon, and self-serve — buyers "who never touched the 15 percent commission" (`tu:era:1:boundary`; edges `e1-buyers-007` classified EXCLUDES agency commission). The sources drew that line, not me.

**Instrument check (a discontinuity that is measurement, not world):** the "15%" is a rate-card nominal. Commissions actually received were already ~14% in 1977, and "already an 11% commission in practice before the web mattered" (`e5-creators-003`, grade A). The received "death of the 15%" partly reflects the gap between the posted 15% and the negotiated rate actually kept. That is a measurement gap, not only a market event.

## Starting hypothesis vs. what the graph changed (diff)

- **Before:** "One 1875 reform → a century-long cartel price → a death mis-dated to the 1980s."
- **After:** the death is mis-dated three ways at once. The graph also adds a beat the pick did not have: the **1956 DOJ consent decree** (`e3-creators-001`, grade A). It killed the *legal enforcement* of the 15% while the *practice* sailed on for another 30+ years. So "would not die" is literal. It outlived its own antitrust death. The through-line also sharpened from "a price" to "**who knows the price**" — the opacity/disclosure axis. That makes 1875 and 2016 mirror images rather than a simple rise-and-fall.

## Systemic protagonist — POSIWID sheet (systemic-protagonist)

**Subject:** the advertising-agency **compensation regime** (a system, held constant).

**Character sentence:** *This system reliably converts the concealment of the media price into intermediary income. When concealment is forced open, it converts a coordinated fixed rate into the same income by cartel — until competition and unbundling erode it.*

**Two regimes (required):**
- **Opacity regime** — pre-1875 space brokerage (Palmer paid by *publishers*, ~25%, range 10–50% hidden; `e1-creators-001`, `tu:era:1:field:CREATORS`). Also post-2016 principal media (buying inventory in bulk, reselling at an *undisclosed markup*; ANA/K2 2016 rebate report; `tu:era:7:field:CREATORS`, `tu:era:7:field:PRICING`).
- **Disclosure regime** — 1875–c.1997 open contract / fixed commission (nominal 15%, actual ~14% → ~11%; `e1-pricing-002`, `e4-pricing-001`, `e5-creators-003`).

**Residual — outcomes the opacity function does NOT explain (mandatory):**
1. The century-long stability of the disclosed rate at ~14% is not an opacity outcome. It is a **coordination/enforcement** outcome (1893 publisher recognition rule; 1918 4As standardization). Different mechanism.
2. What finally collapsed the cut was **unbundling of media buying from creative** (mix), not concealment and not search (`e6-creators-002`). The opacity story does not predict this. A services-repricing story does.

**Gap (the engine):** the *stated* purpose of the open contract (1875) is a disclosed agent loyal to the buyer. The *revealed* function is an intermediary paid from a price gap that periodically goes dark. Era 7 is the gap re-opening.

## ABT spine (extract-thread-spine)

The ABT is one sentence by form (Olson). Rendered as its three moves, in short sentences:

- **AND:** the US ad agency was born as a broker paid a hidden cut by the seller. Ayer's 1875 open contract forced the price into the open and fixed the agent's take at a visible 15% of gross. A publishers' cartel enforced that rate from 1893, and the 4As froze it in 1918.
- **BUT:** the number would not die on cue. A 1956 federal consent decree struck down the enforcement, yet 71% of big advertisers were still paying it in 1982. The census rate held near 14% to 1992. What finally cut the agency's take was the 1991–94 unbundling of media buying from creative — not search, and not the rate.
- **THEREFORE:** the "death of the 15%" is a mis-dated legend. Era 7's shift to undisclosed-markup principal media (ANA/K2, 2016) does not end the story. It returns it to the concealed-price world Ayer reformed away in 1875.

## Opposition web — the corrective edges (narrative-opposition-web)

The graph wired the correctives as real L0→L0 edges. These *are* the spine:

1. **`e6-creators-002` — "decline in billings-based agency compensation" NOT_CAUSED_BY "arrival of search advertising."** The most load-bearing corrective in the thread. Cause = 1991–94 unbundling; grade B.
2. **`e3-creators-002` — "Era 3" EXCLUDES "commission death."** Era 3 is the commission's *peak* (71% in 1982), not its death; grade B.
3. **`tu:era:4:field:PRICING` — "advertiser commission mix" EXCLUDES "commission rate."** The formal split at the heart of the answer: mix eroded, rate held.
4. **`tu:era:1:event:5` — "commission payment rule" EXCLUDES "advertisers."** The 1893 recognition rule "turned a customary 15 percent into a cartel price" by paying commissions only to recognised agents, never to advertisers.
5. Corroborating: `tu:era:3:field:PRICING` "different pricing regime" EXCLUDES "absence of commission" (classified/direct never carried the commission).

**Constraint, not villain (system framing):** the opponent is not a party. It is the **opacity/disclosure oscillation** and its enforcement scaffolding (publisher recognition → 4As standardization → DOJ decree → competition/unbundling). The DOJ appears as the state rupture that removes enforcement without removing the practice. It is evidenced and dated (`e3-creators-001`, grade A). No intent is attributed to "the market."

## Evidence ledger — best findings (finding → path → claim → statement, grade preserved)

**F1 — Origin was hidden and seller-side.** Palmer paid by publishers, ~25% of space price, sources split 1840/41/42.
Path: *Advertising Structural Shift* → agency compensation shift → `e1-creators-001` (B). Also `tu:era:1:field:CREATORS`: 1850s range "about 10 to 50 percent."

**F2 — 1875 open contract = the disclosure reform.** Ayer offered the buyer a disclosed rate and a fixed percentage. "Once the agent's income no longer depended on hiding the price, the agency had to sell something else."
Path: `tu:era:1:field:CREATORS` (era text). Stabilized at 15% of gross by early 1890s: `e1-pricing-002` (B).

**F3 — 1893 turned a custom into a cartel price.** Publishers resolved commissions go only to recognised agents, never advertisers.
Path: `tu:era:1:event:5`; edge "commission payment rule" EXCLUDES "advertisers." Formalized as 15% of gross by the 4As in 1918 (`tu:era:1:boundary`).

**F4 — The legal cartel died in 1956; the commission did not.** US v. AAAA consent decree (filed 1955, judgment 1956) ended collective enforcement of the 15% and the recognition system.
Path: `e3-creators-001` (**A**). Yet 71% of the largest national advertisers were still on commission in 1982 — "the commission's peak, not its death."
Path: `e3-creators-002` (B); edge "Era 3" EXCLUDES "commission death."

**F5 — The rate held near 14% into 1992 (the census vs. the legend).** 14.13% (1977), 13.92% (1982), 14.16% (1987), 13.18% (1992), then 10.98% (1997). "The 15% commission survived this era largely intact; its collapse comes after it."
Path: `e4-pricing-001` (**A**). Reinforced: nominal 15% "was already an 11% commission in practice before the web mattered" (`e5-creators-003`, **A**).

**F6 — What collapsed was the mix, not the rate.** Advertisers on commission 71% (1982) → 61% (1994) → ~10% (2003); labour fees 8% → 31%.
Path: `e5-creators-001` (B), `e6-creators-002` (B), `tu:era:4:field:PRICING`; edge "advertiser commission mix" EXCLUDES "commission rate."

**F7 — The corrective: not search advertising.** The collapse "followed the 1991-94 unbundling of media buying from creative, not the arrival of search advertising."
Path: `e6-creators-002` (B); edge NOT_CAUSED_BY "arrival of search advertising." *The thread's keystone.*

**F8 — Era 7 = the price goes dark again.** Agencies moved to principal media — buying inventory in bulk and reselling at an *undisclosed markup*. The ANA/K2 report of 2016 documented pervasive undisclosed rebates. "The 15% commission the era-1 agencies invented finished dying here."
Path: `tu:era:7:field:CREATORS`, `tu:era:7:field:PRICING`. Alongside, 82% of ANA members ran an in-house agency by 2023 (`e7-creators-002`, B) — the buying job leaving the agency entirely.

**F9 — The structural rhyme (hypothesis, hedged).** Pre-1875 opacity (seller-side hidden cut) and post-2016 opacity (principal-media hidden markup) bracket a ~120-year disclosure interval. The corpus supports the *parallel*. It does not assert 2016 = 1875 as a causal identity. Mark it a **hypothesis / L4-adjacency at the causal level**, not a proven mechanism.
