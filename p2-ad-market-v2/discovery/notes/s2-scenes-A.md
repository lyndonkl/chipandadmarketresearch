## 0. The form ruling that governs every seed

This corpus is a **documentary record** — SEC filings, census tables, ratings books, court opinions,
trade-press yardsticks, a historian's spreadsheet. **Nobody wrote down the room, the light, the gesture.**
So the SCAM gate's Setting slot (a located place with recorded physical specifics) is **empty for almost
every candidate**, and the honest output is not a literary scene. It is one of three lower forms:

- **Documentary tableau** — scene-grammar (named actor, dated ordered actions, a distinguishing behaviour)
  built *only* from an artifact a source records. Two candidates clear this bar.
- **Telling detail** — one sourced concrete particular that carries an inference. Ship as an anchor line.
- **Exhibit** — a quantified beat best carried by a chart or a landed number, not a scene.

**Empty-slot rule (SCAM Gate 1):** an unfilled slot is a *result*, not a problem. It demotes the moment;
it is never filled by inference to keep the form. **No sensory detail may be invented when any of these is
drafted** — no last-night-on-Carson, no clerk at a mail bench, no glow of a monitor. If it is not in the
record it does not exist. That most candidates demote is the method working, exactly as the dossier
predicted (§3).

## 2. The two documentary tableaux (build candidates)

### DT1 — Targeting arrives by acquisition (spring 2007)
**One line.** In two months of 2007 the ad industry did not *build* audience targeting; it *bought* it,
in four dated deals led by a $3.1bn cash agreement.

**Exact ids.** `e6-targeting-004` **A** (confirmed). Artifact quote from the verdict evidence
(`verdicts-era-6.json`, claim_id `e6-targeting-004`): Google's Form 8-K exhibit 99.1 of **13 April 2007**
states *"a definitive agreement to acquire DoubleClick Inc. ... for $3.1 billion in cash."*

**The real sourced detail.** From the claim `statement` (`e6-targeting-004`): audience-based targeting
*"entered the market by acquisition at the end of the era."* The four deals, each dated:

- Google agreed to buy DoubleClick for **$3.1 billion** (April 2007).
- Microsoft bought aQuantive for **about $6 billion** (May 2007).
- Yahoo bought Right Media for **$680 million**.
- WPP bought 24/7 Real Media for **$649 million**.

**SCAM slots.**
- **S** — the US ad market, April–May 2007. Time solid; **place is abstract** (a market, not a room). No
  physical setting is recorded, so none is written.
- **C** — the acquirers as named actors with their prices: Google/$3.1bn, Microsoft/~$6bn, Yahoo/$680M,
  WPP/$649M. The distinguishing behaviour of the system: the targeting layer changes hands as an *asset*.
- **A** — three-plus ordered, sourced, observable events: (1) 13 Apr 2007 Google agrees DoubleClick,
  $3.1bn cash (8-K); (2) May 2007 Microsoft agrees aQuantive, ~$6bn (closed ~$6.3bn Aug 2007, per verdict);
  (3) Yahoo agrees Right Media, $680M; (4) WPP agrees 24/7 Real Media, ~$649M net of cash.
- **M** — the buyer's targeting infrastructure became a seller-owned asset, purchased not invented. Maps
  straight to the targeting dig's DIAL thread: the machinery that *chooses the audience* consolidated into
  the hands that already own the count and the price.

**Why it is vivid.** It is the rare corpus moment with a real action sequence — four datable deals, each a
filing, one of them a verbatim 8-K line. It clears Gate 4 cold: no comparator, no date, no price survives
if you strip the sources.

**Representativeness flag (mandatory).** These are the **largest** targeting deals of the year, selected
for scale. If used in a load-bearing slot, say so on the page: "the four largest such acquisitions of
2007," not "advertisers were buying targeting." Atypical-and-vivid, flagged per the ledger rule.

### DT2 — The two doors (2002)
**One line.** In 2002 two search marketplaces sold admission on opposite terms: Overture's door made a
human read every listing; AdWords Select's door let a machine approve it and charged five dollars once.

**Exact ids.** `e6-pricing-005` **A** (adjusted) carries the gate list from **Overture's own Form 10-K
FY2002**. Event node `era-6 event 2002-02-20` ("AdWords Select") carries the launch and *"Self-serve, four
steps, most ads live immediately"* — **synthesis (event desc), flag it.** Advertiser counts from
`e6-buyers-002` **C** (Overture ~80,000 end-2002).

**The real sourced detail (two verbatim fragments from `e6-pricing-005`, split for length, words
unchanged).** Overture *"required a 10-cent minimum bid per listing plus a $20 minimum monthly spend from
all new US advertisers, with human editorial review of every listing."* AdWords Select, by contrast,
*"charged a one-time $5 activation fee with no minimum monthly spend and automated approval."*

**PROVENANCE CATCH — do not repeat the dig's number.** The same claim self-flags: *"(The 5-cent AdWords
minimum bid and Overture's 2001 5-cent floor are widely repeated but are not supported by either source
cited here and need their own citation before the record carries them.)"* The buyers dig used "5-cent bid";
**the corpus explicitly does not carry it.** Ship the door contrast **without** the 5-cent figure.

**SCAM slots.**
- **S** — the paid-search marketplace, 2002; the two price sheets side by side. Time solid; place abstract.
- **C** — two systems, each with a distinguishing behaviour that *is* the finding: **a human reviews every
  listing** (Overture) versus **a machine approves it** (AdWords Select). That behavioural contrast is the
  telling detail; it is sourced, not decorative.
- **A** — thin. Two contrasting *states* plus one dated event (AdWords Select announced 20 Feb 2002). Not a
  true three-event sequence, so this is a **diptych/tableau**, not a full scene. Gate 1 demotes it honestly.
- **M** — the market's one un-gated door (self-serve, no human gate, near-zero rent) is the door that won.
  Maps to the buyers dig: the rent did not vanish; it moved to the platform's take, and the census of who
  came through went dark.

**Why it is vivid.** "A human read every listing" is a concrete, sourced, behavioural fact that carries the
whole admission argument in one clause. The $5-once-vs-$20-a-month contrast lands the rent collapse without
a chart.

**Cautions.** Advertiser count ~80,000 is grade **C** (`e6-buyers-002`, interval 550k–2.6M applies to the
1.1M endpoint, not this one). The "long tail of plumbers, locksmiths" phrasing is **era-6 BUYERS summary
synthesis** — colour, never a graded number.

## 3. Telling details (ship as anchor lines, not scenes)

### TD1 — Cigarettes off the air (2 January 1971)
- **One line.** Overnight, a buyer class paying broadcasters $225 million a year could no longer buy air
  at all; its $50 million of print spend was all that survived.
- **Ids.** `e3-buyers-004` **B** (confirmed). Verbatim source quotes in the verdict evidence
  (`verdicts-era-3.json`): *"Cigarette advertising accounted for $225 million a year in revenue to
  broadcasters"* and, for print, *"it amounted to approximately $50 million in annual revenue."*
- **Why vivid / why only a telling detail.** A dated-to-the-day expulsion of the market's biggest single
  broadcast buyer — the state as bouncer, quantified. But the famous scene (the last spot on late-night TV)
  is **not in the corpus**. No room, no person, no ordered action. **Do not invent it.** Ship the two
  numbers and the date; that is the whole vivid fact.

### TD2 — The four-pound catalogue to a named household (1904)
- **One line.** Montgomery Ward mailed roughly three million catalogues a year, each to a named household —
  the era's only individually addressable medium.
- **Ids.** `e1-targeting-002` **B** (confirmed). Statement: *"...each addressed to a named household - the
  era's only individually addressable medium."*
- **Candidate telling detail (flag before prose).** The verdict evidence (`verdicts-era-1.json`) quotes a
  source: *"By 1904, the company distributed three million catalogs that weighed four pounds each."* The
  **four pounds** is the object made physical — the weight of individual addressability, 70 years before
  the ZIP code. **Caveat:** this sits in the verdict layer, its primary citation not read inline; confirm
  the source before it reaches prose. The 3M / named-household fact is graded and shippable now.
- **Why only a telling detail.** Named actor, date, artifact — but no located moment and no action sequence.

### TD3 — Coen's spreadsheet admits its own blindness (2007)
- **One line.** The century-spanning ad-spend series flags, in its own worksheet, that it is undercounting
  the internet by half — then stops in 2007.
- **Ids.** `e6-medium-006` **A** (confirmed). Artifact quote from the verdict evidence
  (`verdicts-era-6.json`): the Coen workbook's internet worksheet, row 2, reads *"The Coen internet ad
  expenditure figures are serious underestimates for 2004-2007."* Claim statement: Coen $10,529m for 2007
  against IAB/PwC's $21,206m; the series terminates with 2007 data.
- **Why vivid.** A rare *documentary artifact* seed — a line typed into a spreadsheet cell. The measuring
  instrument writes down the exact moment it goes blind, which is the medium dig's whole mechanism. Clears
  the contemporaneous-written-record bar for a quoted artifact.

### TD4 — "No cookie and no profile" (Q4 2001)
- **One line.** Overture delivered 442 million intent-matched clicks in one quarter by targeting the words
  the user typed — with no cookie and no profile.
- **Ids.** `e5-targeting-004` **A** (confirmed). Statement: *"...targeting by the words the user typed, with
  no cookie and no profile."* ~53,000 advertisers, Q4 2001.
- **Why vivid.** A one-clause rebuttal to the "targeting always meant surveillance" story: the finest intent
  targeting of its moment carried no identity at all. Telling detail, not a scene (no located action).

### TD5 — The ZIP key (1 July 1963)
- **One line.** The state shipped a machine-readable geographic key a decade before anyone built
  geodemographic targeting on top of it.
- **Ids.** `e3-targeting-004` **A** (adjusted). Statement carries its own caveat: the Post Office's planned
  announcement mailing of *"about 72 million cards"* is *"from period press reporting rather than from the
  USPS or Library of Congress records cited here."* **Preserve that hedge; the date is the solid part.**
- **Why vivid.** The state as enabler (pairs with ATT 2021 as re-separator). The dial the market later
  handed back was first cut by a government key. Telling detail.

## 4. Exhibits (number-landings and series — carry as charts/anchor lines)

Run per `numbers-in-narrative`: direction before magnitude, a comparison on the same screen, source
precision held, intervals and hedges preserved.

- **EX1 — 279,000× finer (1975).** `e3-targeting-005` **A** (adjusted). Direct mail could address **71.1
  million US households one at a time** — a granularity *"roughly 279,000 times finer than the average unit
  spot television could sell."* Comparison on the same screen: Nielsen's TV-universe count was 70.5M the
  same year. **Precision note (adjusted verdict):** 71,120k is the **Census** figure; 70,520k is the
  **Nielsen panel** universe — different denominators, do not blend. The peak of the reach ladder; the
  yield never followed (see EX4).
- **EX2 — The razor-thin crossover (1992).** `e4-medium-002` **B** (confirmed). Total television first
  passed newspapers as the largest US medium in 1992 — **$31,079M against $30,737M**, a gap of about
  **$342M, roughly 1 percent**. Broadcast TV alone was *still behind* newspapers in 1993 ($28,020M vs
  $32,025M). The "TV revolution" crossed the line late and by a nose. **Do not round the margin away — the
  thinness is the point.**
- **EX3 — The census goes dark.** `e5-buyers-001` **A** (~3,800, 1998) → `e5-buyers-002` **A** (~53,000,
  2001) → `e6-buyers-002` **C** (~1.1M, 2008, *"Google never disclosed the figure,"* interval **550k–2.6M**)
  → `e7-buyers-001` **B** (Meta *">10 million active advertisers,"* no located 2025 primary filing). A
  public SEC-filed series that runs into a seller-held hole. Ship the endpoints **with their intervals**;
  the hole is the finding.
- **EX4 — Two of a hundred was a success.** `e4-targeting-004` **B** (confirmed). *"The best-targeted medium
  of the era still missed 98 out of 100 recipients: a 2% response rate on a rented list was the working
  definition of a successful direct-mail campaign."* The yield floor against which EX1's precision is
  measured. **Guard:** this is one era's rate (1976–93); the corpus forbids stretching it to a century (see
  `e5-targeting-005`'s read-vs-mailed guard).
- **EX5 — Radio survives by re-pricing its unit (1950–60).** `e3-medium-009` **B** (confirmed). Network
  radio **collapsed from $196M to $43M** while local spot radio **grew from $273M to $428M** — *"radio
  survived the era by becoming a local retail medium."* The carrier did not die; its unit of sale changed.
- **EX6 — Overture's own 10-K yardstick (2001).** `e5-targeting-005` **B** (confirmed). Overture set its
  **170–180M searches/day** against Yellow Pages (**25–30M calls/day**) and direct mail (**1.7M responses
  on 10.7M pieces READ/day**). **Built-in denominator guard (ship it, never strip it).** 10.7M read is
  ~4% of the ~250–300M pieces delivered daily. The implied 15.9% is a rate *on pieces read*. It *"must not
  be compared"* with EX4's 2% *on pieces mailed*.

**Also available, lower priority.**

- TV enters at 1.1% / $58M in 1949 (`e2-medium-006` **B**).
- Machine Market runs $23,448M (2008) → $294,593M (2025), only down year 2009, −3% (`e7-medium-001` **A**).
- Radio's 1932 basis war: 3.9% vs "nearly 11 percent," *"the conflict is not resolved"* (`e2-medium-003` **B**).
- PRIZM sorts all ~36,000 ZIPs into 40 clusters, 1974 (`e4-targeting-001` **B**).
