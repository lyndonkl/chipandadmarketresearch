## The built tableaux

### B1 — Benjamin Day prints the loss-leader on his own front page

**One line:** On 3 September 1833 Day launched the New York *Sun* at one cent — below the cost of printing it —
and set the business model in type on page one.

**Claim ids:** `e1-events-001` (B; 1¢, ~15,000 copies/day within ~2 years) + graph event node
`tu:era:1:event:1` (the front-page quotation, "The penny press invents ad-subsidised content").

**The telling detail (one particular, verbatim):** Day printed the paper's purpose himself. It would lay the
news before the public *"at a price within the means of every one, and at the same time offer an advantageous
medium for advertisements."* That single line *is* the sell-side invention: the reader is sold content below
cost; the advertiser is invited to pay the difference. It could be photographed. It cannot be written without
the source.

**SCAM slots (system protagonist):**
- **S —** New York, first issue, 3 Sep 1833; a one-cent daily priced below production cost.
- **C —** system state: 1¢ cover, ~15,000 copies/day within ~2 years — a below-cost product that scaled.
- **A —** three ordered, sourced events: (1) launched at 1¢; (2) printed the model on the front page; (3) reached
  ~15,000/day inside two years.
- **M —** below-cost, ad-funded content was a *sell-side* invention — the ur-text every later free medium reruns.

**Why it is vivid:** the founder wrote the deal on the door. A price (1¢), a scale (15k/day), and the man's own
words, all datable — Gate 4 passes hard.

**Non-negotiable caveats for drafting:**
- **Anachronism flag on the page:** "loss-leader / cross-subsidy / subsidy" are *modern* frames. Day's own word
  was "an advantageous medium." Use his frame, mark ours as ours.
- **Representativeness:** Day is the *inventor*, i.e. atypical-and-vivid, not the median 1830s paper. Say so.
- No added sensory detail (no weather, no press-room). The record holds a front page and two numbers; that is all.

### B2 — "Butternut Squash": the price knob the record cannot reproduce

**One line:** In the 2024 Mehta findings of fact, three levers that set the price of Google's search ads are
named — and one of them was launched internally under the codename "Butternut Squash," its actual number still
sealed.

**Claim id:** `mech-knobs-001` (A; FOF ¶¶245–255).

**The telling detail (verbatim, court-sourced):** the auction's own operators called these levers *"intentional
pricing"*; one, *"Butternut Squash"* (squashing — an exponent applied to predicted click-through rate), has
*"never been publicly disclosed, so its price effect cannot be reproduced from the record."* A grocery-aisle
codename on the lever that meters the world's search-ad prices — and the exponent behind it is a blank in the
public record.

**SCAM slots (system protagonist):**
- **S —** the Mehta opinion's findings of fact (Doc. 1033), 2024; the search text-ads auction.
- **C —** system state: three named knobs on one auction — squashing, format pricing, rGSP — all internally
  labelled "intentional pricing."
- **A —** three sourced facts: (1) the knobs are named in the FOF; (2) they are described internally as
  intentional pricing; (3) the squashing exponent has never been disclosed and cannot be reproduced.
- **M —** the "neutral" clearing price is a *governed* price — set by seller parameters, one of them still secret.

**Why it is vivid:** the whimsy of the codename against the weight of what it does, and the single undisclosed
number that ends the sentence. Grade **A**, court-sourced — the strongest telling detail in Thread #5.

**Caveats:** "Butternut Squash" is one of three knobs — illustrative of the "intentional pricing" pattern, not
the whole mechanism; keep the other two (format pricing, rGSP) in frame so it is not read as *the* cause.

### B3 — The stated ideal, dated and quoted: "pay the lowest amount possible"

**One line:** On 20 February 2002 Google announced AdWords Select — a quality-weighted second-price auction —
and promised, in the launch language, that advertisers would "pay the lowest amount possible."

**Claim ids:** `mech-adwords-001` / `e6-pricing-001` (A).

**The telling detail (verbatim, press release):** ads ranked by *"a combination of ad performance (click-thru
rate) and how much an advertiser agrees to pay per click,"* with a discounter so the advertiser would *"pay the
lowest amount possible."* This is the neutral ideal in the seller's own words — the runner-up sets the price —
and it is the promise every later knob (B2, B4, B9) is measured against.

**SCAM slots (system protagonist):**
- **S —** the AdWords Select announcement, 20 Feb 2002.
- **C —** system state: quality-weighted second-price pricing from launch (a discounter above the runner-up bid).
- **A —** three sourced facts: ranked by CTR × bid; discounter applied; the "lowest amount possible" promise made.
- **M —** the neutral auction was *stated* as an ideal at a datable moment — so its later governance is a
  departure the reader can time.

**Why it is vivid:** a verbatim founding promise with a date, grade A. It is the "before" the whole governed-price
thread needs, and it is quotable without embellishment.

**Caveats:** pair with the **residual** (B12): the runner-up price was never the market's baseline — Overture's
founding auction was pure *first*-price, "pay the amount of its bid" (`e5-pricing-004`, A). Second-price is a
Google-era artifact, not an Eden.

### B4 — Dischler on the stand: the undisclosed "tunings"

**One line:** Google's VP of ads testified that the company adjusted its search auction and reserve prices to
hit quarterly revenue targets — without telling advertisers — and that the changes raised prices by as much as
about 5% on average.

**Claim ids:** `mech-tuning-001` / `e7-pricing-003` (**B** — testimony; do not upgrade).

**The telling detail:** the record renders Jerry Dischler's testimony that the *"tunings"* raised prices *"by as
much as about 5% on average,"* and that *"a 10% increase was possible in some instances"* — undisclosed to the
advertisers who paid them. A named officer conceding, on the record, that the quarter set the price.

**SCAM slots (system protagonist):**
- **S —** the antitrust trial record; the search ad auction.
- **C —** system state: auction and reserve prices adjusted to meet quarterly revenue targets, undisclosed.
- **A —** three sourced facts: prices tuned to targets; not disclosed to advertisers; Dischler quantifies the lift.
- **M —** the seller does not merely run the auction — it moves the clearing price to hit its own number.

**Why it is vivid:** a human name and a conceded figure. The scene *is* the concession.

**Caveats:**
- **Bright-line 1:** *"tunings," "as much as about 5%," "a 10% increase … in some instances"* are the **record's
  rendering** of testimony, not certified verbatim dialogue — present as *what he testified*, not as speech in
  quotation marks unless the transcript string is confirmed.
- **Grade B — cannot carry the thesis alone.** The A-grade court numbers (B2, B9) do the load-bearing; this is
  corroboration, and its hedges ("as much as," "about," "possible … in some instances") survive the brevity pass.

### B5 — Bok gives up the money: the residual

**One line:** In 1892 Edward Bok announced the *Ladies' Home Journal* would take no more patent-medicine
advertising — surrendering a major revenue line — and within two years seven other periodicals followed.

**Claim ids:** graph event node `tu:era:1:event:4` (**ungraded — event text only**). Graded neighbours for
context: `e1-buyers-001`/`e1-buyers-002` (patent medicine 26% of Ayer's revenue in 1878 → 15% by 1900),
`e1-pricing-003` (LHJ ad revenue ~$500,000/yr by 1897). The Adams/Collier's figure — US patent-medicine spend
put at $75,000,000 in 1905 — sits on `e1-scale-014` (C).

**The telling detail:** a seller *destroying its own inventory on trust grounds* — the one outcome a "maximise
advertiser payment" reading of the whole model cannot predict. The record calls it "the first documented case
of a seller restricting its own inventory on trust grounds, and the opening move in the fight that produced the
Pure Food and Drug Act."

**SCAM slots (system protagonist):**
- **S —** the *Ladies' Home Journal*, 1892.
- **C —** system state: a profitable magazine (~$500k/yr ad revenue by 1897) refusing a live revenue category.
- **A —** three sourced events: Bok announces the refusal; a major revenue line is given up; seven periodicals
  follow within two years.
- **M —** the below-cost model has a governor: when the payer's product is toxic, the seller can restrict its
  own inventory — the residual that keeps the thread honest.

**Why it is vivid:** it cuts against its own thesis, which is exactly why it must ship (mandatory residual).

**Caveats — read before use:**
- **Provenance gap:** the refusal itself is an **event node with no graded L0 claim.** It can carry the *moment*
  (color) but **no number rides on it.** Any figure attached must come from the graded neighbours above.
- Representativeness: a first-mover, explicitly atypical. Flag on the page.

## Summary / number beats (correctly NOT scenes)

### B6 — The funding click (contested; do not harden)
**Ids:** `e7-events-008` / `e7-measurement-004` (B) — *Google disputes the Pew study.*
**Landing (contested-figure grammar, direction before magnitude):** where an AI summary appeared, Pew found
readers clicked through to a result on about **8%** of visits, against about **15%** where none did. On just
**1%** of visits did they click a source *inside* the summary. Google disputes the study. **Permitted verbs:** "was
lower where," "coincided with." **Banned:** "AI Overviews cut the click." This is a number, not a scene: no
located moment, no actor in a room.

### B7 — 1922 vs 2025 (the load-bearing contrast)
**Ids:** `e2-scale-004` (B; 3.0% of GDP 1922, highest in the 1919–2007 series), `e7-scale-002` (C; ~1.32% in
2025, $405B/$30.762T), `e2-scale-005` (B; 1.2% floor in 1944).
**Landing:** US ad spend ran at **3.0% of GDP in 1922** — the highest reading in the whole 1919–2007 series —
and at about **1.32% in 2025**, barely above the wartime floor of **1.2%** it hit in 1944. **Mandatory
disclosure travels with this comparison.** The 1919–2007 Coen/McCann series and the 2025 MAGNA basis are *not
strictly commensurable*. The fall is "partly real and partly a change in what 'advertising' counts." Silk &
Berndt call it "a growth problem, a measurement problem, or both." A data tableau; no scene.

### B8 — The instrument disagrees with itself (telling detail)
**Id:** `e2-scale-009` (C). Coen's 1938 radio figure ($167M) is about **1.66×** the FCC's audited net-time-sales
figure for the whole broadcast industry that year ($100.9M) — "consistent with the series being built on list
rate-card prices rather than transaction prices." The reflexive gut-punch: the century-long instrument the
paradox rides on overstates one audited year by two-thirds. Superb detail; not a scene.

### B9 — rGSP 2019: the constraint that binds (systemic peak, not scene)
**Ids:** `mech-rgsp-001` / `e7-pricing-001` (A). rGSP raised top-slot CPC on non-navigational queries by
**5.91%** (PC/tablet) and **4.85%** (mobile) in pre-launch tests; a 5.74% revenue gain persisted two months.
The finding that lands: **"advertisers cannot opt out."** This is the CONSTRAINT-BINDING climax of Thread #5
(a systemic state transition), staged as an exhibit — not a located scene.

### B10 — Not everything is the platforms' doing (number + guardrail)
**Id:** `e6-scale-009` (**A**, NOT_CAUSED_BY search). Help-wanted classified fell from its 2000 peak of
$8,712.6m to **$786.8m by 2009 — a 91% collapse**; real-estate classified peaked in 2006 and then "fell with
the housing market, not with search." A graded guardrail against blaming Google for the whole decline. Ship
with Thread #6; number, not scene.

### B11 — Munsey's price cut (summary beat)
**Id:** `e1-sellers-005` (B). *Munsey's* cut its cover from **25¢ to 10¢ in 1893, below production cost**; ad
revenue then averaged **~$25,000–$35,000 per issue**. A dated decision with a price — the second proof of the
sell-side invention — but no artifact/scene grammar. Runs as summary alongside B1. (Munsey's own reach estimate,
250k→750k magazine-buying public, is `e1-events-003`, **C**, promoter's number, no independent count — keep the
hedge, do not lead on it.)

### B12 — Overture's hand-checked auction (texture)
**Ids:** `e5-pricing-004` / `mech-overture-001` (A). The pre-Google auction was pure first-price — "each
advertiser pays Overture the amount of its bid" — with a **10¢-per-listing floor**, a $20 monthly minimum, and
**"human editorial review of every listing."** Realised ~$0.31/click in 2002. The texture that makes the
residual concrete: the runner-up price was never the baseline, and the founding auction was hand-checked.
