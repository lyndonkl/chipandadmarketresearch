## 0. The one-line finding

**Session 1's spine is entirely sell-side and state-side** — *who counts, who prices, who concentrates.*
The hub sweep says the largest structurally-central cluster it never made a protagonist is the **buyer**,
and the buyer was **never one actor**. For ~150 years the demand side splits four ways — **national brand /
local retail / classified / direct-response**. One of those four, the **direct-response buyer**, never
accepted the seller's audience number at all. It paid per *keyed action* — cost per order, not cost per
thousand — from the 1900s. That buyer was 16% of spend in 1914, **larger than broadcast by 1956**, the
single largest national medium by 1975, 22% by 1993 — and its pay-per-action pricing model **won the
internet** (57% of US internet revenue, 2008). The platform era is not only the seller re-capturing the
count (S1); it is the **direct-response buyer's logic becoming the whole market's logic.**

**How it complicates the meta-spine:** S1 explains *who owns the number.* The buyer cluster explains *why
the market kept demanding one* — because a large, load-bearing buyer class always paid by it. And it closes
on an irony the spine sharpens. The direct-response buyer spent 150 years escaping the seller's audience
count by paying only for measured actions. It then arrived in the one medium — search — where the seller
counts the actions too (Threads 1/5: Google validates its own clicks; click-fraud unadjudicated). **The
buyer who most wanted an independent number ended up in the market with the least independent number.**

## 2. Hub 1 (highest yield) — Direct-response buyers: the keyed number that was never new

**The node anchors the single cleanest cross-era through-line in the quarter.** Six eras, each quantified,
grades preserved:

- **1914** — direct-response buyers (mail-order houses, patent-medicine makers, "every advertiser working a
  keyed reply") spent **~$192M, ~16% of the $1,200M benchmark** `[e1-buyers-008 **C**, wide CI80 96–360]`.
- **1935 → 1949** — advertising mail per US household **$8.70 → $17.60** `[e2-buyers-007 **C**]`.
- **1956** — direct mail was **24% of all US national advertising expenditure — a larger share than
  broadcast** — "which is why direct-response buyers **cannot be treated as a later-era phenomenon**"
  `[e3-buyers-001 **B**]`. ← **built-in corrective edge.**
- **1950 → 1975** — direct-response (proxied by direct mail) **14.1% → 14.8% of all US ad spend ($4.12bn),
  the largest single national medium at era end** `[e3-buyers-009 **B**]`.
- **1993** — ~**$31B, ~22% of measured US ad spend, "bought against a measured cost per order rather than a
  cost per thousand"** `[e4-buyers-007 **C**]`.
- **2000** — direct-response measured media spend **~$61.2B, 24.7% of the market** (direct mail $44.6B
  dominant); **Overture's whole FY2000 revenue was $103M inside that pool, and Overture's own 10-K
  benchmarked itself against direct mail** `[e5-buyers-013 **C**]`. On the cross-era-comparable rule (all
  directory money in the intent pool) it is **~$72.3B, 29.2%** `[e5-buyers-015 **C**]`.
- **2008** — **57% of US internet advertising revenue was bought on a performance (pay-per-action) basis,
  up from 51% in 2007; only 39% on CPM** — "direct-response buyers dominated the new medium's pricing"
  `[e6-buyers-008 **A**]`.

**The bridge the graph already wired:** Yellow Pages is tagged **"the structural ancestor of paid search"**
`[tu:era:5:field:MEDIUM, synthesis clause]`, and Overture's 10-K self-identified against direct mail
`[e5-buyers-013 C]`. Paid search did not invent the performance buyer; it *inherited* one that had been the
largest single medium for fifty years. **Grade watch:** this thread's spine is B/C at the early ends and
lands on a single **A** (`e6-buyers-008`) at the 2008 hinge — the arc is real but the pre-1975 dollars are
soft (note the $192M CI80 of 96–360).

## 3. Hub 2 — National vs Local: the corrective that kills "the internet democratised demand"

**National advertisers** (deg 18) and **Local advertisers** anchor a share series that runs *against* the
received long-tail story, twice:

- **1915** — national **$55M** of newspaper space vs local+classified **$220M (4×)**; national ~20% of
  newspaper `[e1-buyers-005 / e1-buyers-006 **B**; both third-hand via Borden 1942 → Galbi, PROVENANCE
  FLAG]`.
- **1949** — national advertisers **57.4%** of all US ad dollars `[e2-buyers-004 **B**]`.
- **1976 → 1993** — national **55.1% → 58.1%**, local **44.9% → 41.9%** — national gained `[e4-buyers-001 /
  e4-buyers-002 **B**]`.
- **2002 → 2007** — national **61.4% → 66.3%** ($145.4bn → $185.3bn); national grew 27.4%, local 3.2%, so
  local's share fell ~⅛ — **"the opposite of what a 'search eats local budgets' story predicts"**
  `[e6-buyers-005 **B**, verdict adjusted]`. ← **built-in corrective edge.**
- **2002 → 2007** — brand TV buyers **did not leave**: broadcast $42.1bn → $44.5bn, cable $16.3bn → $26.3bn
  `[e6-buyers-004 **B**]`. ← **corrective edge** (kills "digital killed TV brand money in the 2000s").

**These four are anti-narrative guards for the meta-spine** — the same gift Session 1 found on the seller
side. The demand side did *not* democratise to the long tail through the platform transition; national
brand money *gained* share while the direct-response *pricing model* spread. Two different buyers, two
different stories, routinely conflated.

## 4. Hub 3 — the addressable / geographic targeting lineage (extends R4, adds an axis)

The TARGETING hubs anchor a finer-and-finer addressability ladder — Session 1 flagged the R4 spine
(title→household→person→opt-out); the hub sweep adds the distinct **geographic** rung between household and
query:

- **1869 → 1914** — buyer's targeting menu **5,778 → 22,754 selectable audience units** `[e1-targeting-001
  **B**]`; **Montgomery Ward mailing ~3M catalogues/yr by 1904, each addressed to a named household — "the
  era's only individually addressable medium"** `[e1-targeting-002 **B**]` (anchored by hubs Named
  household, Montgomery Ward, Sears).
- **1966 → 1970** — Nielsen/Arbitron **DMA/ADI, ~200 markets**; average DMA **~279,000 TV households — the
  finest geographic unit a TV advertiser could buy** `[e3-targeting-001 **B** adjusted / e3-targeting-002
  **C** adjusted]`.
- **1974** — **PRIZM classified all ~36,000 US ZIP codes into 40 lifestyle clusters** `[e4-targeting-001
  **B**]`; by **1993, ~253 pieces of third-class mail per person/yr (from 133 in 1980) — "the physical
  footprint of geodemographic targeting"** `[e4-targeting-003 **A**]`.
- **2003–05** — Google extends keyword-intent from queries to page text (AdSense) and makes Quality Score
  (CTR-based) a targeting filter — **"the query, not the person"** `[e6-targeting-001/003 **B**]`.
- **2021–22** — the person opts out: **ATT opt-in ~16% → ~25%** `[e7-targeting-002 **B**]`; GDPR effects
  "modest and contested," 2.1% CTR / 5.7% rev-per-click falls `[e7-targeting-003 **B**]`.

Anchoring hubs Session 1 never named: **PRIZM, Designated Market Area, Named household.** This is R4 with a
sharper spine — *addressability climbs (title → household → DMA → ZIP-cluster → query), then reverses when
the person is handed an opt-out.*

## 5. What this does to the meta-spine (the connection, stated once)

S1: *the independent number was the anomaly; by default the judged seller owns the count/price.* The buyer
cluster supplies the **demand-side half** the spine was missing:

1. **The demand for an independent number had a name and a size.** It was the direct-response buyer, who
   paid per keyed action, not per stated audience — 16% (1914) → largest single medium (1975) → 22% (1993)
   → 57% of internet pricing (2008). The market kept re-litigating "who counts" partly because a
   structurally-central buyer class refused to pay any other way.
2. **The platform era is the direct-response buyer's model winning** — pay-per-action becomes the market
   default. This is *additive* to S1's "seller re-captures the count," not a rival to it.
3. **The closing irony sharpens the spine, not softens it.** The buyer that spent 150 years escaping the
   seller's audience number by paying per measured action arrived, via search, in the one medium where the
   seller counts the actions too (T1/T5). **Most-wanted independent number → least-independent number.**

**Agency reading (required).** The strongest agency reading — *platforms engineered pay-per-action to lock
buyers into a seller-counted metric* — is **not adopted.** The direct-response buyer's preference for keyed
response predates every platform by a century (`e1-buyers-008`, `e3-buyers-001`), and Overture positioned
*itself* against direct mail (`e5-buyers-013`); the pricing model is a buyer-pull inheritance, not a
seller-push design. Named living parties (Google, Meta) surfaced, not adjudicated.
