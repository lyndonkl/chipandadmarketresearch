## 0. The corpus-level finding (the meta-spine six independent threads keep landing on)

Six deep dives and four breadth sweeps, run separately, converged on **one shape**: across ~185 years the
US ad market's *default* condition is that the party being judged owns the number. The seller sets the
price and counts the audience, in the dark. The market's celebrated founding disciplines are the
**exceptions to that default**, not its nature:

- a **disclosed price** (N.W. Ayer's open contract, 1875), and
- a **third-party count** (the Audit Bureau of Circulations, 1914).

Both were hard-won corrections of a pre-reform world of hidden cuts and self-stated circulation. Both then
**decayed back toward the default**. In the platform era they reverted to it wholesale, now at platform
scale and, unlike 1912–14, with **no enforceable third-party backstop**. The only force that has ever
re-separated the counter/pricer from the counted/priced is the **state** — and the state *relocates* or
*ceilings* the structure, it rarely *breaks* it. Meanwhile the whole drama plays out inside a share of the
economy that **peaked in 1922 and has shrunk since**, so "each new medium captured the market" is, on the
one century-spanning instrument, mostly false.

**One line:** *The independent number was the anomaly.* Three threads are the same POSIWID gap at three
layers: #1 (who counts), #2 (who knows the price), #5 (who sets the clearing price). #3 is the state as the
only re-separator. #4 is the money-layer rendering of #1. #6 is the honest denominator that keeps all of
them from over-claiming. This meta-spine is **structural, not agentic** — it held in 1946 and 1950, absent
any platform intent, and the residuals (1987, 2021) show the honest number sometimes hurting whoever paid
for it. Named living parties (Google, Meta) are **surfaced, not adjudicated**.

### THREAD 1 — Who counts the audience *(deep-1)*
**Through-line.** The market's founding discipline — a *third party* counts the audience (ABC 1914,
tripartite-funded, "facts without opinions") — was itself a correction of era-1 publisher self-counting.
That independent count was the **~90-year anomaly (1914–~2004), not the default**. The counter kept ending
up on the payroll of the side it judged: buyer-committee → private vendor 1946 → seller-platform 2002+. In
eras 6–7 it reverted to the pre-1914 condition — the party being judged produces its own number — now with
**no enforceable filing** to backstop it. Recurring question: *who audits the counter?*

**Form: EXPLANATORY NARRATIVE (rung 2).** System subject; YES to form-gate Q1–Q4; **ABSENT** on Q5 (no
single system-wide self-revelation — reform is local per era) and Q6 (ongoing to 2026). Not a Truby arc;
`systemic-protagonist` fits, opposition-web skipped (constraints, not corners). Structure: **SPIRAL over
chronology** (same question re-entered at widening radius, 1869→2026); runner-up strict linear, lost
because recurrence not sequence is the argument.

**ABT.** AND a third party counted the audience (ABC 1914) `[e1-measurement-002 B]`. BUT each
consolidation put the counter on the payroll of the side it judged; by eras 6–7 the same firm sold,
auctioned, counted and attributed, and no third party could adjudicate `[tu:era:6/7:field:MEASUREMENT;
e6-measurement-006 A]`. THEREFORE the discipline took 45 years to build and reverted to the pre-1914
default at platform scale; the IAB scores that failure at **>$26B** of misallocated US media
`[e7-measurement-005 B]`.

**Best cited findings (grades preserved):**
1. ABC 1914, world's first third-party circulation audit, tripartite-funded, members suspendable for
   padding `[e1-measurement-002 B]`; corrected era-1 self-counting `[e1-measurement-001 B]`.
2. Regime transfer #1 (1946): CAB wound up 31 Jul, "sixteen years of buyer-controlled measurement ended
   … passed permanently … to private vendors selling to both sides" `[tu:era:2:event:9]`.
3. Cost of that transfer, quantified: even on Hooper's own method, Hooper's ratings ran **~20% above** the
   CAB's — the count inflated toward the vendor's paying side `[e2-measurement-003 B]`.
4. Auditor-of-the-counter born under threat: Harris hearings 1963–64; the industry "pre-empted regulation"
   by funding the Broadcast Rating Council (1964 → MRC) `[tu:era:3:field:MEASUREMENT]`.
5. **The instrument, not the audience (L1, residual):** people meters (Sept 1987) measured ~10% fewer
   prime-time viewers (CBS/ABC −13%, NBC −4%), make-goods owed exceeded upfront sold; "nothing about the
   audience had changed; only the instrument had" `[e4-measurement-001 B]`.
6. Seller becomes auditor: Google recorded the impression, ran the auction, decided which click was valid,
   billed its own log; the court-appointed examiner was "commissioned by the defendant and paid for by a
   settlement" `[tu:era:6:field:MEASUREMENT; e6-measurement-001 B]`.
7. **Publishes the rate, never the count (grade A hinge):** Google disclosed ~18% YoY paid-click *growth*,
   "never an absolute click count, an average CPC, or a query count" `[e6-measurement-006 A, SEC 8-K]`.
8. Century-old rule breaks: Facebook overstated video watch time ~2 yrs; plaintiffs *alleged* up to 900%;
   **$40M** settlement 2019 `[e7-measurement-001 B]`.
9. **Surviving auditor's bite (residual):** MRC stripped Nielsen's TV accreditation 2021 (2–6% undercount,
   VAB ≥$468M), reinstated national 2023 `[e7-measurement-002 B]`; bots >50% of web traffic
   `[e7-measurement-003 B]`; AI summaries cut the funding click 15%→8% `[e7-measurement-004 B]`.

**Constraint (not a villain):** the *economics of counting* — whoever funds the count controls it, and the
cheapest place to put the counter is inside the seller. **Mandatory residuals (block the fatalist and the
malign-optimizer readings):** 1987 people meter (honest count hurt the seller); 2021 MRC bit the incumbent.
**Hypothesis diff:** started at three regimes / "in kind or scale?"; ended at a **four-state spiral** —
1914 was itself a correction, so eras 6–7 are a *reversion to the pre-1914 default*, different in scale
and enforceability, **largely the same in kind**.

### THREAD 2 — The 15% that would not die *(deep-2)*
**Through-line.** For ~185 years the US ad agency has been paid out of one gap — between what the
advertiser pays and what the media costs — and it is paid most when that gap is *hidden*. Ayer's 1875 open
contract forced the price into the open and fixed the take at a visible 15%. That number then refused to
die on every date the received story assigns it, and era 7's shift to undisclosed-markup "principal media"
quietly returns the trade to the concealed-price world Ayer reformed away. The visible surface was always
"15%"; the real variable was always **who knows the price** — and it has gone dark again.

**Form: EXPLANATORY (myth-buster).** Near-STORY (single continuous entity, rise→peak→fall), system
protagonist = the agency **compensation regime**.

**ABT.** AND the agency was born a broker paid a hidden cut by the seller; Ayer 1875 disclosed the price
and fixed 15%, cartelized 1893, frozen by the 4As 1918. BUT the number would not die on cue — a 1956
consent decree struck the *enforcement* `[e3-creators-001 A]` yet **71%** of big advertisers were still on
commission in 1982 `[e3-creators-002 B]`, and the census rate held near **14% to 1992** `[e4-pricing-001
A]`. THEREFORE the "death of the 15%" is a mis-dated legend; era 7's undisclosed-markup principal media
(ANA/K2 2016) returns the trade to 1875's concealed-price world.

**Two series, deliberately not merged (the keystone split):**
- **RATE** (Economic Census, grade A, `e4-pricing-001`): 14.13% (1977) · 13.92% (1982) · 14.16% (1987) ·
  13.18% (1992) · **10.98% (1997)**. "The 15% commission survived this era largely intact; its collapse
  comes after it." Already ~11% in practice before the web mattered `[e5-creators-003 A]`.
- **MIX** (ANA, grade B): advertisers on a commission basis **71% (1982) → 61% (1994) → ~10% (2003)**;
  labour fees **8% → 31%** `[e3-creators-002, e5-creators-001, e6-creators-002]`.

**Keystone corrective.** What collapsed was the **mix, not the rate**, and it "followed the 1991–94
unbundling of media buying from creative, **not** the arrival of search advertising" `[e6-creators-002 B,
NOT_CAUSED_BY edge]`. **Era-7 close:** principal media = resale at an undisclosed markup; ANA/K2 2016 found
pervasive hidden rebates; 82% of ANA members ran an in-house agency by 2023 `[e7-creators-002 B]`.

**Constraint (not a villain):** the opacity/disclosure oscillation and its enforcement scaffolding
(1893 recognition rule → 4As → 1956 DOJ decree → competition/unbundling). **DEAD/CONTRADICTED (must
ship):** "15% died in the 1980s" — CONTRADICTED `[e4-pricing-001 A, e3-creators-002 B]`; "search killed the
commission" — CONTRADICTED `[e6-creators-002 B]`. The **1875↔2016 rhyme is a hedged L4 hypothesis**, not a
graph-proven causal identity.

### THREAD 3 — The state moves the top of the stack *(deep-3)*
**Through-line (corrected, non-teleological).** Concentration at the top of the ad stack is **built by the
market** — network effects, below-cost distribution cheques, acquisitions — and has only ever been **moved
by the state**. But *moved* is not *broken*: across ~90 years the state **relocated** it (1943, 1984),
**ceilinged** it (1954), or **named it unlawful with the remedy pending** (2025). The one genuine
deconcentration came from a **supply shock the state licensed but did not design** (the postwar station
boom). The 2024–25 Google rulings are the fourth turn of the same wheel.

**Form: GATHERING / braided (3–4 rupture nodes).** System protagonist (seller-plus-intermediary layer);
outcome already known; no single continuous willing entity → not a Truby arc.

**Two currencies tracked separately (never one series):**
- **1938 peak:** NBC+CBS took 44% of the industry's **$100,892,259** net time + 7% owned = **51%, two
  firms**; networks **kept 73% ($32,046,218)**, paid 27% to 253 affiliates `[e2-sellers-001 A,
  e2-sellers-002 A; FCC 1941]`.
- **2025 peak:** top-10 sellers **84.1%** (up from 78.6% in 2021) `[e7-sellers-001 A]`; GOOG/META/AMZN
  ~61% (eMarketer) / **71.9%** (`e7-sellers-004` basis — both kept, different bases); AdX ~20% take rate
  since 2009 vs rivals ~10% (DOJ-alleged) `[e7-pricing-006 B]`.

**Best cited findings:**
- **1943, state broke it.** FCC forces RCA to sell NBC Blue; Noble buys it $8M → ABC. But the *actual*
  dollar reallocation came from a supply shock: **~900 AM stations (1945) → ~2,500 (early 1950s)**
  `[e2-sellers-006 B]`.
- **1954, standing ceiling.** FCC caps any owner at seven TV stations `[e3-sellers-002 A]`.
- **1984, new sellers.** The AT&T divestiture handed the RBOCs Yellow Pages ($9.5B in 1993, > radio,
  > magazines) `[e4-sellers-004 B]`.
- **2024–25, fourth rupture.** Mehta 2024-08-05 (89.2% search, 88% text ads, **$26.3B** default share in
  2021) + Brinkema 2025-04-17 (ad server + exchange) `[e7-events-009 A, mech-mehta-004 A]`.
- **Market built it by capital, not a better product.** Overture **led** entering era 6 ($667.7M vs
  Google's $439.5M total, `e6-sellers-001 A`); "Google's answer was not a better auction but a bigger
  cheque" (AOL ~$100M guarantee + ~85% rev share, `mech-aol-001 B`).

**Load-bearing human:** Judge Leonie Brinkema (E.D. Va.) — let the keyword-auction model stand in 2004
`[e6-events-004 B]`, found Google a monopolist of the ad server + exchange in 2025 `[tu:era:7:event:8]`.
One courtroom, 21 years. **Opposition web (market vs state):** A the market (driver, not opponent); B the
state (constraint with documented carriers — FCC, DOJ, Brinkema); C supply shock (environmental,
evidenced); D "capture" skeptic (concentration ≠ capture; pie shrinking). **DEAD:** remedy is *not*
deconcentration on this record — Chrome divestiture declined 2025-09-02 ("generative AI had changed the
course of the case"); Brinkema remedy pending at freeze.

### THREAD 5 — The neutral auction was a governed price *(deep-5)*
**Through-line.** The "open" second-price auction was **governance-thin from birth** (2002–08: no external
audit; the seller ran, validated and billed on its own log). It was sold as a neutral ideal — the
runner-up's bid sets the price, advertisers "pay the lowest amount possible" — but the seller set the
clearing price to hit its own revenue targets, and the advertiser **cannot opt out**. 2019 is not the
death of neutrality; it is the year the seller stopped even *claiming* the runner-up set the price.

**Form: EXPLANATORY (mechanism), rung 2.** YES to Q1–Q4, **NO POINT OF INSIGHT** on Q5 (an external
*court* made the gap legible, Aug 2024 — the system did not see itself differently), NO on Q6. Peak type =
**LEGIBILITY** (the stated/revealed gap becomes court-documented). Opposition-web skipped; constraint
inventory used.

**ABT.** AND era 6 ran on an "open" second-price auction pricing the query, stated as "pay the lowest
amount possible" `[mech-adwords-001 A]`. BUT the seller estimated the quality weight, ran the auction,
validated the click and billed against its own log with **no independent audit 2002–08** `[mech-audit-001
A]`, and by 2019 set the clearing price with named knobs. THEREFORE the neutral price was a **governed**
price all along; 2019–2024 is when the governance became visible and court-graded.

**Best cited findings (A-grade court numbers are load-bearing):**
- **rGSP** (2019, search) raised top-slot CPC on non-navigational queries **5.91%** (PC/tablet) / **4.85%**
  (mobile); **"advertisers cannot opt out"** `[mech-rgsp-001 A, Mehta FOF]`.
- **Three knobs** named in the FOF — squashing (internally **"Butternut Squash"**), format pricing, rGSP —
  all "intentional pricing"; the squashing exponent "has never been publicly disclosed, so its price
  effect cannot be reproduced from the record" `[mech-knobs-001 A]`.
- Format pricing had grown to **~20%** of search text-ads revenue per thousand queries before rGSP replaced
  it `[mech-format_pricing-001 A]`.
- Dischler (VP ads) testified the undisclosed "tunings" raised prices "**by as much as about 5% on
  average**," up to **10%** in some instances, to hit quarterly targets `[mech-tuning-001 B — do not
  upgrade]`.
- Leakage: publishers keep **~69¢** of an advertiser dollar inside Google's stack; DOJ-alleged Google keeps
  **≥35¢** of open-web display; ISBA/PwC only **51%** reaches the publisher; ANA ~**36¢** of a DSP dollar
  reaches a consumer `[e7-pricing-006/004/005 B]`.

**Constraint inventory (replaces opposition web):** seller revenue-target incentive (A/B); buyer
counter-move (header bidding, bid shading, B); measurement vacuum (no IAB/MRC search-click standard
2002–08, A); external adjudication (Mehta FOF 2024, A). **Mandatory residuals:** (1) the take-rate ratchet
**reverses under competition** (24.7%→21.3%, 2006–08, `mech-tac-003 A`) — a pure maximizer would not have
let the rate fall; (2) the "runner-up price" was **never** the market's baseline — Overture's founding
auction was pure **first**-price `[e5-pricing-004 A]`; second-price is a Google-era artefact, not an Eden.
**DEAD:** "the second-price auction *died*" — did_not_support (rGSP is *randomized* GSP, second-price
lineage; the display first-price move is a *different market*, `mech-first_price-001` EXCLUDES search).

### THREAD 4 — The loss-leader that built free media, and the box that may end it *(deep-4)*
**Through-line.** Ad-funded **below-cost** content was a **sell-side invention**, twice: Benjamin Day's
penny *Sun* (1833) and Frank Munsey's ten-cent magazine (1893). The reader was sold content below cost; the
advertiser paid the difference; by 1914 advertising supplied **64.9%** of newspapers' gross income. 190
years later the AI answer box is **not breaking** the model — it is **transferring the funding click** from
the third-party publisher to the platform. This is Thread #1 at the money layer: *the counter of the
funding action changed hands.*

**Form: GATHERING / DUAL-PROFILE (rung 3).** Q4 (continuous action sequence) fails — 190 years, discrete
dated events. Two nodes (Pole A 1833–1914; Pole B 2007–2026) on one denominator ("advertising subsidises
below-cost content"), with a convergence beat (the model reappears inside the box, 2026). **Do not
manufacture a climax.**

**Best cited findings:**
- Penny *Sun* 1¢, ~15k/day `[e1-events-001 B]`; *Munsey's* 25¢→10¢ below cost, ~$25–35k ad/issue
  `[e1-sellers-005 B]`.
- Ad share of newspaper income **44% (1879) → 64.9% (1914)** `[e1-sellers-004 B, e1-sellers-003 A]`.
- Search is the same engine, ~85% gross margin on a monetised query 2007 `[e6-unit_econ-003 C]`.
- The funding click **8% with an AI summary vs 15% without**, 1% *inside* the summary — **Google disputes
  the Pew study** `[e7-events-008 / e7-measurement-004 B]`.
- Google CBO says the AI Overview "monetises at approximately the same rate as classic search," >2B users
  `[e7-targeting-004 B, CLAIMED_SAME_RATE_AS]`.

**Constraint:** below-cost content **requires** an outside payer, who then holds leverage (in print the
advertiser, until Bok/Adams/Pure Food & Drug 1906; in the box the platform). **Mandatory residual:**
**Bok 1892** — the *Ladies' Home Journal* refusing patent-medicine revenue on trust grounds
`[tu:era:1:event:4, EXCLUDES]`: a pure "maximise advertiser payment" function does not predict a seller
destroying its own inventory. **DEAD (ships):** "AI Overviews *cut* the click" — CONTRADICTED/contested
(observational cross-section, Google disputes); "the 130-year subsidy is being broken" — did_not_support
(no corpus measure of publisher revenue dependence on search referral — **research gap**); "same mechanism
that created it" — did_not_support (creation mechanism ≠ threatened referral click). **Anachronism flag:**
"loss-leader / cross-subsidy" are modern frames; Day called it "an advantageous medium."

### THREAD 6 — Capture, not expansion: the ad-intensity paradox *(deep-6)*
**Through-line.** Every era's advertising "explosion" sits on a real *intensity* that peaked at **3.0% of
GDP in 1922** and never came back. The platform era's ~$405B looks like conquest; on the one instrument
that spans the century it reads as the lowest ad/GDP since WWII. The corpus author built this
capture-vs-expansion frame into the era fields — the graph already knows it is the spine.

**Form: EXPLANATORY (governing thesis / single-measure-that-moved).** No human protagonist — the
load-bearing actor is **the instrument** (Coen/McCann series via Galbi, problematized by Silk & Berndt
NBER WP 28161).

**The quantified arc (ad spend as % of US GDP, grades/verdicts preserved):** 1922 **3.0%** (series max,
`e2-scale-004 B`) · 1944 **1.2%** floor (`e2-scale-005 B`) · 1975 **1.66%** (`e3-scale-004 B`) · 2000
**2.5%** Galbi / 2.3–2.4% Silk & Berndt (`e4-scale-003, e5-scale-002 B`) · 2007 2.0% · **2025 ~1.32%**
($405B / $30.762T, `e7-scale-002 C`). **Two-regime paradox:** TV regime nominal spend rose **4.89×
(+6.6%/yr)** 1950–75 while intensity **fell** 1.9%→1.66%; platform regime nominal ~$280B→$405B while
intensity **fell** 2.0%→1.32%. "Television took share from other media rather than enlarging the pot"
`[tu:era:3:field:SCALE]`; the four money-type pools barely moved (43/36/7/14 in 1950 → 40/38/8/15 in 1975,
`tu:era:3:field:BUYERS`).

**Mandatory residuals (kept, not resolved):**
1. A **real intensity climb** 1976→2000, 1.66%→2.5% — ~0.85 pts of genuine deepening, not pure
   reallocation (`e4-scale-003 B`, formally EXCLUDES "summit phase").
2. The 2025 fall is **"partly real and partly a change in what 'advertising' counts"**. Platforms sell
   money older series never counted (Yellow Pages, classified lineage, co-op/trade, catalogue, lead-gen);
   Silk & Berndt frame it "a growth problem, a measurement problem, or both" `[e7-scale-002 C]`.

**The non-negotiable disclosure that travels with every cross-splice comparison:** the 1919–2007 Coen
series and the 2025 MAGNA basis are **not strictly commensurable**. **Instrument bias at both ends:** Coen's
1938 radio figure runs **~1.66× the FCC's audited** net-time-sales (rate-card not transaction prices,
`e2-scale-009 C`) — peak possibly overstated; the 2025 basis excludes direct mail — trough possibly
understated. **Guardrail (grade A):** real-estate classified "peaked mid-era in 2006 and then fell with the
housing market, not with search" `[e6-scale-009 A, NOT_CAUSED_BY]`.

## 3. Scene-seeds worth keeping

**Form ruling for the whole corpus:** this is a **documentary record** — ratings books, FCC filings, SEC
exhibits, court opinions, census tables. Nobody wrote down the room, the light, the gesture. So almost

## 6. Discipline carried forward (keep the next session honest)

- **Provenance, not verification.** Every finding is *sourced*; none is *verified*. Never write that
  anything was fact-checked or confirmed.
- **`tu:era:*` numbers are synthesis (SECONDARY).** Re-pull the backing `e#-` claim before any number
  reaches prose; flagged wherever a SCALE/PRICING field shows `...`.
- **Cross-check every `e#-` id against `verdicts-era-*.json` before use.** A **rejected** claim still wears
  its original grade inside `era-*.json` (integrity seam). Known rejected: `e2-creators-001`,
  `e7-medium-003`, `ds-gdp-001`, `ds-total-001`.
- **Never harden a grade or a causal tier.** A correlation stays a correlation; "alleged" stays alleged
  (FB 900%); "reported/approx." stays hedged (AOL $100M, never SEC-filed).
- **The commensurability caveat is non-removable:** the 1919–2007 Coen series and the 2025 MAGNA basis are
  **not strictly commensurable**; every cross-splice ad/GDP comparison ships with it. The Coen 1938 radio
  figure runs ~1.66× the FCC's audited number — ship that with any 1920s–30s intensity claim.

- **Named living parties (Google, Meta, the judges) are surfaced, not adjudicated** — routed to human
