## 0. The Session-2 finding

Session 1 converged on one shape: **the independent number was the anomaly.** By default the judged seller
owns the count and the price. The founding disciplines — a disclosed price, a third-party count — were
hard-won exceptions that decayed back to the default. That spine was **entirely sell-side and state-side**:
who counts, who prices, who concentrates, who re-separates.

The unexplored quarter (TARGETING / BUYERS / MEDIUM, ~140 graded claims) supplies the three things that spine
lacked. Each **re-renders the same contradiction at a new layer**, so the corpus is coherent, not forked.

1. **THE DEMAND SIDE — the buyer, never one actor.** The quarter's densest unfeatured cluster is the buyer.
   It splits four ways for 150 years: national / local / classified / direct-response `[hub sweep §0]`. Three
   new threads render it — the **dial** (who chose the target, `s2-dig-targeting`), the **door** (who was
   admitted, `s2-dig-buyers`), the **meter** (who priced a response, `hub sweep §2`). The spine gains a twin:
   *the buyer's independent hand was also the anomaly.*

2. **THE MECHANISM — birth, not only drift.** S1 framed the count's reversion to the seller as
   *consolidation*: the counter ends up on the payroll. `s2-dig-medium` shows a second, deeper route:
   **birth.** Every new carrier ships a new *unit of sale* — the rating, the spot, the impression, the click,
   the algorithmic conversion. Each new unit is born before any third party can measure it, so the seller
   counts it first. The century-spanning Coen series understates internet by half exactly as the web arrives
   `[e6-medium-006 A]`. S1 had a pattern; S2 gives it a motor.

3. **THE BASIS — the floor under the headline number.** S1's Thread 6 hinges on "advertising fell to ~1.32%
   of GDP." `s2-dig-directmail` shows the denominator itself is the anomaly. Direct mail was the largest or
   second-largest medium for ~70 years. It is **written out of the total eight separate times**, and out of
   the 2025 headline basis entirely `[e2-medium-004 B; e6-medium-001 B; e7-scale-001 B]`. The spine gains a
   basis-layer twin: *the independent denominator was also the anomaly.*

**One line for Session 2:** *the buyer's independent hand was the anomaly, too — and the count starts
seller-owned at every new medium's birth, not only after consolidation.* The POSIWID gap S1 found at three
layers (who counts, prices, concentrates) is now evidenced at six more. Those six: who targets, who is
admitted, who meters the response, what enters the base, which unit is priced, how many hands take the cut.

**And the corpus is ~34× more self-correcting than S1 could see.** S1 surfaced ~10 opposition edges. The real
count is **339** (88 in the quarter). They are overwhelmingly the corpus author's own built-in BUTs (§2). Half
are two guards: *every share statistic is a basis, not a fact*, and *the targeting unit was never the person
until the very end.* The second one **complicates the R4 addressable-base spine before it is built.**

### THREAD 8 — The front door and the rent to get in *(s2-dig-buyers)*

**Through-line.** For a century the ad market's front door was gated, and an intermediary collected the rent.
From 1893 only *recognised agencies* were paid the commission, never advertisers direct. That turned a
customary 15% into a cartel toll on national space. The buyer who would not pay it had one entrance: the
self-serve, no-agency classified counter. The state acted as bouncer, expelling whole buyer classes through
that door. Paid search then inherited the un-gated door and dropped the rent to near zero. **But the one place
the market got *more* open is where the count of who is admitted went dark, and the rent merely moved to the
platform take.**

**Distinct from Thread 9 (the meter).** T8 is the **turnstile** — admission, exclusion, and the *count of who
is in the room*. T9 is the **meter** — the pricing model. They are complementary halves of the buyer side.

**Form: EXPLANATORY NARRATIVE, rung 2**, system protagonist ("the front door"). YES Q1–Q4; ABSENT Q5, Q6.
**Structure: SPIRAL** — the same question (*who is admitted, and who collects the rent?*) re-entered at four
gatekeepers: the cartel gate, the state, the self-serve counter, the platform. **Runner-up: dual-profile**
(Regime 1 gated / Regime 2 open). **Two series, never merged:** HEADS (advertisers admitted) opened; DOLLARS
(share of spend) did not.

**ABT (three moves):**
- **AND** for a century the front door was gated. Only recognised agencies were paid the commission
  `[tu:era:1:event:5]`; the toll-free entrance was the self-serve classified counter `[e1-buyers-007 C]`; the
  state expelled whole classes through it `[tu:era:1:event:6; e3-buyers-004 B]`.
- **BUT** paid search inherited that door and dropped the rent to near zero. AdWords Select "removed each of
  those gates," and "the 15% commission simply stopped existing for a whole class of buyer"
  `[tu:era:6:field:BUYERS+CREATORS synth]`.
- **THEREFORE** the one place the market got *more* open is where the count went dark `[e6-buyers-002 C;
  e7-buyers-003 C]`, and the money never democratised at all `[e6-buyers-005 B]`.

**Best cited findings:**
1. **The count explodes, then goes dark (HEADS series).** ~3,800 (Yahoo 1998, A) → ~53,000 (Overture 2001, A)
   → ~80,000 (2002, A) → **~1.1M (Google 2008, C, "never disclosed," CI 550k–2.6M)** → ">10M" (Meta 2025, B;
   Google "not published," C) `[e5-buyers-001/002/003; e6-buyers-001/002; e7-buyers-001/003]`. Grade-A SEC
   middle, soft ends — ship the endpoints with their intervals.
2. **The gates, named and dated (the hinge/peak).** Overture: 10¢ min bid, $20 monthly minimum, human
   editorial review `[e5-buyers-002 A]`. AdWords Select removed them ($5 activation, no minimum, automated
   approval). **DO NOT ship the "5-cent bid":** `e6-pricing-005` (grade A) self-flags it as unsupported (§6).
3. **The commission-free door is ninety years old.** Classified carried "no agency commission and no cost of
   preparation," 1914 `[e1-buyers-007 C, EXCLUDES agency commission]`. It became "the direct ancestor of paid
   search," 1975 `[e3-buyers-008 C]`.
4. **The state as bouncer, twice.** Patent medicine "regulated out of its old form" (Pure Food & Drug 1906,
   `tu:era:1:event:6`). Cigarettes ~$225M/yr "could no longer buy television or radio at all," 2 Jan 1971
   `[e3-buyers-004 B]`. The mark shows in the series: **1971 was the only year of the era US TV ad spend fell**
   (−1.7%) `[e3-medium-011 B]`.
5. **Heads democratised, dollars did not (the corrective).** National's share *rose* **61.4%→66.3% (2002–07)**,
   "the opposite of what a 'search eats local budgets' story predicts." Brand TV "did not move" `[e6-buyers-005
   B; e6-buyers-004 B]`.
6. **The demand-for-the-number had a carrier.** The ANA buyer bloc (founded 1910) "forced audited circulation
   in 1914." The national brand demanded the independent count; the self-serve buyer never needed it, and its
   door is the one that won `[tu:era:1:field:BUYERS]`.

**Constraint inventory (system → no corners).** B = the recognised-agency / 15% cartel gate (rent). C = the
state (bouncer). D = the un-gated self-serve door. **B and D genuinely collide** and pass the satellite test:
D is exactly the money B's commission never touched, and D's heir (paid search) dissolved B's cut. **Mandatory
residuals (block the malign-optimiser read):** Overture *chose* a gated editorially-reviewed door when it
could have opened first; disclosure *existed* (SEC-filed counts) and was lost, so the number-hole is a
Google-era artefact, not the market's nature `[e5-buyers-001/002 A]`.

**DEAD / CONTRADICTED (ships):**
- "search democratised demand toward a long tail of *money*" — CONTRADICTED (long tail of heads, not dollars).
- "self-serve / commission-free buying is a platform-era invention" — CONTRADICTED (existed by 1914).
- "the front door opened monotonically" — did_not_support (the state slammed it twice).

**Connection to the meta-spine — extends.** The demand-side twin: **the seller now owns the guest list too.**
The most-open door in the market's history is the one with no doorman anyone outside can see. This is Thread

### THREAD 9 — The direct-response meter: the keyed number that was never new *(s2-sweep-hub §2)*

**Through-line.** The buyer was never one actor. One of the four classes — the **direct-response buyer** —
never accepted the seller's audience number at all. It paid per *keyed action* (cost per order, not cost per
thousand) from the 1900s. It was 16% of spend in 1914, larger than broadcast by 1956, the largest single
national medium by 1975, 22% by 1993. And its pay-per-action pricing model **won the internet** (57% of US
internet revenue, 2008). The platform era is not only the seller re-capturing the count; it is the
direct-response buyer's logic becoming the whole market's logic.

**Form: EXPLANATORY NARRATIVE, rung 2.** System subject (the pricing-model regime); YES Q1–Q4; ABSENT Q5, Q6.
Cleanest quantified quarter through-line. **Grade watch — B/C at the early ends, lands on a single A at 2008.**

**Best cited findings (the cross-era arc):**
- 1914 — direct-response ~**$192M, ~16%** of the benchmark `[e1-buyers-008 C, CI80 96–360]`.
- 1956 — direct mail **24% of all US *national* ad, larger than broadcast** — "cannot be treated as a
  later-era phenomenon" `[e3-buyers-001 B]` (built-in corrective edge).
- 1975 — direct-response (proxied by direct mail) **14.8% of all US ad, largest single national medium**
  `[e3-buyers-009 B]`.
- 1993 — ~**$31B, ~22%**, "bought against a measured cost per order rather than a cost per thousand"
  `[e4-buyers-007 C, EXCLUDES cost per thousand]`.
- 2008 — **57% of US internet revenue on pay-per-action, up from 51% in 2007; only 39% on CPM**
  `[e6-buyers-008 A]`.

**Constraint / bridge.** Yellow Pages is graph-tagged "the structural ancestor of paid search"
`[tu:era:5:field:MEDIUM synth]`. Overture's 10-K benchmarked itself against direct mail `[e5-buyers-013 C]`.
Paid search did not invent the performance buyer; it *inherited* one that had been the largest single medium
for fifty years.

**DEAD / CONTRADICTED (ships):**
- "platforms invented performance advertising" — CONTRADICTED (`e1-buyers-008 C, e3-buyers-001 B`).
- **Provenance flag:** the 1915 national/local split is third-hand via Borden 1942 → Galbi
  (`e1-buyers-005/006 B`). Do not harden the pre-1975 dollars.

**Connection to the meta-spine — supplies the demand-side WHY.** S1 explains *who owns the number*. The meter
explains *why the market kept demanding one*: a structurally-central buyer class always paid by it. **The
closing irony sharpens the spine.** The buyer that spent 150 years escaping the seller's audience count, by
paying per measured action, arrived via search in the one medium where the seller counts the actions too
(click-fraud unadjudicated, S1 Thread 1). **Most-wanted independent number → least-independent number.** The
dial (T7) and the meter (T9) are the same buyer moving in opposite directions — the residual that keeps both
honest.

### THREAD 10 — The denominator war: direct mail, the medium the totals write out *(s2-dig-directmail)*

channel it was settled against. Direct mail was the largest or second-largest named medium for ~70 years

headline basis entirely. The same booking convention makes it biggest **or** invisible. So "TV conquered
advertising," "search ate the market," and "advertising is 1.32% of GDP" are each **claims about a basis, not
facts about the world.**

**Form: EXPLANATORY NARRATIVE — McPhee DUAL-PROFILE on one denominator**, rung 2 (or rung 3 if the two regimes
carry the piece). **Peak type: LEGIBILITY** — the gap becomes undeniable at `e7-scale-006`'s method note.
**Best built as the denominator correction *inside* S1 Thread 6**, not a standalone (its edges are B/C).

**ABT (three moves):**
- **AND** direct mail was the largest-or-#2 named medium for seventy years `[e2-medium-004 B, e6-medium-001 B]`.
- **BUT** it is written out of the total eight times, and out of the 2025 basis entirely, because a mailer is
  not a media owner `[e7-scale-001 B; e7-scale-006 C]`.
- **THEREFORE** every "share of advertising" is a claim about a basis. The total is a franchise boundary, not
  a fact.

**Best cited findings:**

### THREAD 11 — The metrology reset: the carrier changed the unit, not the share *(s2-dig-medium)*

**Through-line.** The received story is a relay race of carriers (newspaper→radio→TV→internet, each conquering
the last). The MEDIUM row contradicts it twice. **(1) The shares moved glacially.** Newspapers were the
largest single US medium 1914→2000, and total TV did not pass them until 1992, by ~1%. **(2) What actually
changed at each carrier was the unit of sale and who could count it.** The corpus names its own eras by the
priced unit — Spot → Impression → Auction → Machine. Each new unit arrived before any third party could
measure it, so the seller counted it first. **This is the mechanism behind the meta-spine, not another
instance of it.**

**Form: EXPLANATORY NARRATIVE — SPIRAL over chronology**, rung 2. YES Q1,3,4; NO Q2 (system — revealed
function, not desire); ABSENT Q5, Q6. Re-enter *who can count the new unit?* at each carrier.

**Best cited findings:**
1. **The "revolution" was glacial.** TV entered at 1.1% ($58M, 1949) and did not pass newspapers by total
   spend until **1992** ($31,079M vs $30,737M, ~1%) `[e2-medium-006 B, e4-medium-002 B]`. Newspapers led ~86
   years `[e5-medium-001 B]`.
2. **The mechanism claim.** The Coen/McCann series **understates internet by roughly half** at era end
   ($10,529M vs IAB/PwC $21,206M for 2007) and **terminates with 2007 data** — "making 2008 a hard seam in
   every 1919-onward series" `[e6-medium-006 A]`. The century-spanning instrument goes blind exactly when the
   new carrier arrives.
3. **The carrier does not die; its unit re-prices.** Network radio collapsed **$196M→$43M (1950–60)** while
   local spot radio grew **$273M→$428M** — "radio survived by becoming a local retail medium" `[e3-medium-009 B]`.
4. **The price unit is a function of the addressable base.** US TV households 3.88M (9%, 1950) → 68.5M
   (**97.1%**, 1975) — "the supply-side fact that made the 30-second spot a mass-reach instrument"
   `[e3-medium-004 B]` (ties to Thread 7).
5. **The intent incumbent grew *through* the disruptor.** Yellow Pages/directory **$9.825B (1994) → $13.228B
   (2000)**, peaked mid-2000s `[e5-medium-003 B]`. The ~$14B directory market was still at/near peak as the era
   closed `[e6-medium-002 B]`. Search *inherited* the intent unit rather than displacing it.
6. **The Machine Market close.** US internet ad revenue **$23,448M (2008) → $294,593M (2025)**, 12.6×
   `[e7-medium-001 A]`. 2025 split search $114.2B (38.8%) `[e7-medium-004 A]`. Digital ≈ **74% of all US ad
   spend** (MAGNA rail; EMARKETER ≈82% — $23B apart, never one axis) `[e7-medium-002 C]`.

**Constraint inventory (the built-in `NOT_*` correctives).** `e3-medium-008` TV national `NEVER_REACHED`
newspapers' total; `e4-medium-006` radio `NOT_NEAR_TOP`; `e5-medium-003` directory pool `NOT_PEAKED_UNTIL`
mid-2000s; `e6-medium-006` Coen `UNDERSTATES` internet; `e2-medium-003`/`e5-medium-004` share `CONFLICTS_WITH`

not an optimiser's coup).

**DEAD / CONTRADICTED (ships):**
- "each new medium captured share by killing the last" — CONTRADICTED (`e4-medium-002 B, e3-medium-008 B,
  e5-medium-003 B`, plus NOT_COLLAPSED ×5).

### THREAD 12 — The middleman's cut was never one number: the broadcast stack *(s2-dig-middleman)*

least of them.
